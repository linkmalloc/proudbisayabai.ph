President Ferdinand Marcos Jr. returned to the Philippines on Friday, Nov. 21, with P41.1 billion in investment pledges from Chinese companies expected to generate more than 7,000 jobs.

The presidential aircraft landed at Villamor Air Base in Pasay City at 5:01 p.m. following his participation in the 33rd Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation (APEC) Economic Leaders’ Meeting in Shenzhen, China.

In his arrival statement, Marcos said the pledges came from business engagements with major Chinese companies covering manufacturing, electric vehicles, renewable energy and other industries.

“These commitments underscore the continued confidence of investors in the Philippine economy as they pursue opportunities to expand production capacities, modernize manufacturing facilities, and generate additional employment across the country,” he said.

Among the major discussions was a proposed fully integrated production base for bearings and bearing components by C&U Group in Alitagtag, Batangas. The planned facility is envisioned to become the company's largest production base outside China.

Marcos also met with BYD Founder and Chairman Wang Chuanfu to explore opportunities to expand the electric vehicle manufacturer's presence in the Philippines and support the growth of the country's domestic EV industry.

The President separately held discussions with Mingyang Smart Energy Group on planned investments in offshore wind energy infrastructure.

An executive roundtable involving TCL Group, Washington Building Materials Group, Shenzhen Ganson Electronic and Picon International also explored expansion plans and investment opportunities aligned with the country's manufacturing priorities.

Marcos said the engagements were intended to attract new investments, expand existing manufacturing operations, create quality jobs, increase exports, strengthen local industries and encourage technology transfer.

He added that the government would work to turn the commitments into employment opportunities and business prospects for micro, small and medium enterprises.

Beyond investment discussions, Marcos said the Philippines' participation in the APEC meetings focused on keeping markets open, securing supply chains and strengthening regional cooperation amid global economic uncertainty.

He also highlighted the signing of the Philippines-Chile Comprehensive Economic Partnership Agreement, the country's first bilateral free trade agreement with a Latin American nation.

The agreement is expected to open additional markets for Philippine goods and services.

During his China visit, Marcos also met with fellow APEC leaders from Chile, Peru and Mexico to strengthen bilateral relations and expand cooperation in trade and investment.
