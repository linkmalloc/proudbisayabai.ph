Mandaue City Hospital is looking to expand its 25-bed capacity by 10 beds as rising dengue admissions, particularly among children, put pressure on its limited facilities.

  

Hospital head Dr. Ligaya Dargantes said the facility has been receiving around 10 to 15 dengue patients daily at its Emergency Room, apart from those seeking consultation through the outpatient department.

  

The hospital began noticing an increase in dengue cases in October, with admissions climbing more sharply this November, she said.

  

“Wala tay extra beds sa pagka karon, but we are planning to add more beds. But sa gidak-on sa atong hospital, kutub ra gyud ta sa 10 atong ma-add nga dili pod ingon nga naa sila sa gawas sa building,” Dargantes said. (We do not have extra beds at present, but we are planning to add more. However, given the size of our hospital, we can only add up to 10 beds without having patients stay outside the building.)

  

The hospital currently allocates its 25 beds among different wards: five for male medical patients, five for female medical patients, five for obstetric and surgical cases, and 10 for pediatric patients.

  

Dargantes said most of the pediatric beds are now occupied by children with dengue.

  

To manage admissions, the hospital is coordinating with the City Health Office on the possibility of allowing patients who are already fit for discharge to continue recovering under monitoring at their respective community health centers.

  

However, patients who remain weak, continue vomiting or show signs of bleeding, particularly children, cannot be discharged until their condition improves, she said.

  

Dargantes said the hospital continues to accommodate patients even when all its beds are occupied, referring them to other facilities when necessary. However, nearby public hospitals are also experiencing high occupancy.

  

The hospital also plans to meet with barangay officials to discuss measures to address the rising number of cases.

  

City Health Office head Dr. Debra Catulong will coordinate with other hospitals to determine their available bed capacities, following an arrangement similar to the one used during the Covid-19 pandemic.

  

The coordination aims to help the city manage patient referrals and prepare for a possible further increase in dengue admissions.

  

Meanwhile, Mayor Thadeo Jovito “Jonkie” Ouano is planning to hire three additional doctors and four nurses to augment the hospital's medical workforce.

  

Dargantes said the hospital currently has one doctor assigned to the Emergency Room and another handling patients in the wards.

  

The ward doctor attends to patients occupying the hospital's 25 beds, while the Emergency Room has 13 beds for various medical concerns, including injuries from vehicular accidents, vomiting, diarrhea and women about to give birth.

  

She said the limited number of doctors and nurses makes it challenging to attend to the different emergencies handled by the facility.

  

Beyond hospital capacity, Dargantes also warned against delaying medical consultation, noting that some patients arrive after experiencing fever for about a week.

  

She urged parents to bring their children for medical assessment as soon as they develop a fever instead of waiting for their condition to worsen.

  

Early consultation, she said, allows health workers to diagnose the illness and provide appropriate medical attention sooner.
