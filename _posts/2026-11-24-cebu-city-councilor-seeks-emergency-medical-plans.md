Sports organizers in Cebu City may be asked to provide trained first responders, emergency equipment and ambulance arrangements at athletic events under a proposal aimed at improving survival chances during cardiac emergencies.

  

Councilor Nyza Archival raised the proposal in a privilege speech before the City Council on Tuesday, Nov. 24, following the reported death of former Philippine Basketball Association player Peter June “PJ” Simon, who collapsed after experiencing difficulty breathing while playing basketball in Davao City.

  

Archival said the incident underscored the need for emergency preparedness in sporting activities, including those intended to promote fitness and healthy living.

  

“Are we truly prepared when a person suddenly collapses from a cardiac emergency?” she asked.

  

Her proposal covers professional tournaments, barangay basketball leagues, school competitions, fun runs and recreational sports activities.

  

She wants organizers to prepare emergency response plans that provide for trained medical personnel or first responders, accessible first-aid equipment, ambulance referral arrangements and clear emergency contact procedures.

  

Archival also urged the city to explore acquiring automated external defibrillators (AEDs), devices used to help respond to certain life-threatening cardiac emergencies.

  

She identified the Cebu City Sports Center, public sports complexes, gymnasiums, city-owned recreational facilities and major event venues as possible locations for the equipment.

  

However, she stressed that acquiring AEDs would not be enough without personnel trained to operate them.

  

The councilor also proposed expanding cardiopulmonary resuscitation (CPR) and Basic Life Support (BLS) training in barangays and schools.

  

She wants barangay health workers, coaches, sports coordinators, athletic officials, security personnel and community volunteers included in the training.

  

“Sometimes, the person who saves a life is not a doctor; it is the ordinary individual who knows what to do before a medical professional arrives,” she said.

  

Beyond emergency response, Archival called for a city-wide heart health awareness campaign through barangay health centers.

  

The proposed campaign would promote regular blood pressure checks, cardiovascular risk assessments and diabetes screening, while educating residents about warning signs such as chest discomfort, difficulty breathing, fainting and sudden collapse.

  

She also cited City Ordinance 2819, which institutionalizes semi-annual basic survival skills training for Cebu City Government employees, as a measure that could support wider emergency preparedness.

  

Through her proposed motions, Archival urged sports event organizers to establish emergency response plans and public and private schools to implement Republic Act 10871, or the Basic Life Support Training in Schools Act.

  

She also asked the City Council to direct the Cebu City Health Department to conduct a city-wide heart health awareness and prevention campaign.

  

Archival said strengthening emergency response systems would help ensure that people experiencing cardiac emergencies receive timely assistance.

  

“We cannot change what happened, but we can make sure the next person who experiences a cardiac emergency has a better chance of surviving,” she said.
