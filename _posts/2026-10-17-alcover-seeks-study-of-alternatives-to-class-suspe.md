Repeated class suspensions during haze episodes have prompted Councilor Pastor “Jun” Alcover to propose a study of additional ways to protect students from unhealthy air while minimizing disruptions to classroom learning.

  

In a proposed resolution, Alcover asked Mayor Nestor Archival to direct concerned city offices, in coordination with the Department of Education (DepEd) and other agencies, to assess evidence-based measures for protecting students and the public during periods of poor air quality.

  

The proposal follows several class suspensions in recent weeks and calls for the city to examine whether cancellations should remain the primary response or be complemented by other precautions, depending on the severity of air pollution.

  

Alcover’s proposal does not seek to automatically replace class suspensions. Instead, it calls for an assessment of the conditions under which additional protective measures may be appropriate and when unhealthy air still warrants the cancellation of classes.

  

Among the options for evaluation are the use of appropriate face masks by students, teachers and school personnel during periods of unhealthy air quality, as well as temporary restrictions on outdoor school activities.

  

These may include physical education classes, sports, school programs and other activities that could expose students to polluted air.

  

The proposed assessment would also cover air-quality monitoring around schools and other places where children gather, as well as classroom ventilation and indoor air conditions.

  

The resolution further calls for timely advisories to parents and guardians, including guidance on keeping children indoors and limiting unnecessary exposure during haze episodes. It also highlights the need to account for students with respiratory sensitivities when developing protective measures.

  

Alcover proposed coordination among the Cebu City Health Department, Cebu City Environment and Natural Resources Office, Cebu City Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Office, Cebu City Schools Division Office and other relevant agencies.

  

The assessment would consider air-quality readings, the duration and movement of haze, children’s vulnerability and the availability of protective measures. The resolution emphasizes that responses should be proportionate to health risks and guided by reliable monitoring and health advice.

  

Alcover’s proposal noted that suspending classes does not necessarily mean students will remain indoors or avoid polluted air, as some may still go outside or participate in recreational activities during class cancellations.

  

Repeated suspensions can also interrupt lessons, examinations and school activities while creating childcare difficulties for working parents and guardians, the resolution said.

  

The proposed study aims to help the city balance these disruptions with the need to protect students from potentially harmful exposure to polluted air.

  

If pursued, the assessment could guide clearer protocols for future haze episodes, including when class suspensions are necessary and when additional protective measures may be suitable.
