With nearly 700,000 school-aged children in Cebu potentially eligible to join scouting, the Boy Scouts of the Philippines (BSP) Cebu Council is using its upcoming jamborette to encourage more young people to develop skills, build friendships and experience life beyond their screens.

  

Former Cebu City councilor Joy Pesquera, the council's second vice chairman, said children need more opportunities for outdoor activities and face-to-face interaction to support their social development and mental well-being.

  

“However, what we're encouraging them is for their social development,” Pesquera said. She added that “without jamboree, without camping, it's high time for them to go out, experience life.”

  

She said scouting exposes children to experiences they cannot gain by staying indoors and teaches them to live by the Scout Oath and Law, which promote love of God, country and fellowmen, respect for the environment and volunteerism.

  

Pesquera urged parents and the media to help introduce scouting to the younger generation.

  

The council will gather around 2,000 scouts and adult leaders from across Cebu for the 14th Cebu Council Jamborette on Dec. 18 to 22, 2026, at the Capitol Hills Scout Camp in Lahug, Cebu City.

  

The five-day event will mark the council's 80th Charter Anniversary under the theme “80 Years of Scouting: Honoring Our Legacy, Inspiring Generations Towards Centennial.”

  

Council Chairman Noel Alicaya said Cebu has nearly 200,000 registered scouts, compared with an estimated 700,000 school-aged children who could potentially join the organization.

  

He said the council's membership has generally grown annually, except in 2025, when the September earthquake and subsequent typhoon disrupted activities during the usual recruitment period. 

  

The council also uses social media to promote scouting and attract new members.

  

Organizers have prepared seven modules, each with five activities, for participants from the 10 school divisions across Cebu province.

  

Council First Vice Chairman Jose Emery Roble said the activities will teach children independence and survival skills, including managing themselves away from their families and preparing their own food.

  

The aquatics and water safety module will cover basic water rescue, swimming and non-swimming approaches, water safety and basic life support. The campcraft and woodcraft module will feature pioneering for Senior Scouts, survival cooking for Boy Scouts, fire building and bamboo cooking.

  

Participants will also explore native arts and crafts under the Crossroad of Culture module, including flower-making using recycled plastic bottles and creating improvised burners from empty cans.

  

The program will include outdoor challenges, competitions, cultural presentations and campfire activities.

  

Boy Scouts, generally in Grades 4 to 6, and Senior Scouts in junior high school will participate in the full program. Cub Scouts will have a separate one-day activity because they cannot join overnight camping, while Rover Scouts will focus on community service.

  

The jamborette is open to students from public and private schools. Applicants do not need school endorsements, and scout coordinators and adult leaders can assist with registration.

  

Each participant must pay P800 and submit an application by Dec. 11. Organizers will not accept on-site registration. 

  

Application forms, participant rosters and health and medical clearance forms are available through the BSP Cebu Council's Facebook page.

  

Registered participants will receive a jamborette patch, customized neckerchief, printed lanyard and identification card.

  

BSP Youth Development Officer Jon Oliver said organizers were coordinating with the Cebu Council to provide medical services, police presence and other assistance throughout the event.

  

Organizers will brief activity officers on the BSP's safeguarding policies and guidelines. 

  

They will also coordinate with nearby barangays for security assistance and the Cebu City Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Office for emergency response support, including ambulance services.

  

The Capitol Hills Scout Camp can accommodate up to 5,000 scouts, although organizers are targeting around 2,000 participants for the December gathering.
