Six public school properties in Cebu City may host groundwater wells as the Metropolitan Cebu Water District (MCWD) seeks additional supplies to meet rising demand and strengthen its water sources against drought.

  

The Cebu City Government and MCWD signed a memorandum of agreement on Tuesday, Nov. 24, allowing the district to explore portions of the school properties and build production wells if studies establish that the sites are suitable.

  

The schools identified are Pardo Elementary School, Basak Elementary School, Punta Princesa Elementary School, Labangon Elementary School, Barrio Luz Elementary School and Talamban Elementary School.

  

Water extracted from productive wells will be distributed to MCWD customers in the host communities and nearby areas, according to the agreement.

  

The proposed groundwater projects are intended to provide an interim supply while the district works to secure other sources, including desalinated and surface water.

  

MCWD is considering groundwater because it is generally less vulnerable to El Niño and drought conditions than surface water, making it a potential supply option for communities dependent on surface sources.

  

However, the agreement does not guarantee that all six schools will have operating wells. MCWD must first conduct water exploration and determine whether each location can support drilling and sustained operations.

  

Only sites found suitable may proceed to the construction, installation and operation of production wells, pumping stations and related facilities.

  

The agreement will initially remain in effect for at least 10 years from its signing. The city and MCWD may renew it for another 10 years by mutual agreement.

  

For every productive well, MCWD will pay the city P4 for each cubic meter of water extracted once distribution begins.

  

Meters will be installed at the project sites to monitor extraction volumes. The city may also assign personnel to join MCWD representatives in conducting monthly meter readings.

  

The payment obligation will take effect only when a well becomes productive and the district starts extracting water for distribution.

  

The Cebu City Government will retain ownership of the school properties, while MCWD will be responsible for the facilities and equipment it installs.
