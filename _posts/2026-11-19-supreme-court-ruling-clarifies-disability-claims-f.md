The Supreme Court has clarified the medical obligations of employers and the standards for disability compensation involving Filipino seafarers who contract illnesses while working at sea, a ruling the Department of Migrant Workers (DMW) said could guide similar cases.

  

Migrant Workers Secretary Hans Leo Cacdac welcomed the SC en banc decision in the case of RCCL Crew Management Inc. and Royal Caribbean Cruise Ltd. vs. Jonathan Ian H. Ponio (G.R. No. 263434), which involved a seafarer who developed an ear infection while employed as a galley steward.

  

Jonathan Ian Ponio passed his pre-employment medical examination with normal hearing before deployment. While performing galley duties, including cleaning and sanitizing workspaces, he contracted an infection in his right ear.

  

He was medically repatriated on Oct. 30, 2017, after doctors diagnosed him with moderate to severe conductive hearing loss.

  

However, his employers did not provide him with a written copy of his final medical assessment.

  

Under the 2010 Philippine Overseas Employment Administration Standard Employment Contract (POEA-SEC), an illness not included in the list of occupational diseases is presumed work-related and compensable if substantial evidence shows that it developed during the seafarer's employment.

  

Employers may dispute a claim by presenting medical evidence that the illness was not caused by the seafarer's work or existed before deployment. If the employers establish either ground, the seafarer must then present evidence linking the illness to the job.

  

The Supreme Court also clarified the medical requirements following a seafarer's repatriation. Seafarers must undergo a medical examination within three working days of their return and attend regular medical checkups.

  

Employers, meanwhile, must provide a written copy of the final medical assessment within 120 or 240 days. Failure to issue the assessment within the prescribed period means the seafarer's disability is considered total and permanent under the law.

  

The Court ordered RCCL Crew Management Inc. and Royal Caribbean Cruise Ltd. to pay Ponio USD95,000 in permanent total disability benefits under his Collective Bargaining Agreement, in addition to 10 percent attorney's fees and 6 percent annual legal interest.

  

The DMW said the ruling reinforces the responsibilities of manning agencies, foreign principals and seafarers under the POEA-SEC and Republic Act 12021, or the Magna Carta of Filipino Seafarers.

  

“The decision strengthens fairness, transparency, and predictability in maritime employment relations, in line with Republic Act No. 12021,” Cacdac said.

  

He also urged licensed manning agencies, foreign principals and seafarers to "strictly observe their respective medical and reporting obligations."

  

Seafarers who need legal or welfare assistance may contact the DMW Office of the Assistant Secretary for Seabased OFW Concerns through seabased.asec@dmw.gov.ph.
