The Cebu Provincial Government is moving to secure additional land for its planned solar power facility in northern Cebu, with the Provincial Board authorizing the governor to acquire about 10 hectares in Barangay Talisay, Daanbantayan.

  

The board approved the ordinance on third and final reading during its regular session on Monday, November 24. The measure, authored by Board Member Nelson J. Mondigo, covers properties identified under Tax Declaration Nos. 20-26900 and 20-0017-20527.

  

It authorizes the governor to negotiate with the property owners for the purchase of the parcels. If the negotiations fail, the provincial government may pursue expropriation, subject to applicable legal requirements.

  

The ordinance also allows the allocation of public funds to pay the landowners just compensation for the properties.

  

The acquisition is part of preparations for a proposed 150-megawatt peak (MWp) solar facility intended to help address Cebu's growing electricity demand.

  

Provincial Administrator Atty. Ace Durano earlier said that the project had entered its implementation phase, although the province and its private partner still needed to secure several permits and regulatory clearances before construction.

  

Durano said the Department of Energy (DOE) had awarded the project to Acciona Energia Philippines, which had submitted the required documents, including financial guarantees.

  

The provincial government is also processing land conversion requirements with the Department of Agrarian Reform because the proposed development involves agricultural property.

  

The planned facility is expected to cover about 180 hectares of provincial government land, with an estimated investment of $150 million based on a projected cost of $1 million per megawatt of capacity.

  

Preparatory activities, including soil testing, have started at the site as the project moves toward the next development stage and eventual civil works.

  

However, disputes involving former landowners could affect the project's timeline. Some have challenged the provincial government's acquisition of portions of the property through tax forfeiture.

  

Durano previously estimated that the disputed areas account for around 40 to 50 percent of the proposed project site.

  

The solar project traces its beginnings to a 2023 agreement between the Cebu Provincial Government, then under former governor Gwendolyn Garcia, and Acciona Energia Global.

  

The project later secured a long-term power supply contract through the national government's Green Energy Auction Program after Acciona won the corresponding auction. The DOE also certified it as an Energy Project of National Significance, a designation intended to prioritize the processing of permits and approvals.
