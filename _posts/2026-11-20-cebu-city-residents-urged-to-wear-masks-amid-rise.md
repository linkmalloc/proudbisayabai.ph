Cebu City residents are being urged to wear face masks, practice proper hygiene and stay home when sick as health authorities monitor an increase in influenza-like illnesses in the city.

  

Councilor Dave Tumulak, chairman of the City Council's Committee on Health, Hospitals, Services and Sanitation, said reports from the Department of Health (DOH), City Health Department and Cebu City Medical Center showed a high number of cases involving respiratory symptoms.

  

“For me, it's alarming because there are many cases,” Tumulak said in an interview.

  

He said many residents had been experiencing coughs, colds and other symptoms, while barangay health workers reported that some sick individuals remained at home without consulting medical professionals.

  

Some residents were buying medicines on their own, while pharmacies had reportedly seen increased purchases of medicines for cough, fever and colds, he added.

  

Tumulak advised those experiencing respiratory symptoms to avoid unnecessary trips outside their homes and limit contact with other people to prevent possible transmission.

  

“If you have a cough, please do not go to work,” he said.

  

He also encouraged residents to wear masks when going outdoors, carry alcohol or hand sanitizer and wash their hands regularly. Those coughing at home should also wear masks to protect other household members.

  

He warned that people who continue their usual activities while sick, especially those who do not cover their mouths when coughing, risk exposing others to infection.

  

The DOH explained that changing weather does not directly cause respiratory infections but may create conditions that allow viruses to spread more easily.

  

Some viruses can remain airborne longer depending on temperature and humidity. People also tend to spend more time indoors during cold or rainy weather, increasing opportunities for transmission.

  

Cold or dry air may also dry out the nose and throat, potentially weakening the body's natural defenses against germs, the agency said.

  

To reduce the risk of infection, the DOH advised families to ensure their members are vaccinated against influenza, maintain good ventilation at home and avoid crowded places when possible.

  

It also urged the public to cover their mouths and noses when coughing or sneezing, wash their hands frequently with soap and clean water, and avoid touching their eyes, nose and mouth.

  

Those with coughs or colds who need to go outside should wear face masks, while people who feel unwell should stay home and limit contact with others.

  

The agency identified fever, cough, colds, sore throat, headache, body pain and weakness as symptoms to watch for during flu season.

  

Residents experiencing fever accompanied by cough, colds, sore throat or body pain should seek consultation at their nearest health center or YAKAP clinic. Those with difficulty breathing or other severe symptoms should immediately seek medical attention at a health center or hospital.

  

Meanwhile, Tumulak said Cebu City had also recorded dengue cases, although he described their number as not particularly high.

  

He said stagnant water provides breeding grounds for mosquitoes, making it important for households and communities to remove standing water from containers and other areas around their surroundings.

  

Tumulak said the city was not yet experiencing a dengue outbreak.

  

He urged residents to maintain preventive measures at home and in their communities to help limit the spread of both respiratory and mosquito-borne illnesses.
