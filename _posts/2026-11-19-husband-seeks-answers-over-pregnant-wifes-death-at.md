Jhovell Patlingrao is still haunted by the final words of his pregnant wife, Angelle Fe Isugan, as she battled heavy bleeding at the Barili District Hospital.

“Ayaw kog biyae,” he recalled her telling him before she and their unborn child died.

The loss has left Patlingrao seeking answers about the medical care his wife received, particularly the alleged internal examination performed despite her reported placenta previa diagnosis and the time it took to arrange her transfer to another hospital.

Patlingrao said he woke up to find his wife bleeding heavily while they were asleep. He sought help from relatives to arrange an ambulance, and they arrived at the Barili District Hospital shortly after 5 a.m.

Hospital personnel brought Isugan to the labor room, but Patlingrao claimed he initially struggled to get assistance because nobody was at the nurse station.

He said he repeatedly asked the security guard for help until a nurse attended to his wife. He also alleged that another nurse was asleep inside a room and had to be awakened.

While receiving medical attention, Isugan reportedly complained of dizziness. Patlingrao said a nurse performed an internal examination and found that she was three centimeters dilated. He alleged that another examination followed, causing her pain and heavier bleeding.

Patlingrao said he had already submitted his wife's prenatal records, which he claimed indicated placenta previa, a condition that involves the placenta covering or approaching the cervix.

“Ihatag na nako ang prenatal record so di nato dapat e-IE kay nakabutang didto Placenta previa,” he said, questioning why medical personnel allegedly performed the procedure despite the information in the documents.

He also questioned the time it took to arrange Isugan's referral to the Cebu Provincial Hospital in Carcar for specialized obstetric care.

Patlingrao said he repeatedly followed up on the transfer but was told that his wife's blood pressure needed to stabilize before medical personnel could transport her.

By then, he said, her bleeding had continued and her condition had worsened.

“Perti nang daghanang dugo, luoy na kaayo na akong asawa. Kanus-a man na ninyo i-refer?” he recalled asking hospital personnel.

He said their unborn child's heartbeat was initially normal while Isugan received intravenous fluids and oxygen. However, her breathing later became difficult and her blood pressure dropped.

Hoping to save her, Patlingrao said he eventually asked the attending physician to intubate his wife.

He recalled that medical personnel first declared their baby dead. Isugan was later pronounced dead after doctors could no longer detect the child's heartbeat.

Patlingrao said the circumstances surrounding their deaths continue to raise questions for him about whether the hospital could have handled his wife's case differently.

“Mangayo ko og hustisiya kay sige ko og hunahuna naa juy sayop ang hospital,” he said.

The Cebu Provincial Health Office (PHO), meanwhile, is gathering accounts from the medical personnel involved in Isugan's treatment.

PHO chief Dr. Jenina Canoy said the office had required the physician on duty and other hospital personnel to submit formal incident reports as part of its review.

Canoy confirmed that the Barili medical officer referred Isugan to the provincial hospital in Carcar and consulted an obstetrician-gynecologist (OB-GYN), who provided instructions before the planned transfer.

She also explained that medical personnel generally avoid internal examinations once placenta previa has been confirmed because the procedure may trigger bleeding.

However, Canoy said the PHO still needs to establish what medical records and information were available to the Barili medical team when Isugan arrived.

The office is also awaiting the Carcar hospital's formal account of the telephone consultation, including its assessment and the instructions given to the attending physicians.

The PHO has yet to issue final findings on the incident.
