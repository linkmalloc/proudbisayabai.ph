Cebu City residents may soon have fewer identification documents to present when accessing government services under a proposed policy encouraging offices and private establishments to accept one valid government-issued ID.

City Councilor Harold Kendrick Go filed a proposed resolution seeking to streamline identification requirements in city government offices, with particular emphasis on accepting the National ID as sufficient proof of identity.

The measure aims to reduce the time, inconvenience and expenses residents face when they are required to secure or carry several identification cards for routine transactions.

Under the proposal, concerned offices would generally require only one valid government-issued ID, subject to existing laws and regulations.

The resolution cites Republic Act No. 11055, or the Philippine Identification System Act, which recognizes the National ID as an official proof of identity for transactions with both government agencies and private entities.

It also invokes Republic Act No. 11032, or the Ease of Doing Business and Efficient Government Service Delivery Act of 2018, which promotes simplified government procedures and the removal of unnecessary documentary requirements.

The measure notes that requiring citizens to present multiple valid IDs can create additional costs and unnecessary difficulties.

It also references the Anti-Red Tape Authority's position that one valid identification card, particularly the National ID, should generally suffice for government transactions, subject to applicable laws and regulations.

Aside from city government offices, Go's proposal encourages private establishments to adopt the same practice.

If approved, the resolution would urge the Office of the Mayor to issue directives implementing a uniform identification policy across concerned city government offices.

However, the proposed measure recognizes that some transactions may still require additional documents under specific laws or regulatory rules.

The resolution remains subject to approval and would not immediately change existing identification requirements.
