Hundreds of devotees joined the solemn foot procession for Lapu-Lapu City's patroness on Friday, with city officials expecting a larger crowd for the 291st Nuestra Señora de Regla fiesta the following day.

The city government estimated that around 700 devotees and visitors took part in the procession on Nov. 20, which accompanied the image of the Blessed Virgin Mary through the city's streets.

“The estimated crowd for the solemn procession yesterday was around 700. Expected nga mas daghan kay fiesta na jud,” (The estimated crowd for yesterday's solemn procession was around 700. We expect more because it is already the actual fiesta day) the city government said.

Anticipating an influx of devotees and visitors, the local government implemented measures to ease traffic congestion, including suspending classes in seven barangays and work in several city offices on Friday.

Mayor Ma. Cynthia “Cindi” K. Chan issued Executive Order No. 2026-086 on Nov. 17, ordering asynchronous classes in selected areas and suspending work in city government offices not covered by the four-day workweek arrangement.

The order cited the expected arrival of devotees, residents and visitors for the religious procession and other socio-cultural activities. It also identified the nightly events on Nov. 20 and 21 as potential sources of traffic congestion that could affect access to schools and government offices.

“There is a need to implement measures to manage the effects of traffic congestion and ensure the safety of all who will attend the activities,” the order stated.

Asynchronous classes were implemented at all levels in public and private schools in Barangays Babag, Canjulao, Calawisan, Gun-ob, Looc, Pajo and Poblacion.

Schools outside these areas were also allowed to shift to asynchronous learning based on their assessment of how traffic conditions could affect their operations.

The work suspension covered city offices not following the four-day workweek under Executive Order No. 2026-074.

Essential services continued operating, including the Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Office, City Traffic Management System, Public Utility Jeepney Terminal Office, Clean and Green Office, Solid Waste Management Office, Clearing Team Office and Materials Recovery Office.

The Lapu-Lapu City Hospital, Sta. Rosa Community Hospital, City Health Office, Animal Bite Treatment Center, barangay health centers and City Hall marshals were also exempted.

Private businesses and national government agencies were allowed to decide on their own work arrangements.

After Friday's procession, Chan thanked the devotees who joined the religious activity and offered prayers to the city's patroness.

“We offered our prayers, thanksgiving, and reverence to our beloved Patroness, whose maternal love and intercession we continue to seek,” the mayor said.

For the 291st patronal fiesta, Chan also encouraged residents and devotees to deepen their faith and remain grateful for the Blessed Virgin Mary's guidance and intercession.

“Sa atong pagsaulog sa ika-291 nga Patronal Fiesta, atong hugton ang atong pagtuo ug kanunay kitang magpasalamat sa Mahal nga Birhen sa Regla sa iyang kanunay nga paggiya ug pag-ampo alang kanato,” (As we celebrate the 291st Patronal Fiesta, let us strengthen our faith and always give thanks to Our Lady of the Rule for her constant guidance and prayers for us) she said.

She urged the faithful to follow the Blessed Virgin's example of faith and humility and live with love, compassion and faith in God.
