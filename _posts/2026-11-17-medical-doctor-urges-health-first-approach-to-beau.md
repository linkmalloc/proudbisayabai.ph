Physical appearance should not be the sole measure of beauty, as good health remains the foundation of confidence and overall well-being.

Dr. Charmaine Badon, a physician from Dumaguete City, Negros Oriental, who is establishing her practice in Cebu City, said aesthetic medicine should help patients understand their health concerns rather than simply offer procedures to change how they look.

“So everything so you have to consider being healthy first in order to feel confident. Hence, your beauty will come out eventually,” Badon said.

She said people often associate a slim body or certain physical features with being healthy, but aesthetic care should not reinforce those assumptions.

She added practitioners must understand the medical and personal reasons behind a patient's concerns before recommending treatment.

“I don't just treat the patient. I listen to them,” she said, adding that she takes time to understand concerns beyond skin conditions and appearance, including mental health and other medical circumstances.

She has encountered patients whose struggles with weight affect their self-image and how they believe other people perceive them.

Some experience "depression" because of these perceptions, making it important to consider their emotional well-being alongside their physical concerns.

She also wants patients to understand the rationale behind procedures instead of undergoing treatments without knowing what they are intended to address.

“I want them to know what is the importance of each procedure,” she said, citing even basic facial treatments as an opportunity to explain the importance of maintaining healthy skin and preventing infections.

Her approach reflects an understanding of aesthetic medicine, which she said has expanded beyond conventional beauty treatments to include regenerative and longevity medicine.

“Regenerative medicine is a discipline of medicine which focuses on how to repair the body,” Badon said.

She explained that the practice involves examining how the body responds to inflammation and identifying underlying conditions before supporting its repair and regeneration.

Badon's interest in a different medical discipline developed after she began her career during the Covid-19 pandemic.

She graduated in 2020 and worked in hospitals as an emergency room frontliner. She also assisted in operating rooms and handled patients in intensive care units and hospital wards.

She eventually pursued aesthetic medicine, which she initially thought focused mainly on beauty but later discovered also considered patients' internal health and personal circumstances.

Now establishing her practice at Premier Medical Aesthetics by MediSwift at CityMall along N. Bacalso Avenue, Badon acknowledged that entering a competitive local market is challenging because she is originally from Dumaguete.

Although surgery was her first professional interest, the deaths of medical consultants and fellow doctors during the pandemic prompted her to reconsider the demands of hospital work.

She wanted to continue serving patients while making time for her family and church.

“I wanted to have a life wherein which I can enjoy both my profession and even like my personal life. I have time for the church, family and still see patients,” she said.
