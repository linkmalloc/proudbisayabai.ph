Mandaue City is considering requiring standby medical teams at all public and private sporting events following the deaths of two basketball players, including former PBA star Peter June Simon, to ensure faster emergency response during games.

Councilor Raul Kevin Cabahug V, chairman of the City Council's Committee on Health, said on Nov. 23, 2026, that he had filed a proposed resolution urging the Mandaue City Sports Office and Mandaue City Sports Commission to deploy medical personnel or responders at sporting activities across the city's 27 barangays.

“Kay sila man ang kamao gyud. If ever naay mahitabo, not just cardiac arrest, sila dayon ang maka-respondi right away,”Cabahug said.

A nurse by profession, Cabahug stressed that immediate medical attention is crucial during emergencies, when every second can affect a patient's chances of survival.

“Maong importante kaayo naay maka-respondi nga kamao og basic life support ug makatubag dayon sa panginahanglan sa panglawas,”he said.

He said trained responders could provide first aid and basic life support while waiting for further medical assistance.

“Especially if cardiac arrest, kung naa dayon ang life support, taas ang possibility sa survival rate,” he said.

Cabahug said city and barangay medical responders have received training in basic life support, including cardiopulmonary resuscitation (CPR).

His proposal cited the deaths of Simon on Nov. 20 and Albert Edsel Chua, Most Valuable Player of the 2026 Fil-Chi Athletic Association Inc. 45-and-Above Finals, who died on Nov. 18.

According to the resolution, Simon reportedly lost consciousness while playing basketball. His teammates attended to him before they rushed him to a hospital.

Chua, meanwhile, reportedly collapsed while resting after playing in a basketball game.

The measure also raised concerns over a video of Simon's incident that circulated online, prompting questions about whether medical personnel were immediately available to provide emergency assistance.

Cabahug expressed sadness over the incident, particularly reports that some people at the scene appeared to record videos instead of immediately helping.

“Very sad kay, of course, sagrado baya na ang kinabuhi sa tao. Murag lahi naman hinuon. I don’t know their intentions, but dapat unta dili lang sa, in respect sa grieving family,” (he said.

He said Simon's death should prompt Mandaue to strengthen its own emergency preparations, even though the incident happened in Davao.

“Me as a public servant, it is a learning experience ang panghitabo sa lain. Di man to sa atoa, sa Davao man to. Learning experience for us nga malikayan nga mahitabo diri sa Dakbayan sa Mandaue,” he said.

Cabahug described the deaths as a wake-up call, noting that organizers may sometimes take the presence of medical teams at sporting events for granted.

“It is a wake-up call for the City of Mandaue nga naay medical team every now and then,” he said.

He stressed that even small sporting events should have medical personnel on standby, along with first-aid equipment and emergency response resources, particularly for incidents involving cardiac arrest.

The proposed resolution cites the 1987 Constitution, which directs the State to protect and promote the people's right to health, and the Local Government Code of 1991, which mandates local governments to promote health and safety within their jurisdictions.

It urges the Mandaue City Sports Office and Sports Commission to ensure medical teams are available at all sporting events, whether organized by government agencies or private groups.

The proposed coverage includes athletes, players, coaches, officials, spectators and other participants.

Cabahug said the City Council would also consider converting the resolution into an ordinance, which would require further deliberation.
