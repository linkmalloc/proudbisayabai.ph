High electricity costs and constraints in power generation and transmission are weighing on Cebu's ability to attract investments and sustain industrial growth, business leaders warned, calling for faster development of energy infrastructure across the Visayas.

Industry representatives raised the concerns during a journalist-moderated roundtable organized by the Cebu Federation of Beat Journalists (CFBJ) in Mandaue City on Friday, Nov. 20, saying unreliable and expensive electricity could undermine the competitiveness of manufacturing, logistics and information technology businesses.

Anton Mari Perdices, chair of the Cebu Chamber of Commerce and Industry's Power Committee, said power shortages remain a major concern for businesses, particularly as Cebu and other parts of the Visayas have experienced red and yellow alerts.

"Businesses really need affordable, sustainable, and consistent power throughout for them to operate efficiently and plan accordingly," Perdices said.

He urged business groups and the media to continue monitoring the commitments of the National Grid Corporation of the Philippines, power generators and distribution utilities, saying industry discussions should lead to concrete action.

Perdices also called for more generation facilities to increase competition among power providers and potentially bring down electricity prices.

"The more generation facilities there are, the more competition there is that will automatically drive the price down," he said.

He cited an estimated additional annual power requirement of 150 megawatts in the Visayas, with Cebu accounting for about 60% of the demand.

He also stressed the need to complement renewable energy development with battery storage and other sources that can supply electricity during evening peak hours, when solar facilities cannot generate power.

Mark Anthony Ynoc, board member and immediate past president of the Mandaue Chamber of Commerce and Industry, said electricity prices are a major consideration for investors weighing locations for manufacturing plants and data centers.

Mandaue's manufacturing sector accounts for about 60% of the city's workforce, making reliable and reasonably priced electricity important to sustaining its industrial base, he said.

"If I have to start, it has to start with power," Ynoc said.

He cited electricity rates of about P8 to P10 per kilowatt-hour in Mongolia and Vietnam, compared with P16 to P17 in the Philippines, saying the cost difference could influence where data center developers choose to invest.

Power interruptions also affect production, increase operating expenses and expose machinery to potential damage, Ynoc said.

While businesses have adopted solar installations, battery storage and energy conservation measures to manage costs, he said these initiatives cannot fully meet industrial power requirements.

He also identified permitting delays, land acquisition and right-of-way negotiations as obstacles to the construction of generation and transmission facilities.

Ynoc said the Visayas could not fully access an estimated 1,900 megawatts of excess power in Luzon because of transmission constraints. The existing interconnection can reportedly transmit only about 250 megawatts.

For the information technology and business process management sector, power reliability is equally critical to maintaining operations and attracting international clients.

Darwin John Moises, president of the Cebu IT/BPM Organization, said electricity is essential to support the industry's technology infrastructure and workforce, which also needs communication skills and expertise in artificial intelligence and other emerging technologies.

"What is there? If we have the best speed and the best technology penetration, if there is no electricity to power it," Moises said.

He said outages can disrupt employees working remotely or under hybrid arrangements. Some companies have reduced office seating capacity, leaving workers without available workstations when they need to report onsite during power interruptions.

Such disruptions could affect service delivery, particularly among companies handling time-sensitive operations for overseas clients.

Moises said Cebu's operating costs are about 20% lower than those in the National Capital Region, providing the province with an advantage in attracting IT-BPM investments. However, high electricity prices could erode that cost advantage.

He added that multinational companies also assess their suppliers' dependence on backup generators when evaluating business continuity arrangements.

Leslie D. Lim, president of the Federation of Ecozone Service Providers' Visayas Chapter, said power interruptions could affect logistics companies through disruptions in manufacturing and shipment schedules.

Factories operating under just-in-time production systems and maintaining low inventory levels are particularly exposed to delays when electricity interruptions halt production, he said. Lower factory output can also reduce shipment volumes, affecting freight forwarders and other businesses in the supply chain.

Lim also cited rising fuel prices and surcharges as additional cost pressures for truckers and freight operators.

Despite the challenges, he said businesses in Cebu continue to coordinate and share information to manage disruptions.

"Eventually, because of our culture here in Cebu, we're very resilient and we're very communicative. We always talk about it and share notes on how to work about it," Lim said.
