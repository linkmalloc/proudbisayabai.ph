Floodwaters returned to several parts of Metro Cebu on Friday, July 31, a day after intense rains submerged communities, disrupted transport, and forced authorities to strengthen disaster response measures.

  

The flooding came after Cebu City recorded rainfall equivalent to around nine Olympic-size swimming pools within two hours on Thursday, July 30, overwhelming drainage systems and affecting residents, commuters, motorists, and students.

  

Cebu City Council disaster committee chairman Councilor Dave Tumulak said the rainfall volume contributed to the flooding experienced in several areas.

  

Based on Philippine Atmospheric, Geophysical and Astronomical Services Administration (PAGASA) data, around 22.2 millimeters of rain fell over Cebu City and Mandaue City, while Lapu-Lapu City recorded about 9 millimeters.

  

The downpour affected at least 16 areas in Cebu City, with floodwaters ranging from below knee-deep to chest-deep in some communities.

  

Among the worst-hit areas were Barangays Tisa, Guadalupe, Lahug, Bulacao, and Basak Pardo.

  

In La Paloma Village in Barangay Tisa, floodwaters reached around three meters, trapping residents inside the subdivision.

  

The Cebu City Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Office (CCDRRMO) rescued three residents, including a 74-year-old man, a 63-year-old man, and a 23-year-old woman.

  

The rescue involved the CCDRRMO Special Rescue Squad, Tisa Fire Brigade, Barangay Quick Response Team Habagat, Bureau of Fire Protection, and Provincial Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Office.

  

Responders navigated through strong currents and debris, including logs, furniture, roofing sheets, and electric bicycles.

  

The CCDRRMO reported no deaths or injuries from the flooding.

  

In Sitio Dakit, Barangay Guadalupe, residents also sought evacuation assistance after floodwaters reportedly reached the height of a basketball ring, preventing vehicles from entering the area.

  

Other affected locations included Tambisan in Barangay Tisa, Sitio Sacred Heart in Barangay Labangon, Villa Paloma Subdivision, N. Bacalso Avenue near Elizabeth Mall, and roads in Tinago, Punta, Kinasang-an, Tagunol, and Basak Pardo.

  

The Cebu City Transportation Office monitored flooding along major roads, including Colon Street, Legaspi Street corner V. Gullas Street, M.J. Cuenco Avenue, P. del Rosario Street, and N. Bacalso Avenue, causing slower traffic during the afternoon rush hour.

  

Authorities also monitored nine rivers and creeks, with Tagunol River recording the highest water level at about four meters.

  

Tumulak said clogged drainage systems contributed to the flooding and noted that drainage improvements remain necessary despite regular declogging efforts.

  

He said flooding has continued to challenge Cebu City since 2013, requiring long-term solutions beyond clearing drainage structures.
