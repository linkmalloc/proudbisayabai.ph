More than 31,600 pupils in Cebu are not reading at the level expected for their grade, prompting the Provincial Board to push for a coordinated literacy and learning recovery program across the province.

The figures cover Grade 1 to 3 learners at the end of School Year 2025-2026, according to an Oct. 4, 2026 report by the Second Congressional Commission on Education (EDCOM 2).

Talisay City recorded the highest number of struggling readers at 2,933, followed by Toledo City with 1,331, Consolacion with 1,211 and Liloan with 1,111.

Other localities with high numbers included Balamban with 865 pupils, Minglanilla with 752, Daanbantayan with 642, Carcar City with 609, Naga City with 607 and Barili with 560.

In response, the Provincial Board unanimously approved Resolution No. 3684-2026 on Oct. 26, urging the provincial government to develop a Cebu Provincial Early Literacy and Learning Recovery Action Plan.

Provincial Board Member Malcolm Sanchez authored the resolution, which calls for the Capitol to work with education authorities and local government units (LGUs) in identifying learning gaps and directing support to communities that need it most.

The proposed plan aims to consolidate existing literacy initiatives, set measurable targets and identify areas where additional learning resources and interventions are needed.

The Provincial Planning and Development Office would facilitate its preparation in coordination with the Department of Education-Central Visayas (DepEd 7), concerned Schools Division Offices and local governments.

Among the proposed steps are establishing a province-wide literacy baseline, identifying communities with the widest learning gaps, reviewing current interventions and assessing resource shortages that could be addressed through government and private-sector partnerships.

The initiative also proposes tapping higher education institutions to identify potential tutors for referral to DepEd and encouraging parents and communities to take a more active role in children's reading development.

Learning interventions may also be connected with existing health, nutrition and social welfare programs to address factors outside the classroom that could affect children's ability to learn.

The proposed framework would include monitoring mechanisms to measure improvements in reading skills, the number of learners reached by interventions and reductions in the number of struggling readers.

The resolution stressed the need to address reading difficulties during the early years of schooling, when learners develop the foundational skills needed for more demanding academic work.

DepEd would retain authority over curriculum, learner assessments, teaching standards and classroom interventions. The provincial plan is intended to complement, rather than replace, the department's existing programs.

The proposal builds on the Capitol's literacy campaign, “Basa Ta, Cebu,” which Gov. Pamela Baricuatro launched in August to encourage children to read and develop regular reading habits.

Under the campaign, the provincial government plans to acquire a mobile library through the Provincial School Board. The Provincial Information Office will manage the facility, which is expected to visit schools across the province weekly.

The visits will be coordinated with local government information officers and the Provincial Youth Development Office to organize reading activities and encourage students to engage with books.

Baricuatro has also connected the campaign to the need to develop critical thinking among young people amid the spread of misinformation.

“A Cebu that reads is a Cebu that thinks. And a Cebu that thinks is much harder to deceive,” she said during the campaign's launch.
