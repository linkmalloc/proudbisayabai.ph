Credit card issuers will be allowed to impose higher charges on installment loans and cash advances under revised limits issued by the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP), while the monthly cap on credit card finance charges will remain at 3 percent.

In a statement, the BSP said it increased the monthly add-on rate ceiling for credit card installment loans to 1.5 percent from the previous 1 percent.

The adjustment allows credit card issuers to charge an additional 0.5 percentage point in monthly add-on rates for installment transactions, subject to the new ceiling.

The central bank also raised the maximum one-time processing fee for cash advances to P300 from P200.

However, the 3-percent monthly cap on finance charges for credit card loans remains unchanged under the latest adjustments.

The changes are covered by BSP Circular No. 1242, issued on Nov. 10. The circular will take effect 15 calendar days after its publication in the Official Gazette or a newspaper of general circulation.

The revised ceilings will apply only to new transactions entered into on or after the circular's effectivity date.

Credit card installment loans entered into before the new rules take effect will remain subject to their existing terms and conditions.

This means the revised installment rate ceiling and cash advance processing fee will not automatically change the charges agreed upon under transactions made before the effectivity date.

The BSP said the adjustments aim to balance consumer protection with the need to give credit card issuers greater flexibility in setting charges amid rising operating costs.

The central bank first introduced ceilings on credit card fees and charges in 2020 to establish limits on the costs imposed on cardholders.

It partially adjusted these limits in 2023.

The latest circular follows the BSP's continuing review of the limits, taking into account economic conditions and the financial industry's operating environment.

“The BSP regularly reviews the caps on credit card fees and charges.

It takes into account overall economic conditions, the soundness of banks and other financial institutions, and consistency with the BSP’s monetary policy settings, among other factors,” the central bank said.
