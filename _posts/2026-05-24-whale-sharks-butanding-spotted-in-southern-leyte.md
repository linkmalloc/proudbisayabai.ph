Pintuyan — The calm and pristine waters of this coastal town have once again become a sanctuary for one of the ocean’s most majestic creatures. Ten whale sharks, locally known as “butanding,” were recently spotted swimming gracefully off the shores of Pintuyan, further cementing Southern Leyte’s growing reputation as one of the Philippines’ premier destinations for ethical and sustainable marine tourism.



The remarkable encounter was captured through a stunning drone shot taken on April 10, 2026, by Swiss guest Mr. David Lohrer. According to local observations, the whale sharks have been present in the area since November, marking the beginning of their seasonal migration in the waters surrounding Southern Leyte. Their continued presence has drawn excitement among marine enthusiasts, conservation advocates, photographers, and eco-tourists eager to witness the gentle giants thriving in their natural environment.



Unlike other destinations where whale shark interactions have sparked concerns over overfeeding and commercialization, Pintuyan takes pride in promoting responsible and conservation-driven tourism practices. Local authorities and tourism stakeholders continue to advocate for ethical encounters that prioritize the protection, safety, and welfare of the whale sharks while preserving the ecological balance of the marine ecosystem.



Whale sharks, recognized as the largest fish species in the world, are known for their peaceful nature despite their enormous size. Their seasonal appearance in Southern Leyte not only highlights the richness of the province’s marine biodiversity but also underscores the importance of sustainable tourism initiatives that benefit both local communities and the environment.



Tourism officials are encouraging visitors interested in experiencing whale shark interaction in Southern Leyte to coordinate with the Municipal Tourism Office for proper orientation, documentation, and adherence to established environmental guidelines. Authorities stress that maintaining respectful distances, avoiding physical contact, and following regulated interaction protocols are crucial steps in ensuring the long-term conservation of these marine giants.



The whale shark season in Pintuyan typically runs from November through April, making it an ideal destination for travelers seeking authentic and eco-friendly marine experiences in the Visayas. Beyond whale shark encounters, Southern Leyte also offers breathtaking coastal scenery, rich marine life, and a peaceful atmosphere that continues to attract nature lovers from around the world.



As sightings of the butanding continue to captivate locals and tourists alike, Pintuyan stands as a shining example of how communities can successfully balance tourism development with environmental stewardship — proving that responsible travel and marine conservation can go hand in hand.
