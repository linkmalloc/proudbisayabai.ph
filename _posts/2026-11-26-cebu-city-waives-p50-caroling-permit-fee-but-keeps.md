Cebu City residents can keep the P50 in their pockets this Christmas as Mayor Nestor Archival waives the permit fee for groups going house to house to sing Christmas carols.

  

Archival said carolers would still need to register with the city government, but they would no longer have to pay for the permit.

  

“Walay bayad. Mu-register lang sila,” the mayor said.

  

He explained that registration would allow authorities to identify individuals visiting homes and establishments during the holiday season, following complaints from residents about people allegedly using caroling as a cover for theft.

  

“Daghan kaayong nagreklamo sa mga kabalayan, kanang naa sa mga institusyon, naay mga tawo nga mag-manaygon unya mangawat diay,” Archival said. (Many residents and institutions have complained that some people go around caroling but actually steal.)

  

The mayor described the registration process as a form of profiling that would help the city determine whether carolers are residents of Cebu City or visitors from other areas.

  

Those who wish to participate in the Christmas activity may register at City Hall.

  

Archival also reminded caroling groups to limit their activities to residential areas and establishments. Singing for donations along streets or inside vehicles is prohibited, he said, because it could expose carolers and motorists to road accidents and turn the tradition into roadside solicitation.

  

He said the city would direct the Business Permits and Licensing Office and enforcement teams to review the penalties and guidelines for violations.

  

Archival stressed that the city did not intend to arrest people who wanted to earn money or take part in the Christmas tradition, but expected them to follow the rules.

  

Under Cebu City Ordinance No. 1931, caroling is traditionally allowed from December 16 until the first Sunday of January of the following year, between 7 a.m. and 10 p.m.

  

The ordinance allows groups to go house to house and visit establishments but prohibits caroling inside public utility jeepneys, modern public utility vehicles and similar forms of public transportation.

  

Archival acknowledged that the existing rules include a P50 permit fee. He said the city would study how to adjust the provision to implement the waiver.

  

He also noted that some groups continue caroling beyond the traditional holiday period, sometimes extending their activities until February. However, he said he would not impose an additional restriction on the duration of the activity.
