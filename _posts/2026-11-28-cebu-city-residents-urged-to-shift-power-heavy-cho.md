Cebu City residents are being urged to move electricity-intensive household activities to the morning as the city faces a tight power supply situation that could trigger interruptions during peak consumption hours.

  

Mayor Nestor Archival Sr. said households should avoid running several appliances at once in the evening, particularly between 4:30 p.m. and 8:30 p.m., when electricity demand is highest.

  

The mayor made the appeal after meeting with the National Grid Corporation of the Philippines (NGCP) and Visayan Electric (VECO) to discuss the condition of the city's power supply.

  

“Kung naa gani atong pagabuhaton like charging, panglaba, ari nato ibutang sa buntag,” Archival said. (If we have activities to do, such as charging devices and doing laundry, we should schedule them in the morning.)

  

Archival warned that simultaneous heavy electricity use could put additional pressure on the power system and increase the risk of widespread outages.

  

He said NGCP explained during the meeting that the power system had reached red alert status, the highest alert level, which could result in extensive rotational brownouts.

  

The supply situation was partly attributed to damage to Toledo-based power plant Therma Visayas Inc. (TVI) 2 and reduced operating efficiency at TVI 1.

  

Archival said TVI 1's output had dropped from 169 to 120, although he did not specify the unit of measurement.

  

To help address the shortage, NGCP reportedly secured an additional 450 units of power from Mindanao through an existing connection to Cebu.

  

VECO also asked independent power producers operating within commercial establishments, including malls, to activate their facilities. This would allow some establishments to rely on their own generators and free up electricity from the main grid for other consumers, including households.

  

Archival said the additional supply from Mindanao was helping ease concerns over possible power interruptions in Cebu City.

  

He added that NGCP expected repairs to TVI 2 to be completed on or before December 9, with a more stable power supply anticipated by December 10.

  

The mayor said power conditions were expected to improve once the damaged facility resumed operations.
