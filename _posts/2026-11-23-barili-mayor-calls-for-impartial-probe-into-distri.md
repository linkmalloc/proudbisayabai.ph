Barili Mayor Pablo John “John-John” Garcia IV has urged the Cebu Provincial Government to conduct a fair and transparent investigation into complaints involving the Barili District Hospital, saying affected families deserve answers and the incidents should lead to improvements in healthcare services.

Garcia expressed sympathy for the family of a woman who died along with her unborn child after being brought to the provincial government-run hospital. He also wished for the recovery of another child reportedly treated at the facility after suffering from fever for several days.

“Every patient deserves proper attention, every family deserves to be heard, and every concern deserves a fair and serious response,” Garcia said in a statement.

He called on the provincial government and its Integrated Provincial Health Office (IPHO), which oversees the hospital, to examine the reported incidents thoroughly and without bias.

Garcia said all parties involved should be given the opportunity to present their accounts before investigators reach conclusions or recommend appropriate action.

“Among gilauman nga mapadali ang imbestigasyon aron masayran ang kamatuoran ug matubag ang mga pangutana sa mga pamilya nga naapektuhan,” (We hope the investigation will be expedited so the truth can be established and the questions of the affected families can be answered) he said.

The mayor's statement comes amid mounting scrutiny of the hospital over complaints involving its personnel and services, including the death of Angelle Fe Isugan and her unborn child on Nov. 4.

Isugan's husband, Jhovell Patlingrao, said he brought her to the hospital after she experienced severe bleeding. In a video circulated on social media, he questioned an internal examination reportedly performed on his wife despite prenatal records indicating placenta previa.

He also raised concerns about the time it took to refer her to a higher-level medical facility as her condition deteriorated. These allegations have yet to be established through an official investigation.

The case has drawn the attention of Third District Rep. Karen Garcia, who earlier urged the provincial government to expedite its inquiry into the deaths and other complaints involving the hospital.

The controversy also triggered a public exchange between the congresswoman and provincial health consultant Dr. Nikki Catalan, who presented information about the hospital's staffing, patient load and available medical services.

Catalan said the facility had 58 personnel when the Baricuatro administration took office, including two full-time doctors and 17 nurses. The provincial government added 57 workers by July 2026, bringing the hospital's workforce to 115.

Meanwhile, Garcia clarified that the municipal government operates a separate healthcare facility, the Marlon F. Garcia Infirmary, which is directly managed by the local government and is not under the district hospital's administration.

The infirmary provides a Dengue Fast Lane, general ultrasound, 2D echocardiography, X-ray and laboratory services, including complete blood count, urinalysis and Dengue Duo testing.

Although it primarily serves Barili residents, the municipal facility also accepts patients from neighboring towns.

Garcia said the complaints involving the district hospital should be addressed through a thorough investigation and used as an opportunity to improve healthcare delivery in Barili and nearby communities.
