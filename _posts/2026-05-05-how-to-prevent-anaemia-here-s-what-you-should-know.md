**What is anaemia?**

Anemia is a condition where your body doesn’t have enough healthy red blood cells or hemoglobin to carry adequate oxygen to tissues. This can lead to symptoms such as fatigue, weakness, pale skin, shortness of breath, and dizziness. Anaemia can result from iron deficiency, vitamin deficiencies, blood loss, or underlying medical conditions.

**Tips to prevent anaemia**

Preventing anaemia starts with proper nutrition and healthy habits. Here are key ways to reduce your risk:

**1. Choose iron-rich foods**

Iron is essential for producing hemoglobin. Include foods like:

* Lean meat, liver, and poultry
* Fish and shellfish
* Dark leafy greens (spinach, kangkong)
* Beans, lentils, and tofu
* Iron-fortified cereals

**2. Increase folate intake**

Folate (vitamin B9) helps produce red blood cells. Good sources include:

* Leafy greens
* Citrus fruits
* Avocado
* Legumes

**3. Get enough vitamin B12**

Vitamin B12 supports red blood cell formation and nerve function. You can get it from:

* Meat and fish
* Eggs and dairy products
* Fortified plant-based milk

**4. Include vitamin A in your diet**

Vitamin A helps your body use stored iron effectively. Sources include:

* Carrots and sweet potatoes
* Mangoes and papaya
* Eggs and dairy

**5. Pair iron with vitamin C**

Vitamin C improves iron absorption. Combine iron-rich foods with:

* Citrus fruits (orange, calamansi)
* Tomatoes
* Bell peppers

**6. Limit tea and coffee during meals**

These beverages can reduce iron absorption if taken with food.

**7. Maintain regular health check-ups**

Early detection helps manage anaemia before it becomes severe.

A balanced diet rich in iron, folate, vitamin A, and B12 is key to preventing anaemia. If you experience persistent symptoms, consult a healthcare professional for proper diagnosis and treatment.
