Rising temperatures are putting more than people's health at risk, with mounting consequences for workers, food production, transportation, electricity use and access to education.

The Cebu Provincial Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Office (PDRRMO), citing findings from the 2025 Lancet Countdown, reported that heat-related deaths worldwide reached 546,000 in 2024, up 23 percent from levels recorded in the 1990s.

The growing toll highlights the dangers of prolonged heat exposure, particularly for vulnerable populations and workers whose jobs require them to spend long hours outdoors.

The International Labour Organization (ILO) estimated that about 2.4 billion workers worldwide, or nearly two-thirds of the global workforce, face exposure to excessive heat.

Beyond the direct health risks, high temperatures are also cutting into working hours and economic output. An estimated 639 billion working hours were lost worldwide in 2024 due to heat exposure, resulting in about US$1.09 trillion in economic losses, equivalent to roughly one percent of global gross domestic product.

Transportation systems also face increasing costs as extreme heat disrupts operations. The World Bank projected that heat-related disruptions could raise Europe's annual transportation costs by US$12.2 billion by 2100.

Agriculture is another vulnerable sector, as prolonged high temperatures can damage crops, lower yields and threaten food supplies.

The PDRRMO, citing a 2023 study by Romanello and other researchers, said heat-related crop failures could expose an additional 127 million people to food insecurity, showing how rising temperatures could worsen existing pressures on food production and distribution.

The growing need for cooling also adds pressure to electricity systems. The United Nations Environment Programme (UNEP) estimated that cooling already accounts for about 20 percent of global electricity consumption as households, businesses and public facilities rely on air-conditioning to cope with higher temperatures.

Schools are likewise experiencing disruptions. UNICEF reported that heatwaves forced school closures affecting 171 million students worldwide in 2024, making extreme heat the most disruptive climate-related hazard for education that year.

School closures and adjustments to classroom activities can interrupt children's learning while requiring families, teachers and school administrators to respond to unsafe conditions.

Through its Andam Sugbo information campaign, the Cebu PDRRMO is urging communities to recognize extreme heat as a broader concern that affects public health, livelihoods and essential services.

Adapted from the United Nations Office for Disaster Risk Reduction (UNDRR), the campaign highlights the need for coordinated preparedness efforts to protect communities, sustain livelihoods and strengthen resilience as temperatures continue to rise.
