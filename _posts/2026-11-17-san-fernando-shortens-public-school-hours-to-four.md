CEBU, Philippines – Public elementary and high schools in the Municipality of San Fernando, southern Cebu, will implement shortened class hours starting November 17 as a precaution against rising temperatures and the anticipated effects of Super El Niño.

Under the new schedule announced by the local government, classes will be divided into two four-hour sessions daily to reduce students’ and teachers’ exposure to prolonged heat, particularly during the hottest hours of the day.

The morning session will run from 6:00 a.m. to 10:00 a.m., while the afternoon session will be held from 2:00 p.m. to 6:00 p.m.

Schools offering elective subjects may extend their classes until 12:00 noon, subject to the arrangements of their respective administrators.

Meanwhile, the Cebu Technological University (CTU)–San Fernando Extension Campus and private schools in the municipality may independently decide whether to adopt the shortened class schedule.

Cebu City considers earlier class schedules

In a separate development, the Cebu City Council has approved a resolution proposing earlier class schedules for elementary schools amid concerns over increasing heat exposure among learners.

However, the measure is only a recommendation and does not require schools to modify their existing class schedules. Implementation remains subject to the discretion of school administrators.

The adjustments come as schools and local government units explore measures to protect students and teachers from excessive heat, particularly with the expected development of Super El Niño, which could bring prolonged periods of above-normal temperatures and drier conditions.

Authorities continue to emphasize the importance of prioritizing the health, safety, and welfare of learners and school personnel as communities prepare for changing weather conditions.
