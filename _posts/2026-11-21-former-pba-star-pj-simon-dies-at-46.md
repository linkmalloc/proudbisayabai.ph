Former PBA star Peter June “PJ” Simon, remembered as one of the Purefoods/Magnolia franchise’s most dependable scorers and a key member of its 2014 Grand Slam team, died on Friday, Nov. 20, after collapsing during a basketball game. He was 46.

The Purefoods/Magnolia franchise confirmed Simon’s death in a social media post, paying tribute to the former player for his humility, professionalism and 16-year career with the team.

“Isa si PJ sa pinakamagandang ehemplo ng isang professional basketball player,” the franchise said, describing Simon as a player who competed with quiet confidence and wore jersey No. 8 with pride.

The team also said Simon earned the affection of not only its supporters but Filipino basketball fans in general.

Videos circulating online showed teammates fanning Simon on the sidelines before carrying him off the court following an apparent collapse during what appeared to be a recreational basketball game in Davao City. No official cause of death had been announced as of Friday.

Simon, known as “The Scoring Apostle,” built a 16-year PBA career entirely with the Purefoods/Magnolia franchise after entering the league as the 43rd overall pick in the 2001 draft.

Before joining Purefoods in 2004, the Cotabato native established himself in the Metropolitan Basketball Association and the Philippine Basketball League, where he won two championships and one Most Valuable Player award.

He became a reliable scoring option alongside fellow franchise star James Yap, forming a formidable backcourt combination. Simon earned eight PBA All-Star selections and won the Mr. Quality Minutes award twice for his consistent contributions, often coming off the bench.

One of the defining moments of his career came in 2014, when he helped the San Mig Coffee Mixers complete a rare PBA Grand Slam under coach Tim Cone. The team also featured Yap, Marc Pingris and Mark Barroca.

Simon finished his PBA career with eight championships and averages of 10.1 points in nearly 22 minutes per game across almost 700 appearances.

Magnolia retired his No. 8 jersey in 2019 in recognition of his contributions to the franchise. He formally retired from the PBA in 2020 but returned briefly to professional basketball in 2024 with the Davao Occidental Tigers in the Maharlika Pilipinas Basketball League. He retired again the following year.

Yap expressed shock over the sudden loss of his former teammate and longtime friend in a social media tribute.

“This is so sudden, Part. I didn’t expect it at all,” Yap wrote.

“Thank you for everything, Part, for all the times we spent together that I will never forget. We still have a basketball tour to do. This is painful, Part.”
