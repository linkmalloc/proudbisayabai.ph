The Philippines is exploring a potential partnership with SpaceX to develop commercial rocket launch sites in the country, President Ferdinand R. Marcos Jr. said Friday.

  

Marcos said discussions with the aerospace company founded by billionaire Elon Musk remain at an early stage, but the government hopes the Philippines could become part of SpaceX's expanding launch operations.

  

“We are just beginning any kind of discussions thus far. But what is happening is he is expanding the launch sites for SpaceX. And maybe the Philippines can look into it,” Marcos told reporters in Shenzhen, China.

  

The President clarified that the proposed venture would focus on developing commercial launch facilities rather than establishing a national space program aimed at ambitious missions such as sending astronauts to the moon or Mars.

  

“We are not trying to be NASA. We are not trying to send a man to the moon or to Mars. What we are trying to do is to get into the business of launch sites,” he said.

  

Marcos said the Philippines' location along the western Pacific could make it suitable for rocket launches.

  

He also explained that the recent rocket launch in Cagayan was part of efforts to establish a commercial launch-site business that could attract foreign operators, not the beginning of a national space program.

  

“The launch of our rocket in Cagayan is not the beginning of a space program. It is the beginning of a business that will attract launches. You can pay for that,” he said.

  

The arrangement could also give the Philippines a more convenient and potentially less costly way to deploy its own satellites, Marcos added.

  

Asked about reports that Musk was considering developing two islands in the Philippines, the President said he had no information about the matter and that no concrete arrangements had been made.

  

“I don't have any details on it. I don't think there are any details on it,” he said.
