Mandaue City is being urged to establish a dedicated cybersecurity unit and strengthen its digital security infrastructure as more government services move online and cyberattacks threaten sensitive public records.

The Mandaue City Council approved a resolution calling on the local government to create a rapid-response team and centralized monitoring center to detect, contain and address potential cyber incidents.

Councilor Carlo Fortuna said the measure asks the city's Management Information Systems Office (MISO) and the Office of the City Mayor to establish a cybersecurity unit that would protect government networks and the personal information entrusted to city offices.

“Nagpasar ta og resolution nga awhag sa atong Management Information Systems Office ug sa Office of the City Mayor nga mag-create og cybersecurity unit sa atong kaugalingong local government tungod sa sunod-sunod nga cyberattacks sa lain-laing ahensya sa gobyerno,” Fortuna said.

He cited reported cyberattacks involving national government agencies, including the Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) and the Bureau of Internal Revenue (BIR), as reasons for local governments to improve their defenses before their own systems become targets.

Fortuna specifically raised concerns about the city's Business Processing and Licensing Office (BPLO), where business permit applications and renewals are already processed online.

He said applicants provide personal information when creating accounts and completing transactions, making the security of the office's digital records an important concern.

“Most likely ang mga local governments na ang sunod, especially atong Business Processing and Licensing Office (BPLO) nga kasagaran online na ang transactions,” he said.

Fortuna also disclosed that he personally received an email from the SEC informing him that users' personal information had been harvested in a cyberattack.

He warned that compromised information could be misused, including for identity theft, and said hackers had reportedly threatened to sell the stolen data.

“Personal information is critical information since it can be used for identity theft,” Fortuna said.

Under the approved resolution, MISO will lead the proposed cybersecurity initiatives, which include strengthening network defenses through layered security controls, secure Domain Name System configurations and network segmentation.

The measure proposes creating a Cybersecurity and IT Audit Division within MISO to handle network monitoring, vulnerability assessments, endpoint security and threat mitigation.

It also calls for a Local Computer Emergency Response Team (LGU-CERT), which would respond to cybersecurity incidents, contain attacks and implement recovery procedures to restore affected systems.

Another key component is a centralized Security Operations Center (SOC) that would consolidate the monitoring of the city's digital infrastructure.

The proposed center would use real-time logging, security information and event management tools, and firewall dashboards to help detect suspicious activities.

The resolution further directs MISO to incorporate incident response procedures under the National Computer Emergency Response Team's (NCERT) Disruption Playbooks.

These procedures are intended to guide the city in detecting, containing and mitigating cyber threats, system intrusions and website spoofing attempts.

The city mayor is also requested to designate or strengthen the capacity of the local government's Data Protection Officer and breach-notification officers.

They would oversee vulnerability disclosures and ensure compliance with technical reporting requirements.

Beyond establishing specialized units and monitoring systems, the proposal calls for regular cybersecurity exercises, technical training and information campaigns to improve digital security practices among city government employees.

MISO would coordinate these activities with the Human Resource Management Office and the Department of Information and Communications Technology 7.

Funding for the proposed cybersecurity software, hardware firewalls, system audits, office improvements and training programs would come from available city government funds, subject to existing budgeting, accounting and auditing rules.

The resolution cited the increasing volume of personal information, corporate records and other sensitive data handled by local governments as public services increasingly depend on digital platforms.

It also referenced DICT Department Circulars Nos. 003, 004, 005 and 006, series of 2024, which cover digital asset protection, layered security controls, incident reporting and vulnerability disclosure.

Fortuna said the city should strengthen its preparations rather than wait for a cyberattack to expose weaknesses in its systems.

“We have to double time. We cannot just react; at least we need to be one step ahead to minimize or even totally prevent any cyberattacks on our systems, labi na sa LGU sa Dakbayan sa Mandaue,” he said.
