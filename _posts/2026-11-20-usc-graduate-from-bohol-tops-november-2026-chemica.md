A University of San Carlos (USC) graduate from Sikatuna, Bohol, topped the November 2026 Chemical Engineers Computer-Based Licensure Examination (CHELE), leading 814 successful examinees with a rating of 93.30%.

  

Timothy John Alcantara Jala secured first place in the examination, according to the results released by the Professional Regulation Commission (PRC).

  

His achievement brought recognition to USC, where he earned his chemical engineering degree, and his hometown of Sikatuna.

  

In a social media post following the announcement, Jala thanked his family, teachers, classmates, review center and everyone who supported him throughout his journey toward becoming a licensed chemical engineer.

  

He also expressed gratitude to his late father, whom he included in his acknowledgment.

  

"I will reserve the greater portion of my comments for the oath-taking ceremony to be held in Manila this December," Jala said.

  

The PRC recorded 814 passers out of 955 examinees, equivalent to an 85.24% passing rate.

  

Cyra Pineda Panganiban of the University of the Philippines-Los Baños placed second with 91.10%, followed by Alessandro Niño Glindro Roa of the University of the Philippines-Diliman with 90.80%.

  

Johnaxxel Mark Tomulin Bonjoc of the University of the Philippines-Visayas in Iloilo City ranked fourth with 90.60%, while Mack Lauron Abarca Ang of Ateneo de Davao University took fifth place with 90.30%.

  

Three examinees tied for sixth place with 90%: Gabriel Oliver Miranda Atrero of Pamantasan ng Lungsod ng Maynila, Jumairah Jasmine Macarik Macalabo of Mindanao State University-Iligan Institute of Technology, and Lance Angelo Tamondong Rovillos of Mapua University.

  

Mark Joseph Pantaleon Pastorillo of Colegio San Agustin-Bacolod City and Paul Niño Quisias Rodriguez of Mapua University shared seventh place with 89.90%.

  

Three others tied for eighth place with 89.70%: Ritche Emmanuoel Romero De Villa of the University of the Philippines-Los Baños, Lord Shalom Baldamor Dizon of Mindanao State University-Iligan Institute of Technology, and Charles Aldwin Samson Sarzaba of the University of the Philippines-Diliman.

  

Cheska Hilary Gomez Gopez of the University of Santo Tomas ranked ninth with 89.60%.

  

Meanwhile, Aldrin Guleng Madriaga of Saint Louis University and Gregory Ulric Arrieta Yoro of the University of the Philippines-Diliman tied for 10th place with 89.40%.
