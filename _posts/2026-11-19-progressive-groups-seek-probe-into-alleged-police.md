Progressive groups are seeking an investigation into alleged police violence and attempted arrests during the Nov. 16 protest along Colon Street in Cebu City, disputing the police account of the confrontation that erupted while demonstrators were burning an effigy.

  

At a press conference on Wednesday, the groups accused police personnel of continuing to push and attempt to apprehend protesters even after the fire had been extinguished and organizers had announced the end of the program.

  

Anthony Malibago, head paralegal of Karapatan Central Visayas, said the groups' initial monitoring showed that several protesters suffered injuries and other effects during the confrontation. One person was reportedly brought to an emergency room.

  

Malibago said the protest was peaceful and nearing its conclusion around noon when disaster response personnel approached a small symbolic effigy being burned by demonstrators.

  

He described the effigy as less than three feet tall and said organizers had assigned marshals to manage the activity and maintain safety.

  

According to Malibago, fire rescue personnel moved toward the burning effigy and entered the area where journalists were documenting the protest.

  

He disputed the police account that a demonstrator tried to grab a fire extinguisher, saying the protester only attempted to redirect its hose. The protester was allegedly hit on the head before police tried to pull him away from the group.

  

Malibago said more officers carrying riot shields then advanced toward the demonstrators, pushing the crowd and attempting to break its formation.

  

He further alleged that police directed a fire extinguisher at protesters even after the effigy had been put out.

  

“Daghan nila ang naglisod ug ginhawa, nagka-blurry ang panan-aw,” Malibago said, describing reports of difficulty breathing and blurred vision among protesters.

  

Others reportedly experienced nausea, fainted or sustained injuries after being pushed with riot shields. Members of the media and student publications were also affected, he added.

  

Malibago claimed that police continued pushing and attempting to apprehend demonstrators after organizers announced that the program had ended and participants were dispersing.

  

The groups called on authorities to identify the police officers and commanders responsible for what they described as unnecessary force and attempted arrests without clear legal basis.

  

They also sought the preservation and examination of available video footage, witness accounts and medical records, as well as documentation by the Commission on Human Rights, whose personnel were present during the protest.

  

BAYAN Central Visayas chairperson Jaime Paglinawan also condemned the police response, saying the use of force raised concerns over the protection of the protesters' rights to free expression and peaceful assembly.

  

Paglinawan accused authorities of using Batas Pambansa Blg. 880, or the Public Assembly Act, to intimidate demonstrators and discourage them from exercising their constitutional rights.

  

Malibago said the law does not give police unlimited authority to use force, even when they consider an activity a public-safety concern. He questioned why intervention allegedly continued after the fire was extinguished and organizers had tried to de-escalate the situation.

  

The groups' allegations contrast with the Cebu City Police Office's (CCPO) account that the confrontation began when protesters proceeded with the effigy burning despite police warnings about overhead electrical wires and potential fire hazards.

  

Police Lieutenant Colonel Gerard Ace Pelare previously said authorities allowed the rally to proceed despite the absence of a permit, citing maximum tolerance and their responsibility to maintain public safety.

  

He also claimed that some protesters became aggressive when disaster response personnel tried to extinguish the fire.

  

The CCPO reported that two police officers sustained minor injuries and said it was investigating the incident, including the alleged effigy burning and disruption of public order.

  

Police also announced plans to file charges against 12 protesters. Malibago said the move could intimidate people seeking to exercise their right to join public demonstrations.

  

The Nov. 16 mobilization brought together students and members of various organizations who marched from Fuente Osmeña to Gaisano Metro Colon to oppose the proposed Pax Silica initiative and US involvement in the Philippines.

  

Malibago estimated that around 1,700 students and organization members joined the protest, while the College Editors Guild of the Philippines-Cebu reported that around 1,000 students participated in the walkout from their classes.

  

The groups urged authorities to address the allegations and protect the rights of protesters and journalists covering public assemblies.
