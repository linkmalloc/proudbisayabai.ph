The Philippine Red Cross (PRC) has about 29,000 bags of blood available across its nationwide network, assuring patients and hospitals of sufficient supplies despite continuing dengue monitoring and demand for blood products.

PRC Secretary General Gwendolyn Pang said the organization operates more than 100 blood service facilities nationwide, allowing it to move supplies between areas based on demand and delivery time.

If Cebu runs short of blood, the PRC can source supplies from Bohol, Dumaguete or Manila, depending on which location can deliver them faster, she said.

“We're networked. So, that's the advantage of Red Cross because if it's not in one area, we can get it in other areas,” Pang said in an interview.

She clarified that blood itself is free, but patients pay a processing fee to cover the costs of blood bags, laboratory reagents, testing, storage, transportation and medical personnel.

The PRC follows processing rates prescribed by the Department of Health (DOH). Patients who cannot afford the fees may seek financial assistance through the Blood Samaritan Program established by PRC Chairman Richard Gordon.

Pang also urged the public to donate blood regularly, explaining that different blood products have limited storage periods and require careful inventory management.

Whole blood can be stored for 35 days, while platelets, which dengue, cancer and bleeding disorder patients may need, last only three to five days. Frozen plasma can be stored for up to a year.

Because of these differences, the PRC discourages one-time, large-scale donation drives that could leave the organization with supplies that expire before hospitals can use them.

Pang encouraged hospitals to coordinate their projected blood requirements with the PRC to help ensure that supplies reach areas where they are needed.

Patients and hospitals having difficulty securing blood may contact the organization's nationwide toll-free emergency hotline at 1158. Pang said the PRC would also cover transportation costs when it needs to ship blood from other areas.

The assurance comes as the DOH continues to monitor dengue infections nationwide, although reported cases remained lower than the previous year's figures.

The health department recorded 205,347 dengue cases from Jan. 4 to Oct. 14, 2026, down 22 percent from the 261,749 cases reported during the same period in 2025. The case fatality rate stood at 0.38 percent.

In its latest epidemiological report, the DOH recorded 22,095 cases from Sept. 17 to 30, a two-percent decrease from the 22,479 cases reported from Sept. 3 to 16.

Despite the overall decline, 11 provinces and highly urbanized cities across nine regions reported localized increases.

The DOH continues to monitor unusual increases and strengthen mosquito control measures through the removal of breeding sites, ovitrap and larval trapping, and targeted larviciding.

It also urged communities to follow its “Alas Kwatro Kontra Mosquito” campaign and the 4Ts: Taob, Taktak, Tuyo and Takip, which call for emptying, disposing of, drying and covering containers that can collect stagnant water.
