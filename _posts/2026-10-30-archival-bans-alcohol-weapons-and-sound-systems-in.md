Cebu City Mayor Nestor Archival has urged families observing Kalag-Kalag to leave alcohol, firearms and sound systems at home as authorities prepare for the influx of cemetery visitors on Nov. 1 and 2.

  

The restrictions aim to prevent disturbances and preserve the solemn atmosphere of All Saints’ Day and All Souls’ Day, when families gather to pray and remember their departed loved ones.

  

Archival warned visitors against bringing firearms and other objects that could cause injuries.

  

> “Ang pagdala ug mga hinagiban nga makasamad, kadtong mga kanang mga firearms, number one jud na,” he said.

  

He also prohibited sound systems, saying loud music could disrupt prayers and other religious observances inside cemetery grounds.

  

Alcoholic beverages are likewise not allowed. Archival advised people who are already intoxicated not to attempt to enter cemeteries, as they may be denied entry.

  

The mayor said designated areas have been provided for vendors to help regulate commercial activity and maintain order during the observance.

  

The reminders come as security agencies and city personnel prepare for one of the busiest periods for cemetery visits.

  

The Cebu City Police Office is deploying more than 1,000 police personnel to cemeteries, memorial parks and churches across the city. They will be supported by 553 city government workers and 80 Bureau of Fire Protection personnel.

  

Across Central Visayas, the Police Regional Office 7 is mobilizing 2,914 police officers, 1,702 personnel from partner agencies and 5,491 force multipliers to secure 363 cemeteries in the region.

  

The deployment aims to manage visitor movement, maintain peace and order, and ensure that authorities can respond to emergencies throughout the observance.

  

Authorities are reminding visitors to check the rules of their respective cemeteries, follow instructions from personnel on duty and avoid bringing prohibited items.
