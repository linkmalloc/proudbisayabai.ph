Public school students and teachers in Minglanilla will spend fewer hours in classrooms starting Monday, Nov. 16, as the southern Cebu town adjusts its schedule to reduce exposure to extreme heat ahead of the anticipated Super El Niño.

Mayor Lheslen Abella-Enad announced Saturday, Nov. 14, that public schools would limit classroom sessions to four hours daily until further notice following an agreement with the district supervisor.

"Aron maprotektahan ang atong mga estudyante ug magtutudlo, mahimo na lang upat ka oras matag session ang klase kada adlaw effective Lunes," Enad said.

Under the revised schedule, morning classes will run from 6 a.m. to 10 a.m., while afternoon sessions will be held from 2 p.m. to 6 p.m.

Schools that currently hold classes from 8 a.m. to 3 p.m. may adopt either schedule, depending on their administrators' assessment of their respective circumstances.

Enad also encouraged private schools to determine their own adjustments based on their operating conditions.

Minglanilla's move comes as other local governments in Cebu consider changes to school schedules in preparation for the 2026-2027 El Niño phenomenon.

In Cebu City, the City Council approved an amended resolution on Nov. 10 recommending earlier face-to-face classes for elementary pupils from 5:30 a.m. to 10 a.m. every Monday to Thursday, with Modular Distance Learning on Fridays.

Sponsored by Councilor Dave Tumulak, the proposal seeks to limit children's exposure to high temperatures by moving classes to cooler hours.

However, the council clarified that the resolution is only a recommendation and does not automatically change existing school schedules.

Mayor Nestor Archival said the Cebu City Local School Board must first assess the proposal, including its possible effects on children's health and sleeping habits.

Councilor Sisinio Andales also raised concerns about requiring pupils to wake up before dawn, saying the arrangement could affect their rest. He proposed shorter classes from 7 a.m. to noon instead.

Tumulak said schools have varying operating conditions, classroom capacities and locations, making a single schedule difficult to apply across the city.

The resolution calls on the city government to coordinate with the Department of Education and elementary schools to determine suitable temporary adjustments.
