The United States plans to provide the Philippines with USD2 billion in foreign military financing, including USD400 million intended to strengthen the country's operational reach and deterrence capabilities in the South China Sea, the White House announced Thursday.

The proposed financing package would help equip the Philippines with defense and security capabilities sourced from American suppliers, following US President Donald Trump's state visit to the country on Nov. 18.

Trump visited the Philippines after attending the Association of Southeast Asian Nations-US Special Summit and the Quadrilateral Security Dialogue meeting in Manila.

The White House said the USD400 million allocation would support the Armed Forces of the Philippines' acquisition of long-range fires, command and control systems, critical infrastructure and multidomain drones.

It said the financing aligns with Trump's America First Arms Transfer Strategy, which seeks to direct increased defense spending by US allies toward American manufacturers.

"President Trump's America First Arms Transfer Strategy ensures that the surge in allies' spending benefits the U.S. defense industry, reindustrializing America and supporting American jobs while arming our partners with the best capabilities in world," the White House said.

The Department of National Defense has also secured a USD37-million contract with a US company to provide the Philippines with artificial intelligence-enabled mission support, according to the White House.

The department also committed to working with trusted US technology providers on a USD63-million initiative to modernize its defense cloud infrastructure for artificial intelligence applications.

Beyond military cooperation, the two governments agreed to explore an intergovernmental agreement on civil nuclear cooperation.

The proposed agreement would facilitate the entry of more US nuclear companies into the Philippines and support the country's energy requirements. If finalized, it would be the first US civil nuclear intergovernmental agreement with an Indo-Pacific partner, the White House said.

Other announcements during Trump's visit included connectivity services from US company Astranis through its AGILA satellite and Subic Grain's plan to construct a USD40-million fuel storage depot at Subic Bay.

The two countries also committed to expanding bilateral trade in energy and agriculture.

Trump earlier described the trade commitment as "the biggest trade deal we've ever made by far, and a fair deal for both countries."
