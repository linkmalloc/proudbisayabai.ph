Cebu provincial board members are urging the provincial government to balance disaster preparedness spending between personnel training and the equipment and facilities needed by local responders.

The proposal came up during the 2027 budget deliberations after the Provincial Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Office (PDRRMO) presented its proposed P59-million allocation for training-related activities.

Members of the 17th Sangguniang Panlalawigan questioned whether part of the proposed funding could be redirected to address equipment and infrastructure gaps, particularly in municipalities with limited resources.

Third District Board Member Celestino Tining Martinez III asked PDRRMO head Dennis Pastor to identify the intended beneficiaries of the training programs and clarify whether they would cover disaster responders across the province.

Pastor said the activities would involve local disaster risk reduction and management offices (LDRRMOs) and their personnel. He said the proposed programs were based on the four thematic areas under a joint memorandum circular of the National Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Council (NDRRMC).

Martinez, however, questioned whether the entire allocation was necessary, noting that some cities and municipalities could finance their own training while the provincial government provided technical supervision and support.

He also raised the proposed funding for rainwater harvesting and water impounding projects, which initially received only P1 million, despite the province preparing for the anticipated effects of El Niño.

Martinez questioned the disparity between the amount proposed for training and the funding for small-scale water facilities that could provide communities with more direct benefits.

“If we're to put P59 million on trainings and then we're only providing P1 million for small harvest impounding facilities,” he said.

Board Member Lakamibini “Neneth” Reluya also suggested consolidating some allocations to support more permanent disaster management facilities, including the planned satellite offices in northern and southern Cebu.

She proposed equipping these facilities with adequate resources rather than distributing the budget among several training-related activities.

Board Member Andrei “Red” Duterte, meanwhile, called for equipment procurement based on the specific needs of individual municipalities, particularly fourth- and fifth-class local government units (LGUs) that may have limited funds to acquire their own supplies.

He said the provincial government could purchase the necessary equipment and provide training to ensure local responders could operate them properly.

Martinez clarified that he was not against training programs but wanted to ensure that personnel would have the resources to apply what they learned.

He cited the province's approximately 1,500 disaster response personnel and said capacity-building activities should go hand in hand with adequate operational support.

“We have more than enough money there for trainings. I would like to consider nga there would be something more,” he said.

Martinez said disaster preparedness spending should reflect the actual conditions and requirements of each municipality so the provincial government could prioritize communities with the greatest resource gaps.
