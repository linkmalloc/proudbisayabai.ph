Cebu is preparing for prolonged dry conditions in eight identified vulnerable local government units (LGUs), as the provincial government moves to secure hospital power supply, expand access to potable water and protect agricultural livelihoods ahead of the anticipated El Niño.

The eight LGUs identified as particularly exposed are Tuburan, Daanbantayan, Tabuelan, Bogo City, Catmon, Carmen, Borbon and Sogod.

At the fourth-quarter meeting of the Provincial Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Council (PDRRMC) on Wednesday, Nov. 18, Gov. Pamela Baricuatro raised the proposed installation of solar power systems in all 16 provincial hospitals to keep essential medical services running during possible electricity interruptions.

The council also approved the Provincial Engineering Office’s proposal to acquire P104 million worth of water-treatment equipment.

Baricuatro said the province must begin implementing its preparedness measures rather than limit its response to planning.

“We are in the implementation phase of our preparedness for El Niño. This is an urgent call to action that demands our focus and immediate mobilization. The climate threats facing our province can be very challenging, and we cannot afford to be reactive. We need to be responsive,” she said.

Under the proposed hospital solarization program, the province will prioritize critical areas, including emergency rooms, delivery rooms, hemodialysis units, central supply rooms, and nutrition and dietetics departments.

Baricuatro also called for the development of additional deep wells and improvements to existing water distribution systems, particularly in provincial hospitals that require a dependable supply for patients and daily operations.

Separately, the Provincial Engineering Office recommended purchasing 28 multimedia filtration systems and eight seawater reverse osmosis units at a combined cost of P104 million.

The equipment is intended to treat water from sources such as springs and seawater to produce potable water for communities facing shortages.

The PDRRMC also endorsed the establishment of hydration stations in different parts of Cebu. Water tankers will replenish the stations to provide residents with drinking water during extreme heat and periods of limited supply.

Agriculture has already sustained an estimated P20 million in damage as of October 2026 across eight LGUs: Danao City, San Remigio, Ronda, Carmen, Toledo City, Moalboal, Catmon and Barili.

To help farmers and fisherfolk manage possible losses, the Capitol is encouraging them to enroll in the Agricultural and Fisheries Insurance Program, with the provincial government shouldering the insurance premiums.

The council also endorsed a recommendation to declare a provincial state of calamity once at least three LGUs have declared their own states of calamity.

Health preparations formed another part of the council’s discussions after the Philippine Atmospheric, Geophysical and Astronomical Services Administration (Pagasa) warned that the heat index could reach 49 to 52 degrees Celsius during peak hours, particularly between 10 a.m. and 3 p.m.

A Pagasa Visayas official also warned that the approaching El Niño could become the strongest on record.

In response, the Provincial Health Office presented measures advising residents to limit continuous exposure to direct sunlight to no more than 20 minutes during the hottest periods.

Baricuatro instructed provincial offices to accelerate procurement and project implementation to ensure the planned interventions are in place before the anticipated climate impacts intensify.
