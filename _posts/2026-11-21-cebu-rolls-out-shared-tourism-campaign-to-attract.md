Cebu's tourism industry is looking beyond its established beach destinations to attract more visitors, with business and government stakeholders adopting a unified campaign to promote the island's food, heritage, mountain attractions and other experiences.

The initiative, called “Cebu: Endless Surprises,” seeks to strengthen the province's appeal to domestic and international travelers while encouraging longer stays and repeat visits by presenting Cebu as a destination with more to offer than its coastal attractions.

The Cebu Chamber of Commerce and Industry (CCCI) introduced the campaign during its Thanksgiving Night on Oct. 27, bringing together private sector groups, tourism operators and government stakeholders behind a common destination brand.

The campaign was developed through consultations and market research involving 93 tourism stakeholders, four focus group discussions and a survey of 2,005 respondents from Cebu's key visitor markets, including South Korea, Japan, Australia and the Philippines.

The research found that Cebu already has strong destination awareness but needs to communicate its distinctive offerings more effectively, particularly to international travelers.

It also identified the variety of experiences available within the island as a key tourism advantage, including beaches, mountains, food, cultural heritage, outdoor activities and local communities.

CCCI President Regan Rex King said a coordinated destination campaign could help distribute tourism's economic benefits beyond hotels and resorts to other businesses and communities.

“Tourism creates opportunities beyond hotels and resorts. More visitors to Cebu also mean more opportunities for our restaurants, retailers, transport providers, entrepreneurs, and local communities,” King said.

He said the initiative must translate destination promotion into business opportunities and measurable economic gains for local enterprises.

“By working together behind one destination brand, we can make Cebu more competitive and ensure that tourism growth benefits more Cebuano businesses and communities,” he added.

The Department of Tourism (DOT) in Central Visayas also recognized the role of private sector collaboration in strengthening Cebu's position in the tourism market.

DOT 7 Director Gelena Asis-Dimpas said coordinated efforts among tourism stakeholders could improve destination visibility, create opportunities for host communities and strengthen the sector's competitiveness.

The campaign followed stakeholder discussions in July, when participants reviewed the research findings and agreed on a common direction for Cebu's destination branding.

The next phase will include a market research and data-sharing session in December, followed by the release of a destination brand book and training for tourism frontliners.

Businesses, tourism operators, local government units and other stakeholders are being encouraged to incorporate the campaign into their marketing and tourism development plans for 2027.

Through the unified branding effort, stakeholders aim to build on Cebu's existing market recognition, encourage visitors to explore more of the island and broaden tourism-related economic opportunities for local enterprises and communities.
