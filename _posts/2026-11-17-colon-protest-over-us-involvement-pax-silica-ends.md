A protest against US involvement in the Philippines and the proposed Pax Silica initiative along Colon Street in Cebu City ended in a confrontation between police and demonstrators on Monday, prompting allegations of excessive force and raising concerns over the safety of journalists covering the assembly.

The Cebu City Police Office (CCPO) said the confrontation followed the burning of an effigy despite an earlier agreement with organizers not to proceed with the activity. Protest organizers and human rights advocates, however, accused police of violently dispersing the demonstrators.

Police Lt. Col. Gerard Ace Pelare said authorities allowed the rally to continue from around 9 a.m. to noon despite the organizers' lack of a permit, citing maximum tolerance and the need to protect public safety.

"Despite nga wala sila'y permit, we made sure nga we practiced maximum tolerance," Pelare said.

He explained that public assemblies outside designated freedom parks require permits and that Colon Street is not among the designated areas.

Pelare said police personnel were initially deployed to manage traffic, prevent road accidents and maintain peace and order along the busy downtown street.

He said the situation escalated when demonstrators burned an effigy, which police had asked them not to do because of overhead electrical wires and the risk of fire.

According to Pelare, the station commander appealed to the protesters to stop the burning, but some allegedly became aggressive when disaster response personnel tried to extinguish the fire.

He added that demonstrators took hold of a fire extinguisher brought by personnel of the Cebu City Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Office, triggering a commotion.

The CCPO reported that two police personnel sustained minor injuries. It said it was investigating the alleged involvement of demonstrators in the effigy burning, the injuries and the disruption of public order, and would file charges against those found to have violated the law.

Kilusang Magbubukid ng Pilipinas (KMP) Cebu disputed the police account, accusing authorities of using excessive force against participants in the multisectoral protest.

In a statement, the group reported multiple injuries among demonstrators and around five attempted arrests, including those involving peasant advocates.

The National Union of Journalists of the Philippines (NUJP) Cebu also condemned what it described as "police violence against media workers" covering the demonstration.

The group reported that one journalist was hit by a police shield, while another inhaled a thick cloud of fire extinguisher powder discharged by police and feared for his health.

Neither journalist sustained severe injuries, according to NUJP Cebu. Both requested anonymity.

The organization said student journalists were also covering the protest and stressed that media workers must be allowed to document public demonstrations without being exposed to police violence.

It called on the CCPO and Police Regional Office 7 to investigate the incidents, hold responsible officers accountable and ensure the safety of journalists covering protests.

The University of the Philippines Cebu Committee for the Promotion and Protection of Academic Freedom and Human Rights (CAFHR) also condemned the dispersal and called for an "impartial investigation and accountability" for any abuses committed.

The committee reminded police of their obligation under Section 3(c) of Batas Pambansa Blg. 880 to exercise the highest degree of restraint during public assemblies and their dispersal.

Citing the Supreme Court ruling in Bayan v. Ermita, CAFHR said the principle of maximum tolerance protects the rights of people participating in public assemblies.

"Peaceful dissent is not a crime," the committee said, warning against using public order as a pretext to suppress legitimate grievances.

Progressive groups organized the demonstration to oppose US military involvement in the Philippines and the proposed Pax Silica initiative, which they claimed could threaten farming communities, land rights and local livelihoods.

Bagong Alyansang Makabayan-Central Visayas also urged Cebuanos to oppose the initiative, describing it as "a threat to the country's natural resources and communities."

The CCPO maintained that it respects the constitutional rights to free expression and peaceful assembly but said police would continue enforcing laws governing public demonstrations.

As of writing, the media was still seeking a statement from Cebu City Mayor Nestor Archival on the confrontation.
