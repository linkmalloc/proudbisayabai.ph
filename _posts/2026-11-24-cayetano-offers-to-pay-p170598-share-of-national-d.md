Senate Minority Floor Leader Alan Peter Cayetano has expressed willingness to personally contribute P170,598 toward paying the Philippines' national debt, proposing a public donation drive if the government establishes a mechanism to accept such contributions.

Cayetano raised the idea during the opening day of the Senate plenary debates on the proposed P7.2-trillion national budget for 2027, citing the national government's P19.66-trillion debt as of the third quarter of 2026.

He said the debt translates to an estimated P170,598.03 for every Filipino.

Addressing the Marcos administration's economic team, including officials from the Department of Budget and Management, Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas, Department of Economy, Planning and Development, and Department of Finance, Cayetano asked how citizens could voluntarily pay their respective shares.

“Where can I go if want to pay my share?” Cayetano asked.

Senate Committee on Finance Chairperson JV Ejercito, after consulting with the economic team, said citizens could contribute by paying the correct taxes.

Cayetano disagreed, saying he already pays his taxes and wants to make an additional contribution.

“That’s not true,” Cayetano remarked. “Because I’m already paying the right taxes. I want to go beyond nga eh and I want to pay my 170,000 hindi niyo tatanggapin?”

Ejercito replied that the government currently has no mechanism for accepting such donations.

Cayetano asked the economic team to inform him if the proposal could be considered and once a system for accepting contributions is established.

“I’ll bring myself and try to bring a few people to pay our 170,000. Hopefully create a movement of people who want us to be debt free,” he said.

However, Cayetano said any public contribution drive must be accompanied by safeguards to ensure that government spending is proper and foreign loans are managed responsibly.

He said public confidence in the government remains a concern, making transparency and accountability necessary before people are encouraged to contribute beyond their tax obligations.

Cayetano cited South Korea as an example, recalling how its citizens contributed jewelry to help the government address its debt problems.

He said the effort showed how people could make sacrifices beyond paying taxes when they believed in helping their country recover financially.
