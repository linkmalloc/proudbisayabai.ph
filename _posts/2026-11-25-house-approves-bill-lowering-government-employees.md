Government employees could become eligible for optional retirement at 56 instead of 60 under a bill approved by the House of Representatives on Tuesday.

House Bill 10995 received 209 affirmative votes, no objections and one abstention during plenary voting.

Upon the instruction of Speaker Faustino “Bojie” Dy, Majority Leader Ferdinand Alexander “Sandro” Marcos prioritized the measure for approval.

The bill seeks to amend Section 13-A of Republic Act 8291, or the Government Service Insurance Act of 1997, which sets the conditions for government employees to qualify for retirement benefits.

Under the proposed amendment, a Government Service Insurance System (GSIS) member must be at least 56 years old, have completed at least 15 years of government service and not be receiving a monthly pension for permanent total disability to qualify for optional retirement.

Qualified retirees would receive their benefits from the GSIS.

The measure would give eligible personnel among the more than 1 million workers in the government bureaucracy the option to leave public service earlier and spend more time with their families or pursue other interests and careers.

Its proponents also said the measure could open opportunities for younger government employees to qualify for permanent or higher positions.

The proposed retirement age would align the bureaucracy's optional retirement policy more closely with the retirement ages of uniformed personnel in the military and the Philippine National Police.

Military personnel have a retirement age of 57, while uniformed PNP personnel retire at 56.
