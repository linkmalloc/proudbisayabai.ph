More than 500 houses and other structures may be affected by Cebu City's renewed campaign to reclaim three-meter easements along eight rivers and waterways, as officials assess properties that could obstruct flood-control and maintenance work.

The figure remains preliminary, with river troopers continuing to update the inventory and inspect areas where structures were previously removed but later rebuilt.

Kenneth Siasar, chief of staff to Mayor Nestor Archival, said during a press conference on Monday, Nov. 16, that the city had started clearing areas where assessments and documentation were already completed.

Barangay Ermita is among the first areas covered by the operation. Twenty-three households there voluntarily dismantled their riverside structures and received P20,000 each in financial assistance.

The city is also negotiating with 12 more structure owners in the same area, farther downstream toward the sea, to secure their voluntary removal.

“In less than a month, we were able to remove 23 houses. And in less than a month, we were able to give them financial assistance,” Siasar said.

He said the P20,000 assistance follows the Department of Social Welfare and Services' criteria for totally damaged houses, which sets the amount as the maximum assistance. The grant is not based on the size or construction cost of an individual house.

The city also helped affected families transfer reusable wood and other materials from their dismantled homes, Siasar said.

Relocation is not automatic for all affected households. Some families have other homes or places to stay, while others have been enrolled with the Division for the Welfare of the Urban Poor for possible inclusion in homeowners' associations and future city relocation programs.

Beyond removing riverside structures, the city is also addressing drainage obstructions in other areas to improve water flow.

Near T. Padilla Market, workers have opened a drainage connection leading to the river. The city is coordinating with the Department of Public Works and Highways to address a sheet pile obstructing the flow of water from the market area toward the waterway.

In the Manalili area, the city is preparing a joint cleanup and assessment with nearby business owners during the first week of December. The inspection will cover the rear portions of establishments near Colonnade, 138 Mall, Dimsum and Gaisano Main.

Siasar said business owners who attended a meeting with city officials had committed to cooperating with the cleanup.

However, structures extending across the creek, including access ways used by vehicles to transport goods, have limited the entry of heavy equipment such as backhoes.

The planned assessment will identify which structures need to be removed and determine whether desilting can proceed without affecting nearby building foundations.

Siasar said restricted access to waterways was one of the reasons for renewing the easement campaign. City engineering personnel have reported difficulty bringing equipment close to riverbanks because of structures built along or across them.

“If these areas are not cleared, we will not be able to mitigate flooding in the lowlands, even when there is only light rain, because our equipment cannot get in,” he said.

He added that previous clearing initiatives, including the Gubat sa Baha campaign, were not sustained in some areas, where structures returned after removal. The city is therefore updating its records and reassessing affected communities.

Siasar said officials would first seek voluntary cooperation from residents. Those willing to remove their structures should inform the city early so personnel can process their documents and determine their eligibility for assistance.

“If they will cooperate with us voluntarily, without resistance, whatever financial assistance the Cebu City Government can provide can be given to them,” he said.

If negotiations fail, the city may eventually remove the structures, but Siasar said officials must follow the proper procedures.

The city has yet to complete its assessment and establish the final number of affected structures and households along the eight waterways.

Siasar said officials prioritized Ermita because its documentation was already complete, while inspections and coordination with barangays continue in other areas.
