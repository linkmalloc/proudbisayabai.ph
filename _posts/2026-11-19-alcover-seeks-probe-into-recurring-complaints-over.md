Cebu City Councilor Jun Alcover Jr. is seeking a review of the government's Libreng Sakay program after commuters continued to report being left behind by modernized public utility jeepneys operating along the 01K route.

During his privileged speech on Tuesday, Nov. 17, Alcover urged the City Council to examine recurring complaints involving participating vehicles that allegedly refused to pick up passengers traveling between V. Urgello in Cebu City and Parkmall in Mandaue City.

He said similar complaints had surfaced in 2025 and again in February 2026, when the Department of Transportation (DOTr) announced an investigation into reports of missed trips and insufficient vehicle deployment.

The latest incident involved a viral video that allegedly showed commuters waiting at a designated loading area in Barangay Mabolo while participating jeepneys passed without stopping.

The Land Transportation Franchising and Regulatory Board Central Visayas (LTFRB 7) had earlier issued show-cause orders dated Oct. 31 against JP and Sons Transport Group Inc. and Aleja Fua Rent A Car Corp., directing the two operators to explain the reported incidents.

For Alcover, the recurrence of similar complaints raises questions about whether the program's implementation, monitoring and enforcement mechanisms are working as intended.

"If substantially similar complaints continue to surface for more than a year, then we must determine whether these are merely isolated incidents or indications of deeper problems in the program's implementation, monitoring, and enforcement," he said.

He also emphasized the need to account for the public funds supporting the service, noting that participating transport operators receive government subsidies in exchange for providing free rides.

Launched in Cebu in July 2025, the program initially deployed around 100 modernized jeepneys along the 01K route. It provides free rides during weekday morning hours from 6 a.m. to 9 a.m. and evening hours from 5 p.m. to 8 p.m.

The initiative seeks to reduce transportation expenses for workers, students and other commuters.

Alcover said the government must ensure that the service funded by public money reaches its intended beneficiaries.

"Public funds must translate into public service," he said.

He asked the City Council, through the appropriate committee and in coordination with the Committee on Transportation, to review the program's implementation beginning with its launch in July 2025.

Among the documents he wants examined are the list of participating transport operators and accredited vehicles, deployment schedules, GPS records and completed trip reports.

He also wants the council to review the program's total allocation, funds released and payments made to participating operators to determine how government subsidies have been used.

Alcover further sought copies of the LTFRB 7 show-cause orders, updates on the Nov. 12 hearing involving the two transport operators, and records of complaints and enforcement actions since the program began.

As part of his motion, he proposed holding an executive session with representatives from the DOTr, LTFRB 7, Land Transportation Office, Cebu City Transportation Office and the two transport operators.

The proposed meeting would give the agencies and operators an opportunity to explain the reported service problems and provide records that could help the council assess the program's implementation.

Alcover said the discussion should help identify measures to improve monitoring and ensure participating vehicles fulfill their obligation to serve commuters.

He stressed that passengers were not asking for special treatment but for the transportation service that the government had promised to provide.

"Ang Libreng Sakay kinahanglan Libreng Sakay gyud. Dili libreng pahibalo, dili libreng pangako, ug dili libreng pasagdan ang mga pasahero," he said.
