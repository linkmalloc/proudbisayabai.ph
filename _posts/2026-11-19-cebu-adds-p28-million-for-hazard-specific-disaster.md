Cebu’s proposed disaster risk reduction and management (DRRM) budget for 2027 has increased to P440 million after provincial officials approved an additional P40 million, including funding for preparations against specific hazards.

The Cebu Provincial Development Council (PDC) approved the adjustment during its fourth-quarter meeting on Nov. 16, raising the proposed allocation from the original P400 million.

Provincial Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Office (PDRRMO) chief Dennis Francis Pastor said the additional funding was needed after a review of the original DRRM Annual Investment Plan (AIP) identified requirements that had not been included.

Of the additional amount, P28 million will fund Disaster Preparedness – Anticipatory Action for Hazard-Specific Events, a component absent from the initial investment plan.

Another P12 million will increase the Quick Response Fund (QRF) from P50 million to P62 million.

Pastor said the adjustment was intended to meet the legally mandated 30-percent allocation for the QRF under the DRRM fund.

The Provincial Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Council initially approved the P400-million AIP in September. The PDC endorsed the plan in October before the proposed adjustments were presented.

Governor Pamela Baricuatro, who chairs the PDC, presided over the Nov. 16 meeting attended by fourth district Rep. Sun Shimura, Cebu mayors led by Cordova Mayor Cesar Suan, national government agency representatives and civil society organizations.

The council also endorsed proposed programs, activities and projects of national government agencies and state universities in Cebu for fiscal year 2028.

Among the agencies that presented their proposals were the Philippine National Police, Bureau of Fire Protection, Department of Environment and Natural Resources, Department of Agriculture, Department of Education, Department of Health and Department of Public Works and Highways, along with other government offices and state universities.

The proposed projects still require PDC endorsement before they can be forwarded to the Regional Development Council, which is scheduled to meet in December.

Baricuatro urged national agencies to submit projects that are ready for implementation, saying government preparedness should also be reflected in planning and spending.

She said the value of public investments should be assessed based on how well projects are implemented and how they improve residents' lives, rather than the number of approved projects or the size of their budgets.

"Development is not measured simply by the number of projects we approve, or the budgets we allocate. It is measured by the quality of implementation and, more importantly, by the improvements these investment bring to the lives of the people of Cebu,” Baricuatro said.
