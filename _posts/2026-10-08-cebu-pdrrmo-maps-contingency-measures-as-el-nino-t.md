Cebu is preparing contingency measures for possible prolonged dry conditions, with the Provincial Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Office (PDRRMO) tasked to map communities that could face water shortages, drought, heat and fires.

  

The measures are part of the Cebu El Niño Preparedness and Mitigation Plan 2026-2027, which sets out actions for provincial offices, local governments and partner agencies as the province prepares for the potential effects of El Niño.

  

The provincial government has earmarked P200 million to support departments implementing measures under the plan. Priority spending includes water tankers, storage facilities, rainwater-harvesting systems, water-system rehabilitation, drought-resistant agricultural interventions, heat-health supplies and emergency response resources.

  

The plan also lists portable water-treatment machines, water-quality testing kits, water-treatment tablets, solar power systems, backup generators, medical supplies, oral rehydration products, livestock feed and emergency food stocks as priority resources, subject to existing inventories, technical assessments and available funds.

  

The PDRRMO will establish an El Niño Monitoring and Coordination Desk at its command center to track climate information from the Philippine Atmospheric, Geophysical and Astronomical Services Administration (PAGASA), including rainfall anomalies, drought advisories and heat-index forecasts.

  

It will also develop a risk and vulnerability map covering drought-prone areas, water-stressed communities, agricultural hotspots and fire-prone locations.

  

Water security is among the immediate concerns identified in the plan.

  

The Provincial General Services Office will identify barangays with inadequate water supply and determine priority areas for augmentation. It will also prepare a contingency plan that may include water-rationing arrangements.

  

The province plans to preposition water tankers, mobile storage tanks and portable water pumps and establish distribution schedules for vulnerable and geographically isolated communities.

  

The Provincial Engineering Office will assess springs, wells, reservoirs and other existing water sources and prepare proposals for emergency deep wells, communal tanks, retention ponds, reservoirs and other facilities that could increase available supply.

  

The plan also calls for an assessment of the province’s potential for rainwater collection and the rehabilitation of existing water-storage facilities.

  

Agriculture and livestock operations will also receive attention as dry conditions could affect production.

  

Provincial agriculture and veterinary offices will identify drought-vulnerable production areas and promote drought-tolerant crops, efficient irrigation, soil-moisture conservation and appropriate planting schedules.

  

The province will prepare possible assistance for affected farmers, including seeds, animal feed and other agricultural inputs. Fisheries, aquaculture and inland water resources will also be monitored as water levels and temperatures change.

  

The health sector will prepare for possible increases in heat-related illnesses, dehydration, heat exhaustion and heatstroke.

  

The Provincial Health Office will coordinate with municipal health offices and the Department of Health on disease surveillance, health advisories and emergency response. Provincial hospitals and other health facilities are also expected to maintain adequate drinking water and water-catchment facilities.

  

The provincial social welfare sector will maintain contingency supplies of food, bottled water and Meals Ready to Eat and prepare arrangements for emergency water distribution in affected communities.

  

The plan extends beyond water and agriculture, covering food production, public health, energy, infrastructure, tourism, education and disaster response.

  

It identifies prolonged dry spells and drought as potential risks to Cebu because of the province’s reliance on surface and groundwater sources, agricultural livelihoods and intermunicipal water systems.

  

The provincial government will use an “anticipatory action” approach, requiring preventive measures before the effects of El Niño reach critical levels.

  

It also adopts a “whole-of-government approach” involving provincial, city, municipal and barangay governments, national agencies and private partners.

  

During heightened alert or emergency operations, priority local governments will be required to report critical water shortages, heat-related incidents, fires and other significant impacts daily.
