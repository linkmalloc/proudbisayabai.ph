As Mandaue City continues recovery efforts following Typhoon Tino, the International Medical Corps (IMC) has donated 17 generator sets to help strengthen emergency preparedness and ensure the continuity of health services during power interruptions.

Mayor Thadeo Jovito “Jonkie” Ouano said the donation was extended even without a formal request from the city government, underscoring the sustained support from organizations and fellow local government units despite the typhoon having already passed.

“Pasalamat kaayo ta. Without even asking, daghan kaayong organizations ug LGUs nga nipadayon og tabang nato bisan nilabay na ang bagyo. Dako kaayo among pasalamat sa ilang generosity,” Ouano said.

Of the total units donated, 15 generator sets will be distributed to selected barangays, primarily for use in health centers, while two units will be retained by the Mandaue City Health Office.

City Health Officer Dr. Debra Catulong requested that the two units be assigned to the health office to ensure the proper storage of vaccines and the continued operation of laboratory services during power outages.

In addition to the IMC donation, the city government has also received financial assistance from other local government units. 

Ouano said the Bogo City government donated P500,000, while the Municipality of Daanbantayan contributed P100,000.

“Wala gyud ta nag-expect ani kay recovering pa baya sila. Bilib gyud ko sa mga Cebuano—tinabangay ug sinabtanay lang gyud,” the mayor added.

IMC representatives Ingrid Benigay, Esbie Imperial, and Joel Sevilla personally turned over the generator sets to the city, together with suppliers Glaiza Punis and Lurimarc Alin.

The donation is expected to enhance the city’s capacity to respond to emergencies and disasters, particularly in maintaining essential health services during sudden power disruptions.

Also present during the turnover were Dr. Catulong, Executive Secretary Atty. Riczen Gingoyon, and personnel from the Mandaue City Health Office. (VCL)
