Mandaue City has recorded two dengue-related deaths this November, prompting the city health office to strengthen disease surveillance and conduct inspections in communities where infections have been reported.

The fatalities involved residents of Barangays Tabok and Umapad, according to Dr. Debra Catulong, head of the Mandaue City Health Office (MCHO).

Catulong confirmed that both patients were brought to hospitals before their cases were reported and classified as dengue-related deaths.

“Naabot gyud to sila sa hospital. Reported gyud siya as dengue deaths,” she said.

She also acknowledged that the city has recorded more dengue cases this year than in 2025, although the health office did not disclose its latest case count.

“Mas taas ang kaso nato this year, so mas naninguha sad gyud ta nga malikayan nga naay makalas tungod sa dengue,” Catulong said.

Mandaue recorded 520 dengue cases and three deaths for the entire year of 2025.

Following reports of infections or fatalities, the MCHO conducts surveillance investigations to assess the circumstances surrounding each case and identify possible sources of transmission.

Health personnel visit affected households to establish how long patients experienced symptoms, determine possible reasons for delayed medical consultation and inspect nearby areas for mosquito breeding sites.

The team also provides families and neighboring residents with information on dengue prevention and measures to reduce the risk of further infections.

“Ma-kaso or death ba na siya, amo gyud na siyang adtuon. Automatic gyud dayon once reported sa system, adtuon na dayon na,” Catulong said.

She clarified that the investigations cover both reported dengue cases and deaths, allowing the health office to monitor infections and assess potential risks within affected communities.

To prevent further transmission, Catulong urged residents to observe the Department of Health's (DOH) 4S strategy, which calls for search and destroy, seek early consultation, self-protection measures, and support for fogging or spraying in identified hotspot areas.

She identified the removal of stagnant water and other possible mosquito breeding sites as a primary preventive measure, explaining that eliminating these areas helps prevent mosquitoes from multiplying.

“Search and destroy mao gyud na ang pinakaimportante kay kung walay breeding sites, walay mabuhi nga mosquitoes,” she said.

Catulong also encouraged residents who develop fever to seek medical attention early, particularly within the first two days of symptoms. Early consultation allows health professionals to assess patients and determine whether they need laboratory examinations, medication or additional fluids.

For individual protection, she recommended using mosquito repellents approved by the Food and Drug Administration and wearing clothes that cover exposed skin.

Fogging and spraying, meanwhile, should be limited to areas identified as dengue hotspots, particularly during outbreaks.

To help patients receive prompt attention, the MCHO outpatient department and Mandaue City Hospital have established dedicated dengue fast lanes.

Patients experiencing fever or reporting a concerning history of fever receive priority during consultations. The facilities also provide laboratory examinations and medicines free of charge.

Beyond medical services and surveillance, the health office continues to conduct community lectures and household visits to promote awareness and encourage residents to take preventive measures.

Barangay Dengue Brigades also remain active across Mandaue's 27 barangays, carrying out community-based prevention plans and organizing cleanup drives to remove stagnant water and accumulated garbage.

Catulong said the brigades help mobilize residents to maintain clean surroundings, particularly by addressing unattended water containers and waste that could serve as mosquito breeding sites.
