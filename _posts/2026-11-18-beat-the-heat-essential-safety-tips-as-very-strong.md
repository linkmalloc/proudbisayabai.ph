The public is urged to take extra precautions amid extreme heat, as prolonged exposure to high temperatures can increase the risk of heat-related illnesses, including dehydration, heat exhaustion, and potentially life-threatening heatstroke.

The warning comes as the country experiences a very strong El Niño, which is expected to bring warmer-than-average temperatures and below-normal rainfall across several parts of the Philippines.

### **Understanding the impact of El Niño**

According to the Philippine Atmospheric, Geophysical and Astronomical Services Administration (PAGASA), a Very Strong El Niño is currently present in the tropical Pacific and is expected to persist until the first half of 2027, potentially reaching its peak by the end of 2026.

El Niño is a natural climate phenomenon characterized by warmer-than-usual sea surface temperatures in the central and eastern tropical Pacific Ocean. It influences weather patterns and increases the likelihood of below-normal rainfall in the Philippines, potentially resulting in prolonged dry spells and drought in affected areas.

PAGASA also warned that warmer-than-average air temperatures are expected across most parts of the country. Reduced rainfall may further contribute to dry conditions and increased pressure on water resources.

While El Niño increases the likelihood of hotter and drier conditions, it does not mean that rainfall will completely stop. Tropical cyclones and other rain-bearing weather systems may still bring episodes of heavy rainfall.

With these conditions, the public is encouraged to remain prepared, conserve water, and take preventive measures against heat-related illnesses.

### **How to protect yourself from extreme heat**

The Department of Health (DOH) reminds the public to stay hydrated, avoid unnecessary exposure to direct sunlight, and seek shade or remain in well-ventilated or air-conditioned spaces whenever possible.

Other preventive measures include:

*   Limit outdoor activities. Avoid unnecessary exposure to direct sunlight, particularly during the hottest hours of the day.

*   Stay hydrated. Drink sufficient amounts of water throughout the day, even when you are not thirsty.

*   Wear appropriate clothing. Choose lightweight, loose-fitting, and light-colored clothing to help keep the body cool.

*   Use sun protection. Bring an umbrella, wear a wide-brimmed hat, or use other protective coverings when going outdoors.

*   Schedule physical activities wisely. Exercise or perform strenuous outdoor work during cooler hours, preferably early in the morning or later in the afternoon.

*   Take regular breaks. Rest in shaded, cool, or well-ventilated areas, especially when working outdoors.

*   Avoid excessive dehydrating beverages. Limit alcohol and excessive consumption of caffeinated or sugary drinks.

The public is also encouraged to monitor PAGASA’s heat index forecasts and local weather advisories to determine the level of heat exposure in their respective areas.

Recognize the signs of heat-related illnesses

Heat-related illnesses can range from mild dehydration to severe heatstroke. Recognizing their early warning signs is important to prevent complications.

Common symptoms include:

*   Excessive sweating

*   Dizziness or lightheadedness

*   Headaches

*   Nausea or vomiting

*   Muscle cramps

*   General weakness or unusual fatigue

*   Rapid heartbeat

Individuals experiencing these symptoms should immediately stop their activities, move to a shaded or cooler location, rest, and drink water if they are fully conscious and able to swallow.

### **Heatstroke is a medical emergency**

Heatstroke is the most severe form of heat-related illness. It occurs when the body’s temperature regulation system fails, causing body temperature to rise to dangerous levels.

Warning signs may include extremely high body temperature, confusion, altered mental state, seizures, and loss of consciousness.

Anyone suspected of experiencing heatstroke requires immediate medical attention. While waiting for emergency assistance, move the affected individual to a cooler area, remove unnecessary outer clothing, and begin cooling the body using cool water, wet cloths, fans, or ice packs placed around the neck, armpits, and groin.

Do not give food or drinks to someone who is confused, unconscious, or unable to swallow safely.

### **Protect children, older adults, and vulnerable individuals**

Children, older adults, pregnant women, and people with existing medical conditions may be more vulnerable to the effects of extreme heat.

Individuals who work outdoors, including construction workers, delivery personnel, street vendors, and agricultural workers, should also take additional precautions because of their prolonged exposure to high temperatures.

Families and communities are encouraged to check on vulnerable individuals, ensure they have access to drinking water and cool spaces, and watch for signs of heat-related illnesses.

Never leave children, older adults, or pets inside parked vehicles, as temperatures inside can rise rapidly even when windows are slightly open.

TAKE ACTION: STAY COOL AND PROTECT YOUR COMMUNITY

As the country faces the effects of Very Strong El Niño, the public is encouraged to make heat safety and water conservation daily priorities.

Drink water regularly, limit unnecessary outdoor activities during peak heat hours, take frequent breaks, and check on family members and neighbors who may need assistance.

Conserve water by avoiding unnecessary consumption, fixing leaks, and using available water resources responsibly.

If you experience dizziness, nausea, severe weakness, or other warning signs, stop your activity and move to a cooler place. Do not ignore symptoms or delay seeking medical attention.

Stay informed through official DOH and PAGASA advisories. Prevention, early recognition, and community preparedness remain essential in protecting lives during prolonged periods of extreme heat and dry weather.
