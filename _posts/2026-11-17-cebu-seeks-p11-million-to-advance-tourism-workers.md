Cebu is planning to move its tourism workforce beyond basic instruction, with the provincial government proposing an P11-million training budget next year for specialized courses, including water rescue and advanced adventure tourism skills.

The proposed allocation of the Cebu Provincial Tourism Office (PTO) for 2027 is P4 million higher than its P7-million budget this year.

During the provincial government's budget deliberations, PTO head Rowena Montecillo said the increase would support a ladderized training program that allows tourism frontliners to build on their existing skills and qualify for more advanced instruction.

The courses would target community-based tourism workers, including mountain, cave, waterfall, trail and canyoneering guides. The office also plans to offer water rescue training for personnel assigned to destinations where adventure activities carry safety risks.

Board Member Celestino “Tining” Martinez III questioned the proposed increase, noting that the PTO had already conducted 31 training programs this year using its P7-million allocation.

“We already spent P7 million this year, and then wa nato matiwas and then we're asking for P11 million for trainings. So are these going to be the same people that we're going to train for next year?” Martinez asked.

Montecillo said the proposed courses would build on previous training rather than repeat the same lessons, with participants expected to develop higher-level competencies.

She said local government units had identified many of the intended beneficiaries, some of whom had already undergone training and accreditation programs conducted by the Department of Tourism.

The deliberations also raised the possibility that repeated training could produce more guides than established tourism destinations need.

Board Member Red Andrei Duterte, chairman of the Committee on Tourism, backed the proposed increase, saying the province should provide training beyond basic competencies.

He also pointed to the need to expand training opportunities in northern Cebu, noting that previous programs had largely focused on destinations in the south.

Aside from workforce development, the PTO is seeking additional funding for tourism research, promotion and planning.

One of its priorities is to complete Cebu's 10-year Provincial Tourism Master Plan. The office has begun initial consultations with academic partners, including Cebu Normal University.

It also plans to improve municipal tourism officers' ability to interpret tourism data and strengthen promotional activities through its two information counters at the Mactan-Cebu International Airport.
