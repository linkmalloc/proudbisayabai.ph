The Philippine Embassy in Beijing has reminded Filipinos in China and those planning to travel there to comply with stricter requirements on visas, travel documents and declared activities when new Chinese exit and entry regulations take effect on Sept. 15.

In an advisory posted Monday, the embassy said the new rules stem from State Council Decree No. 841, which reinforces existing requirements on the authenticity of documents and the lawful purpose of a foreign national’s stay in China.

The embassy particularly urged Filipino travelers to ensure that their visa category corresponds with the activities they actually intend to undertake in China.

“The visa category you applied for must reflect your actual activities in China,” the embassy said.

Chinese authorities may question visa applicants and request supporting documents, including electronic records, to verify whether their declared purpose of travel is genuine and lawful, the embassy said.

Filipinos were advised to provide accurate information and cooperate with authorities when asked to submit additional documents or undergo verification.

The embassy also warned against submitting fake or inaccurate documents, saying counterfeit materials or deliberate misrepresentations in visa applications could result in rejection and an entry ban, either during the visa application process or at the border.

Those who issue invitation letters for visa applicants may likewise face penalties or fines if the information they provide is false.

“Signing an invitation letter for someone else’s visa application makes you legally responsible for its accuracy,” the embassy said.

Filipinos seeking assistance from visa or immigration agencies were also advised to verify whether the agencies are properly registered with Chinese immigration authorities.

The embassy clarified that the new regulations do not prohibit legitimate travel or make ordinary visa applications more difficult for travelers who comply with the requirements.

For questions about Chinese immigration requirements, Filipinos may contact the National Immigration Administration through its 12367 hotline or check its official website.

Those requiring consular assistance may also contact the Philippine Embassy in Beijing or the nearest Philippine Consulate General in China.
