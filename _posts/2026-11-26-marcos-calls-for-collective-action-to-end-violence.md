President Ferdinand Marcos Jr. called on Filipinos to speak out against abuse and help protect women from violence as the annual 18-Day Campaign to End Violence Against Women opened on Wednesday, November 25.

  

In his message for the campaign, Marcos reiterated his administration's zero-tolerance stance on violence against women, stressing that abuse has no place in homes, workplaces, schools, communities or online spaces.

  

“Ikinalulugod kong makiisa sa sambayanang Pilipino sa paglulunsad ng 18-Day Campaign to End Violence Against Women sa pangunguna ng Komisyon sa Kababaihan ng Pilipinas,” he said.

  

“Walang puwang sa ating bansa ang anumang uri ng karahasan laban sa kababaihan — maging sa mga lansangan, paaralan, trabaho, bahay, cyberspace, o kahit saan pa mang lugar,” Marcos added.

  

He urged the public to report acts of violence and oppose abuse, saying collective action is necessary to build a society founded on respect, justice and inclusion.

  

The President also cited the administration's continuing implementation of laws and programs to protect women's rights, including the National Action Plan on Ending Violence Against Women 2025-2030.

  

“This campaign is a call to continue to cherish mutual respect and promote human interaction. I encourage everyone to be part of our campaign to prevent, oppose, and expose all acts that defile and devalue the dignity of any citizen,” Marcos said.

  

He further called on Filipinos to remain vigilant against oppression and help teach younger generations to respect women.

  

Marcos said every member of society has a responsibility to create peaceful, inclusive and caring communities where women can live without fear of violence and abuse.

  

“Tiyakin nating ang bawat isa ay makapamumuhay nang payapa, may kalayaang maihayag ang kanilang sarili, at mayroong makakapitan at kasangga laban sa mga pang-aabuso at ibang pang krimen,” he said.

  

The 18-Day Campaign to End Violence Against Women runs annually from November 25 to December 12, seeking to raise public awareness and mobilize action against gender-based violence.
