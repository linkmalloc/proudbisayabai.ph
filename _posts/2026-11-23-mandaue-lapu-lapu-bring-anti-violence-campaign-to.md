Mandaue and Lapu-Lapu cities are seeking broader community participation in preventing violence against women, using the 18-Day Campaign to End Violence Against Women and Children to encourage residents to help protect survivors and make homes safer.

  

In Mandaue, the Zonta Club of Cebu II turned over an Orange Wall of Commitment to the city government on Nov. 23, with plans to bring the advocacy board to all 27 barangays.

  

Mayor Thadeo Jovito “Jonkie” Ouano, city councilors and City Hall employees signed the board to signify their commitment to the campaign.

  

Ouano said preventing violence against women requires collective action because abuse affects not only victims but also their families and communities.

  

“When we talk about violence against women, sometimes we think it’s only a women’s issue. But the truth is, this concerns all of us. It affects our families, our communities, and the kind of society we want our children to grow up in,” he said.

  

He said the commitment to protecting women should extend beyond the annual campaign and be reflected in everyday actions through respect, care and protection.

  

The turnover was attended by Zonta Club of Cebu II president Dr. Asuncion del Prado Tremedal and members of the organization, along with city councilors Atty. Carlo Fortuna, Jennifer Del Mar, Fritz Villamor, Ben Basiga, Jimmy Lumapas, Andreo Icalina and Raul Cabahug.

  

Zonta Club of Cebu II is a local chapter of Zonta International, which advances the status of women and girls through advocacy, service and community partnerships.

  

In Lapu-Lapu City, officials launched the 18-Day Campaign to End Violence Against Women on Nov. 23 at the City Hall lobby after the regular flag-raising ceremony.

  

Led by the City Social Welfare and Development Office (CSWDO), the campaign carries the theme “United for a VAW-Free Philippines: Our Home, Our Community, Our Responsibility.”

  

Mayor Ma. Cynthia “Cindi” King-Chan led the launch with members of the City Council, City Administrator Atty. Danilo Almendras and CSWDO officer-in-charge Sheila Baylosis.

  

King-Chan called for sustained efforts to make homes and communities safe for women, stressing that the campaign should not be limited to an annual observance.

  

“Every woman deserves to live without fear, to be treated with respect, and to feel safe wherever she may be,” she said, urging residents to speak out against abuse and take part in protecting women.

  

Baylosis said preventing violence requires community involvement beyond government intervention, particularly in helping survivors access support.

  

“VAW is not simply a women’s issue. It is a human rights concern, a family concern. It is a challenge that requires participation and commitment from the community,” she said.

  

She added that survivors should be able to seek assistance without fear, shame or judgment, emphasizing the role of social workers in providing support.

  

City councilors Ariane Neil Yap and Annabeth Cuizon also encouraged different sectors to participate, saying respect for others should be taught to children at home.

  

PLT. Lyka Antig, chief of the Family, Juvenile and Gender and Development Unit, represented Lapu-Lapu City Police Office director PCOL Agosto Daskeo and expressed the police office's support for initiatives promoting awareness and protection for women and children.

  

Participants in Lapu-Lapu also signed a pledge of commitment as part of the campaign.
