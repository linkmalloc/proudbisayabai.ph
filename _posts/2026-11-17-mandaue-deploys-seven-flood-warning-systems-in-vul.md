Seven flood monitoring systems have been installed in strategic locations across Mandaue City to help authorities detect rising water levels and warn communities before flooding worsens.

The equipment will monitor rainfall and water levels, giving disaster response teams information to assess possible flooding and alert residents, particularly those living near the Butuanon River.

Mayor Thadeo “Jonkie” Ouano said early warnings would help the city protect communities exposed to recurring floods.

“Here in Mandaue, flooding remains one of our biggest challenges, especially for our communities living down Butuanon River. The earlier we can warn our people, the better we can prepare and protect,” Ouano said.

The seven Flood Early Warning Systems (FEWS) were installed at Canduman Bridge, Sitio Salvacion in Barangay Maguikay, the 6.5-hectare reclamation area in Barangay Paknaan, and Boundary Bridge near Pacific Mall in Maguikay, among other strategic locations.

The monitoring devices are designed to track changes in weather and water conditions, helping authorities make informed decisions on when to issue warnings and mobilize response teams.

Ouano said the city government would train personnel to operate the systems and ensure that the equipment remains properly maintained.

“The City of Mandaue is fully committed to making this system work. We’ll make sure our teams are properly trained, equipment is maintained, and most importantly, this will lead to timely action,” he said.

The Department of Science and Technology (DOST), in partnership with South Korea’s National Disaster Management Research Institute (NDMI), turned over the seven units to the city government on Nov. 9.

Mandaue was selected as one of the beneficiaries of the 2026 project, which aims to strengthen local disaster preparedness through technology-assisted flood monitoring.

Ouano received the equipment during a turnover ceremony at bai Hotel Cebu, attended by DOST Region 7 officials and an NDMI delegation led by Dr. Woojung Choi.

Also present were Consolacion Mayor Nene Alegado, representatives of the Municipality of Liloan, Mandaue City Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Office head Buddy Allain Ybañez, and officials of the Philippine Atmospheric, Geophysical and Astronomical Services Administration (PAGASA).
