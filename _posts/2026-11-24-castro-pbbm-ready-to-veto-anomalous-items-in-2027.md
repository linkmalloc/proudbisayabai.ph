President Ferdinand Marcos Jr. will remove questionable projects from the proposed 2027 national budget if authorities find them anomalous, Malacañang said Tuesday, following Senator Panfilo Lacson's concerns over alleged duplicate allocations and unfunded structures.

Palace Press Officer Claire Castro said Marcos would not hesitate to veto budget items found to involve irregularities, particularly those that could burden taxpayers.

Lacson had flagged alleged double appropriations for flood control projects and questioned allocations for around 4,000 multipurpose buildings that reportedly remain unbuilt despite receiving funding in previous years.

Castro urged Lacson to submit his findings to the Department of Public Works and Highways (DPWH) so authorities could verify the projects and investigate possible irregularities.

“Kung may nakita na po si Senator Lacson na naging ghost project sa sinasabi niyang mga multipurpose building, aba’y umpisahan na po nating sampahan ng kaso, dahil kilala po natin si Senator Lacson na may mga verified documents po siyang panghahawakan,” Castro said during a press briefing.

She cautioned, however, that duplicate appropriations do not automatically mean a project received funding twice, explaining that some allocations may cover separate phases of implementation.

Castro assured the public that the President would act against questionable items identified in the spending plan.

“Hindi po mag-aatubili ang Pangulo na i-veto ito, tanggalin ito sa budget kapag nakita maanomalya ito,” she said.

“So, mas maganda na magkatulung-tulungan tayo at nirerespeto natin ang nakikita ni Senator Lacson, at kailangan ng mas maraming pang Senator Lacson para mas maayos at maging malinis ang ating budget at iyan ang utos kasi ng Pangulo,” Castro added.

Meanwhile, Castro declined to speculate on reported private conversations between Marcos and former House Speaker Martin Romualdez.

Her statement followed reports that former Public Works Secretary Manuel Bonoan had claimed in an affidavit that Marcos reprimanded Romualdez over congressional budget insertions.

Bonoan reportedly said Marcos had considered vetoing the entire 2025 General Appropriations Act after raising concerns over P436 billion in congressional budget insertions attributed to Romualdez.

Castro said the focus should instead be on the President's response after learning that Congress had substantially altered the Executive's proposed budget.

“Ang mahalaga dito, kung ano ba ang naging aksyon ng Pangulo noong nalaman niya na naiba halos ang proposed budget ng Ehekutibo,” she said.

Castro added that safeguards had been introduced through special provisions requiring the conditional implementation of certain projects.
