Traffic enforcers in Mandaue City may leave direct sunlight and take shelter in shaded areas while on duty, provided they remain near their assigned posts and ready to respond to road incidents, the Traffic Enforcement Agency of Mandaue (TEAM) said.

TEAM head Hyll Retuya said personnel are not required to spend their entire eight-hour shifts standing in the middle of the road, particularly as the El Niño phenomenon brings extreme heat.

He said enforcers should maintain visibility in their assigned areas during the early morning, especially before 9 a.m., when temperatures are generally more manageable. They may move to shaded locations as the heat intensifies, as long as they can immediately return when needed.

“Dili mawala sa ilang area. Just in case naay mga breakdowns or ubang incidents which will require their presence, naa ra gyud dayon sila nga makaduol,” Retuya said.

He also advised personnel to bring drinking water during their shifts to prevent dehydration.

Retuya said the availability of traffic signals across the city means enforcers do not need to remain in the middle of intersections at all times. Their physical presence on the road becomes more necessary when congestion requires them to manually operate traffic signals and manage vehicle movement.

“Mao man gud nay misconceptions nga if traffic enforcer ka, naa gyud ka dapat sa tunga,” he said.

TEAM has around 420 personnel, with approximately 300 assigned to field operations.

Retuya said the agency has not received reports of personnel suffering heat-related strokes so far but is preparing additional guidelines to protect its workforce when the Super El Niño reaches its peak.

The agency has also provided personnel with dry-fit uniforms to make field duties more comfortable during hot weather.
