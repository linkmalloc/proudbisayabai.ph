Local governments in southern Cebu are stepping up surveillance and border controls after African Swine Fever (ASF) was detected in Samboan, as provincial veterinary authorities work to prevent the virus from spreading to neighboring communities.

  

Provincial veterinarian Dr. Mary Rose Vincoy said the infections were confirmed in Barangay Colase, involving pigs from semi-commercial farms and backyard raisers.

  

The Cebu Provincial Veterinary Office (PVO) received the positive test result on November 13 and immediately sent a team to contain the outbreak.

  

“By November 13, in Samboan, the PVO received a positive result to one of the barangays. We immediately sent a team at once to contain ASF in the affected barangay,” Vincoy said.

  

Containment efforts have entered their second week, with veterinary personnel continuing sample collection and surveillance across Samboan's 15 barangays.

  

The PVO is awaiting results from 57 additional samples, while all 41 samples from the initial batch tested negative.

  

“Katong initial nga mga 41 negative man,” Vincoy said. (The initial 41 samples were negative.)

  

The office continues to enforce containment measures in Colase while checking other parts of the municipality and nearby towns for possible secondary infections. Vincoy said authorities may expand the quarantine zone if further cases emerge.

  

“And if ever naa, mag-extend ta sa quarantine zone,” she said. (If there are additional cases, we will extend the quarantine zone.)

  

The PVO has also intensified surveillance in three other southern Cebu municipalities. Vincoy said the ASF cases in neighboring Hinatilan had already been contained, while Ginatilan strengthened its border controls after learning of the Samboan infections.

  

Initial investigations in Samboan pointed to the possible entry of the virus through pigs smuggled from Negros. Authorities are also checking reports that some farms may have sourced live pigs from areas in Hinatilan, although the circumstances remain under investigation.

  

Vincoy said local government data placed Samboan's swine population at risk at more than 5,000. She declined to provide the exact number of pigs affected in Colase because the inventory is still changing.

  

“I cannot say now, but ang population at risk for the entire Samboan is 5,000, more than 5,000,” she said.

  

The PVO urged hog raisers across southern Cebu to report unusual pig deaths and suspected infections immediately.

  

Vincoy said early reporting and prompt testing are crucial to containing the disease. The office deploys its full response team once reverse transcription polymerase chain reaction (RT-PCR) testing confirms a positive ASF result.
