The International Criminal Court (ICC) said its upcoming trial of former President Rodrigo Duterte will focus on determining whether the charges against him are supported by evidence and law, rejecting claims that the proceedings would pursue a political objective.

  

ICC spokesperson Oriane Maillet said Tuesday that the court would uphold the rights of all participants and ensure a fair trial.

  

“Judges will ensure that the proceedings are conducted fairly and that the rights of all trial participants are respected. The Court's role is not to pursue any political objective,” Maillet said in a statement.

  

“Rather, its role is to determine, through independent and impartial judicial proceedings, whether the charges have been proven according to the law.”

  

Duterte faces charges of crimes against humanity of murder and attempted murder allegedly committed in the Philippines from November 2011 to March 2019 during his campaign against illegal drugs.

  

His trial is scheduled to open on Nov. 30 before Judge Joanna Korner.

  

On the first day, the judges will determine whether Duterte understands the charges and ask him to enter a plea.

  

Trial Chamber III will then hear opening statements from the prosecution and the Legal Representatives of Victims.

  

The prosecution currently expects to call about 100 witnesses during the trial. More than 500 victims have also been authorized to participate through common legal representatives, who will present their views before the court.

  

After the prosecution presents its case, the defense will have the opportunity to submit evidence and call its own witnesses.

  

The judges will deliberate on the evidence presented by both sides before deciding whether to acquit or convict Duterte.
