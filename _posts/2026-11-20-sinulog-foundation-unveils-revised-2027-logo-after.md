The Sinulog Foundation Inc. (SFI) has released a revised logo for the 2027 festival following public criticism over its initial design, which drew objections from devotees and prompted the Cebu City Council to call for corrections.

  

In a statement posted on Friday, Nov. 20, SFI said it had redrawn and refined the official logo to incorporate suggestions from the public after the original design was unveiled on Nov. 6.

  

“We have heard you. We have listened. And we have taken your suggestions to heart,” the foundation said.

  

SFI thanked devotees of the Señor Sto. Niño, Cebuanos and others who shared their comments and recommendations, saying the response showed how deeply the public values the festival's identity, traditions and religious significance.

  

“Sinulog belongs to the people,” the foundation said, describing the celebration as one that generations of Cebuanos have carried through faith, love and devotion.

  

The initial logo featured a festival dancer holding an image of the Santo Niño. 

  

However, devotees questioned the depiction of the Holy Child's raised right hand and the absence of his scepter.

  

In the traditional image, the Santo Niño raises his right hand in blessing while holding a scepter in the same hand and carries a cross-topped orb in his left. 

  

Critics said the logo's depiction appeared to alter the blessing gesture.

  

Fr. Jules Van Almerez, media liaison for the Basilica Minore del Sto. Niño de Cebu, earlier urged designers to use the religious image as their reference and cautioned against depicting the gesture as a peace sign.

  

The Cebu City Council subsequently approved a resolution authored by Councilor David Tumulak asking SFI to revise the logo. 

  

The resolution also urged the foundation to hire Cebuano artists for official festival logos and promotional materials, following allegations that artificial intelligence may have been used in creating the original design. 

  

At a Nov. 12 press conference, Councilor Pablo Labra II said the design remained under review and could be revised or replaced. 

  

The Mayor's Office had also sought clarification from SFI.

  

In its latest statement, the foundation said the logo represents only one part of the celebration, emphasizing that the spirit of Sinulog is rooted in devotion to the Holy Child, Cebuano heritage, music, dancing and the faith that brings devotees together.

  

SFI said public feedback helped shape a symbol it hopes Cebuanos can proudly call their own as preparations continue for Sinulog 2027.

  

“Viva Pit Señor! Viva Señor Sto. Niño!” the foundation said.
