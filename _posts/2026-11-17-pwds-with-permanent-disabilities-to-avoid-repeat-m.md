Persons with permanent disabilities will no longer need to undergo repeated medical examinations or submit updated medical certificates to renew their registration once their conditions are recorded in the national database, the National Council on Disability Affairs (NCDA) said.

The policy forms part of the nationwide rollout of the Unified Persons with Disabilities Identification System (UPWDIS), which aims to simplify registration and reduce the financial and administrative burden on persons with disabilities (PWDs).

The initiative responds to Senator Christopher Lawrence Go’s call to spare individuals with permanent disabilities from repeatedly visiting government offices to prove conditions already supported by medical documentation.

Go said digitalization should make public services faster, more efficient and secure, adding that PWDs should benefit from systems that are accessible and do not impose additional requirements.

Under the unified system, the NCDA will consolidate PWD records in a secure national database to prevent fraudulent identification cards and improve access to government-mandated benefits.

The council said existing records will be used to pre-fill basic applicant information whenever available, reducing the details applicants need to enter and supporting documents they must upload.

PWD identification cards issued by local government units (LGUs) before the system's rollout will remain valid during a three-year transition period.

To ensure residents can access the system regardless of their location or internet connectivity, the NCDA and its partners will conduct regular mobile and on-site registration activities in geographically isolated and disadvantaged areas.

LGU offices will also maintain physical assistance desks for applicants who have limited or no access to digital platforms.

The council and LGUs will establish a dedicated feedback channel to identify registration delays and procedures that impose unnecessary burdens on applicants.

“The purpose of the UPWDIS is precisely to eliminate the need for repeated submission of the same documents across different offices," the NCDA said.

"A permanent disability means permanent recognition — our system will reflect that,” it added.

The NCDA urged Persons with Disability Affairs Offices to coordinate with LGUs and barangay officials to reach residents and assist them with registration within their communities.

It also welcomed continued legislative oversight and support as it works with partner agencies to keep the registration process accessible and prevent unnecessary costs and delays.
