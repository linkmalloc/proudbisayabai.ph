Cebu Gov. Pamela Baricuatro wants to turn an unreleased P100-million assistance package into financial support for backyard hog raisers, but she must first convince Malacañang to allow the province to use the money for a different purpose.

  

Baricuatro said the Office of the President (OP) has renewed its demand for the provincial government to return the financial assistance originally intended for farmers, fisherfolk and families affected by the 2024 El Niño.

  

During a press conference on November 25, the governor said she would personally appeal to Executive Secretary Lucas Bersamin and seek permission to retain the funds for another program.

  

“We have been asked by the Office of the President to return P100 million,” Baricuatro said.

  

She plans to travel to Malacañang by December 9 to negotiate with Bersamin, arguing that the province could still use the money to help sectors facing economic difficulties.

  

“I will do my best. I will go to Malacañang by December 9 to see ES, to try to negotiate unta makuha nato balik ang P100 million kay sayang ni,” she said. 

  

Baricuatro wants to redirect the assistance to small-scale hog raisers struggling with inflation and rising production costs.

  

“Kani ra ni atong itabang sa atong mga backyard hog farmers na apektado man sa inflation,” she said. 

  

The P100-million grant originally sought to provide financial assistance to households and livelihood sectors severely affected by the 2024 El Niño. Under its guidelines, qualified farmers, fisherfolk and marginalized families could receive up to P10,000 each, with distribution required within 30 days of the grant.

  

However, the previous provincial administration under former governor Gwendolyn Garcia did not distribute the funds after proposing a different mechanism for releasing the assistance.

  

Garcia had proposed using QR-coded cards and limiting the aid to agricultural, fishery and livestock production supplies purchased through the Office of the Provincial Agriculturist.

  

In a letter dated August 27, 2025, the OP rejected the proposed arrangement, saying the assistance should go directly to qualified beneficiaries in cash rather than being restricted to purchases of production inputs.

  

Baricuatro said the conditions attached to the grant required the province to distribute the money as financial assistance, contributing to the delay in its release.

  

“Gipabalik nila, but I will do my best,” the governor said.

  

Malacañang later maintained that the purpose of the grant had lapsed because the provincial government failed to distribute the assistance within the prescribed period.

  

In a letter dated October 6, 2025, the OP rejected the province's appeal to proceed with the Deed of Donation under the new administration, reiterating that the funds should have reached beneficiaries within 30 days.

  

The OP renewed its refund demand in a letter dated October 15, 2026, noting that its Finance Office had yet to receive the P100 million from the Cebu provincial government.

  

Provincial Treasurer Roy Salubre also raised the matter in a November 23, 2026 letter, asking Baricuatro to authorize the Treasurer's Office to expedite the remittance.

  

The governor's proposed redirection of the funds remains subject to Malacañang's approval because the original grant specifically covered assistance for those affected by the 2024 El Niño.
