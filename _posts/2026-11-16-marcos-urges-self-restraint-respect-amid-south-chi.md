President Ferdinand Marcos Jr. called on ASEAN and China to translate their commitment to peaceful dispute settlement into concrete actions, emphasizing self-restraint, mutual respect and practical cooperation amid regional tensions.

Speaking at the 29th ASEAN-China Summit in Manila, Marcos said the 50th anniversary of the Treaty of Amity and Cooperation in Southeast Asia (TAC) should reinforce the region's commitment to international law and peaceful resolution of disputes.

“During this anniversary year, our shared commitment to international law and the peaceful settlement of disputes must be reflected in our actions, including through self-restraint, mutual respect, and practical cooperation,” he said.

Marcos did not identify any specific dispute in his remarks, which came amid continuing tensions in the South China Sea, particularly in the West Philippine Sea, where the Philippines and China have overlapping maritime claims.

He also cited progress in negotiations for a substantive and effective Code of Conduct (COC) in the South China Sea, saying discussions had moved forward under guidelines adopted in 2023.

The President stressed that the proposed agreement must remain consistent with international law, particularly the 1982 United Nations Convention on the Law of the Sea (UNCLOS).

As ASEAN chair for 2026, the Philippines has been pushing for the early completion of the COC negotiations to help manage maritime disputes and prevent incidents in contested waters from escalating.

The Department of Foreign Affairs (DFA) has said the negotiations are about 80 percent complete, although discussions remain ongoing.

Marcos also highlighted the 35th anniversary of ASEAN-China dialogue relations and the fifth anniversary of their comprehensive strategic partnership, citing cooperation in trade, investment, energy security and environmental protection.

He pointed to the joint statement on the regional impacts of developments in the Middle East and regional energy cooperation, which the two sides adopted in July.

Chinese Premier Li Qiang said two-way trade between China and ASEAN exceeded USD1 trillion last year, while cumulative two-way investments surpassed USD500 billion.

Li also cited expanding cooperation in the digital and green economies, artificial intelligence and regional connectivity.

The Philippines continues to pursue economic engagement with China while strengthening its security partnerships with other countries amid persistent tensions in the South China Sea.
