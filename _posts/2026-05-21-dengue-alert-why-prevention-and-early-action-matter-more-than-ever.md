As temperatures continue to rise and weather patterns become more unpredictable due to El Niño, health experts are warning the public about the increasing risk of dengue transmission in many communities. The combination of heat, humidity, and stagnant water creates ideal breeding conditions for mosquitoes, allowing the dengue virus to spread more quickly and affect more people.

Dengue is a viral infection transmitted through the bite of infected Aedes mosquitoes, particularly the Aedes aegypti species, which commonly breeds in clean but stagnant water found in containers, old tires, flower pots, canals, and uncovered water storage areas. Once infected, a person may experience symptoms ranging from mild fever to severe complications that can become life-threatening if left untreated.

Health authorities emphasize that dengue is preventable through proper sanitation, community cooperation, and early medical intervention. Residents are encouraged to remain vigilant and practice simple but effective preventive measures at home and in their surroundings.

Among the most important ways to help prevent dengue are:

* Eliminating stagnant water where mosquitoes breed
* Cleaning and covering water containers regularly
* Properly disposing of garbage and unused containers
* Using mosquito repellents and protective clothing
* Installing screens or nets to avoid mosquito bites
* Participating in community clean-up drives

The public is also advised to recognize the early signs and symptoms of dengue, which may include:

* High fever
* Severe headache
* Muscle and joint pain
* Skin rashes
* Nausea or vomiting
* Fatigue and weakness

Medical experts strongly advise individuals experiencing these symptoms to seek immediate consultation at the nearest health facility. Early diagnosis and proper hydration can greatly reduce the risk of severe dengue and other complications. Self-medication, especially with unprescribed drugs, should also be avoided.

Environmental awareness and community participation remain key in reducing dengue cases. By maintaining clean surroundings and practicing preventive measures consistently, communities can help protect children, senior citizens, and other vulnerable groups from the dangers of dengue infection.

As dengue cases continue to threaten many areas during warmer months, authorities remind the public that prevention starts at home and that collective action can save lives.

Stay informed. Stay protected.
