Sen. Panfilo Lacson pushed back against Senate Minority Leader Alan Peter Cayetano’s claim that government officials are widely perceived as thieves, saying honest public servants should not be included in such a sweeping judgment.

  

“I speak for myself and other public servants, elected or appointed, who don’t steal public funds or take bribes: Don’t drag us into his mess,” Lacson said in a social media post on Wednesday.

  

Lacson was responding to Cayetano’s remarks during a Facebook livestream on Tuesday, when the latter said public distrust in government had discouraged Filipinos from voluntarily contributing to efforts to reduce the country’s national debt.

  

“The sentiment is that all of us in government are thieves,” Cayetano said.

  

Cayetano raised the idea of creating a mechanism that would allow Filipinos willing and able to contribute money toward paying down the national debt, which reached a record P19.66 trillion at the end of September 2026.

  

He clarified that his proposal was not meant to impose another financial burden on ordinary citizens or make them responsible for the government's debt. Instead, he said, voluntary contributions would depend on public confidence that the money would be properly managed.

  

“There is no mechanism \[for voluntary contributions\] because no one believes anyone would donate. That’s because we perceive our government — not just the present administration, but successive administrations — as being engaged in a contest over who is more corrupt,” he said.

  

Cayetano said rebuilding trust would require the government to demonstrate that public funds are spent properly and borrowings are managed responsibly.

  

His remarks also drew a response from Malacañang, with Palace Press Officer Claire Castro saying it would be better for Cayetano to lead by example.

  

“It would be better for us to see Sen. Alan Cayetano himself starting it,” Castro said.

  

Lacson had earlier disputed another broad characterization of corruption in government, saying the problem should not be used to define all public servants.

  

“I beg to disagree. Such a statement is far too sweeping. Corruption may exist across the bureaucracy, but it does not define everyone. There are exceptions at every level and in every branch of government — the executive, the legislature and the judiciary,” Lacson said on X, responding to Vice President Sara Duterte’s claim that corruption “has embedded itself deep within the DNA of the government.”

  

Lacson entered public service as a law enforcer in 1971 and has spent more than five decades in government. He said honest public servants continue to work across government institutions against crime, corruption and other forms of wrongdoing.

  

The Bureau of the Treasury reported that national government debt stood at P19.66 trillion as of end-September 2026, equivalent to more than P170,000 per Filipino.
