Livestock owners in Lapu-Lapu City were reminded to keep their animals properly secured after city authorities conducted a stray animal operation in Barangay Bankal following complaints that roaming livestock were damaging crops and creating safety risks.

  

Mayor Ma. Cynthia “Cindi” King-Chan urged residents to practice responsible animal ownership and prevent livestock from wandering onto roads, sidewalks, pedestrian areas, and other public spaces.

  

The reminder came after the City Environment and Natural Resources Office (CENRO), together with the City Clearing Team and Barangay Bankal officials, conducted an operation at Sitio Mahayahay, Old Gym, on Thursday, July 23.

  

Authorities during the operation captured 20 cows, including 18 adult cattle and two calves, as well as five goats composed of three adult goats and two young goats.

  

CENRO said residents reported that stray animals had been damaging garden crops and posing hazards to motorists and pedestrians in the area.

  

The office warned that owners of roaming livestock may face penalties under the city’s Comprehensive Environmental and Sanitation Code (City Ordinance No. 484-97), which includes provisions against stray animals, animals defecating or urinating in public areas, and obstructions caused by roaming livestock.

  

The city government also cited Republic Act No. 10631, or the Animal Welfare Act, which sets regulations on the proper care and handling of animals.

  

City officials said livestock found roaming in public areas may be confiscated if owners fail to secure them properly.
