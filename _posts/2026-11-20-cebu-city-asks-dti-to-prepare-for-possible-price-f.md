Cebu City is calling for closer monitoring of food, medicine and other basic commodity prices, with a possible price freeze among the measures being considered ahead of the anticipated severe El Niño.

  

The City Council approved a resolution urging the Department of Trade and Industry (DTI) to strengthen price monitoring and assess the need for price-control measures before prolonged dry spells and extreme heat potentially disrupt supplies.

  

Councilor David “Dave” Tumulak, chairman of the Committee on Disaster Risk Reduction Management, Safety and Climate Change Adaptation, said early intervention would give distributors and suppliers time to prepare their inventories for possible shortages.

  

“We urge the DTI to act as soon as possible so that they can also plan,” Tumulak said in an interview.

  

He encouraged distributors to stock up on essential medicines and food products, including rice, instead of waiting for the effects of El Niño to intensify.

  

The resolution asks the DTI, in coordination with the Department of Health and other concerned national agencies, to monitor prices and evaluate appropriate controls against unreasonable increases in the cost of essential medicines, medical supplies, basic necessities and other commodities.

  

Tumulak said a price freeze is among the options the city wants the DTI to consider.

  

The resolution also urges Mayor Nestor Archival Sr. to direct concerned city offices and departments to prepare for the expected effects of El Niño, particularly on public health, water supply, agriculture and food security.

  

“To be sure that our food supply, medicines and other basic essentials remain available,” Tumulak said, explaining the objective of the proposed measures.

  

He stressed that essential supplies should remain available and accessible before, during and after the weather phenomenon.

  

The proposed preparations would involve coordination with the Philippine Atmospheric, Geophysical and Astronomical Services Administration, National Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Council, Department of Agriculture, Department of Social Welfare and Development, Department of Environment and Natural Resources, National Irrigation Administration and Metropolitan Cebu Water District, among other agencies.

  

The resolution cites the risks associated with severe El Niño, including prolonged dry spells, below-normal rainfall and extreme temperatures that could deplete water sources, reduce agricultural production and increase heat-related illnesses.

  

It also warns that reduced farm output and supply chain disruptions could push food prices higher, adding to the financial burden of households, particularly low-income families.

  

The measure invokes Republic Act 12287, or the Declaration of State of Imminent Disaster Act, which provides for anticipatory action and preventive interventions before projected hazards develop into catastrophic events.

  

However, Tumulak clarified that the council's approval of the resolution does not mean the city has declared a State of Imminent Disaster or a State of Calamity.

  

“No. Not yet,” he said when asked whether Cebu City had grounds to declare a state of calamity.

  

He explained that the resolution serves as a recommendation for the mayor and national agencies to begin preparations ahead of the anticipated effects of El Niño.

  

Tumulak said the city was still awaiting data from its departments to determine the extent of preparations needed. The resolution had also been furnished to the NDRRMC and other concerned agencies for their information and appropriate action.

  

Any price freeze or other price-control measures would still depend on the assessment and action of the DTI and concerned national agencies. 

  

The council resolution does not automatically impose price controls on commodities.
