Sen. Francis “Kiko” Pangilinan has called for an investigation and possible criminal charges against those responsible for the alleged improper disposal of 29 pets in San Jose del Monte, Bulacan, after their owners reportedly paid for cremation services.

In a statement issued on Monday, Nov. 2, Pangilinan said the incident raised concerns over the treatment of grieving pet owners who entrusted their animals’ remains to a service provider expecting them to receive a dignified final resting place.

He urged local authorities to establish what happened and hold those responsible accountable if evidence confirms that the animals were discarded instead of cremated as promised.

Pangilinan said the alleged disposal of the animal remains could violate Republic Act 9003, or the Ecological Solid Waste Management Act, as well as local sanitation and cleanliness ordinances.

He added that collecting payment for cremation services that were not performed could constitute estafa under Article 315 of the Revised Penal Code. Those responsible could also face claims for damages over violations of contractual obligations under Article 1170 of the Civil Code.

The senator called on the local government and concerned agencies to investigate the incident promptly and assist affected pet owners in filing appropriate complaints.

He also cited his previous work on animal welfare legislation, including his collaboration with the Philippine Animal Welfare Society (PAWS) during the 15th Congress that led to the passage of Republic Act 10631, which amended the Animal Welfare Act of 1998 and strengthened penalties for animal cruelty, neglect and abuse.

Pangilinan said he continues to push for stronger animal protection through Senate Bill 2501, or the proposed Revised Animal Welfare Act, also known as Dolly’s Act.

The measure seeks to establish an Animal Welfare Bureau tasked with setting and enforcing standards for animal care, including access to adequate food, water, shelter and veterinary services, as well as protection from cruelty.

He said he would also seek to include facilities such as pet crematoriums in the final version of the proposed legislation.

Pangilinan stressed that responsible care for pets should extend beyond their lives, saying they deserve proper and dignified treatment even after death.
