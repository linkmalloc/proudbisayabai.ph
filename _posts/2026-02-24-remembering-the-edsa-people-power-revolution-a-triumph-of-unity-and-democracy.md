Every year, Filipinos commemorate the anniversary of the EDSA People Power Revolution, a historic four day movement from February 22 to 25, 1986 that reshaped the nation’s political landscape and restored democratic governance in the Philippines.

**A Peaceful Uprising That Changed History**

What set the 1986 revolution apart was its peaceful nature. Millions of Filipinos gathered along Epifanio de los Santos Avenue in Metro Manila, standing side by side in prayer, protest, and solidarity. Religious leaders, civilians, government officials, and members of the military converged in a remarkable display of unity, calling for political reform and accountability.

Instead of violence, the movement was defined by courage, faith, and nonviolent resistance. Ordinary citizens offered food, flowers, and prayers to soldiers, symbolizing a collective desire for peaceful change. The world watched as Filipinos demonstrated that the power of the people could overcome authoritarian rule without bloodshed.

**A Defining Moment for Philippine Democracy**

The success of the EDSA Revolution marked the end of a long period of political turmoil and ushered in a new democratic government. It reaffirmed the principles of freedom of expression, fair elections, and constitutional rule.

Beyond its political impact, EDSA became a global symbol of peaceful resistance. It showed that meaningful change can be achieved when citizens unite with a shared vision for a better future.

**Why It Still Matters Today**

Decades later, the legacy of EDSA continues to resonate. The anniversary is not only a remembrance of history but also a reminder of civic responsibility. It encourages Filipinos to remain vigilant in protecting democratic institutions and to value the freedoms gained through sacrifice and solidarity.

As the country observes this significant day, the spirit of People Power stands as a testament to unity, courage, and the enduring strength of a nation determined to shape its own destiny.
