From its geological formations to its cultural traditions, Bohol has secured another four years of international recognition, reinforcing its standing as a destination that must balance tourism growth with heritage conservation.

The Bohol Island UNESCO Global Geopark received a unanimous Green Card recommendation from the UNESCO Global Geoparks Council, confirming its continued compliance with the organization's standards and retaining its status as the Philippines' first and only UNESCO Global Geopark.

The council reached the decision during its 11th session at UNESCO headquarters in Paris, France, on Nov. 2 to 4, 2026, following the province's first revalidation mission on Aug. 3 to 7.

The Green Card affirms Bohol's compliance with UNESCO criteria covering geological heritage and conservation, territory and visibility, and sustainable development policies and activities.

Gov. Erico Aristotle “Aris” Aumentado credited the recognition to the cooperation of local government units, communities, stakeholders and partners in protecting the province's natural and cultural resources.

“Ang Green Card bunga sa panaghiusa ug paningkamot sa atong mga LGU, komunidad, stakeholders, partners, ug ang tibuok katawhang Bol-anon nga adunay papel sa pag-atiman sa atong pinalanggang probinsya. Apan kauban niini ang mas dakong responsibilidad nga atong panalipdan ug ampingan ang atong natural ug kultural nga bahandi alang sa sunod nga mga henerasyon,” Aumentado said.

The geopark connects communities, nature and culture through three major areas: geoeducation, geoconservation and geotourism. Its initiatives promote environmental learning, conservation, sustainable tourism, local entrepreneurship and culinary traditions.

Bohol's renewed recognition comes alongside efforts to make its transport infrastructure more sustainable.

Bohol-Panglao International Airport (BPIA), operated by Aboitiz InfraCapital Bohol Airport Corporation, won the Sustainability Initiative of the Year – Philippines award at the 2026 Travel Daily Media Travel Trade Excellence Awards – Asia.

The airport received the distinction on Nov. 10 at the Sands Expo and Convention Centre in Singapore for its ECO-Airport Project, which comprises seven initiatives aimed at reducing the environmental impact of airport operations.

These include solar power generation, water conservation, electric ground support equipment, plastic reduction and a partnership with the Bohol Island UNESCO Global Geopark.

BPIA General Manager Aldwin Uy said the airport's sustainability programs complement the province's efforts to protect its natural assets while expanding tourism and connectivity.

“Bohol's continued recognition as a UNESCO Global Geopark reinforces the importance of developing tourism in a way that protects the very assets that make the island unique. At ABAC, we believe that infrastructure development and environmental stewardship must go hand in hand,” Uy said.

He said BPIA aims to support the province's tourism industry, empower local communities and help preserve Bohol's natural and cultural heritage through sustainable airport operations.

The airport's recognition formed part of a sweep by Aboitiz InfraCapital Airports, with its other two airports also receiving awards at the 2026 ceremony.

Mactan-Cebu International Airport won Airport Experience of the Year – Philippines, while Laguindingan International Airport in Misamis Oriental received Health and Safety Initiative of the Year – Philippines.

The three awards recognized the operator's work in improving passenger experience, environmental responsibility and health and safety standards across its airport network.

Bohol's renewed UNESCO recognition and the airport's sustainability award highlight the province's efforts to make tourism development work alongside the protection of its geological heritage, biodiversity and cultural identity.
