The Office of the Ombudsman has rejected Dalaguete Mayor Ronald Allan G. Cesante's appeal and maintained his six-month suspension without pay, along with five other municipal officials, over irregularities in the construction of a municipal warehouse on privately owned land.

  

In a Consolidated Order, the Ombudsman denied the officials' motion for reconsideration and upheld its Aug. 29, 2025 decision finding them administratively liable for Gross Neglect of Duty.

  

The other officials are Municipal Assessor and Bids and Awards Committee (BAC) Chairperson Edmund M. Centeno; Municipal Budget Officer and BAC Vice-Chairperson Brenda S. Vergara; and BAC members Essandre P. Amprad, Donald Abraham P. Lara and Cristina Y. Miranda.

  

The case began after Jose Rene E. Moran and Braulio G. Moran questioned the municipal government's decision to build a warehouse on a privately owned lot covered by a Special Power of Attorney (SPA) that the heirs of Teodorica Severino and Pedro Moran executed in favor of Hetzel Moran.

  

The officials defended their actions, saying they had the authority to initiate and conduct pre-procurement proceedings because Hetzel Moran had previously signed a Permit to Enter.

  

Cesante also argued that the BAC, rather than the mayor as head of the procuring entity, bore responsibility for the alleged procurement irregularities.

  

He invoked the Arias doctrine, citing the Supreme Court's 1989 ruling in Arias v. Sandiganbayan (G.R. No. 81563), which allows heads of government offices to rely on subordinates to process documents in good faith and does not automatically hold them liable for irregularities simply because they signed the documents.

  

The Ombudsman rejected these defenses after finding that the SPA did not authorize Hetzel Moran to let the municipality enter the property and construct the warehouse.

  

The document only authorized her to enter into a Deed of Exchange with the municipal government, the Ombudsman said.

  

It faulted Cesante and the BAC members for relying on the Permit to Enter without first verifying whether Hetzel Moran had secured authority from all the legal heirs to grant the municipality access to the property.

  

“Respondents Mayor and BAC members should not have merely relied on the Permit to Enter but proactively verified from all the legal heirs if indeed Hetzel Moran had the legal authority to allow the Municipality unlimited access over the said lot,” the order stated.

  

The Ombudsman said the officials should have checked the validity and completeness of the documents before proceeding with procurement, awarding the project and building the warehouse.

  

It also rejected Cesante's reliance on the Arias doctrine, stressing that the principle does not automatically shield public officials from administrative accountability.

  

“They should have taken meticulous efforts to ensure that all documents were in order before the procurement and award of the project, and before the warehouse inside the questioned lot was constructed,” the decision stated.

  

The Ombudsman found that the officials' motion for reconsideration presented no newly discovered evidence or grave error of fact or law that could justify reversing its earlier ruling.

  

It said their arguments merely repeated points that the office had already examined and discussed in its previous decision.

  

The order therefore retains the finding of Gross Neglect of Duty against all six officials and the penalty of six months' suspension from government service without pay.
