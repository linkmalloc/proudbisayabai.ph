A dispute over patient care at the Barili District Hospital has widened into a public exchange between a Cebu lawmaker and a provincial health official, with both sides insisting on accountability while presenting different concerns about the facility.

Third District Rep. Karen Garcia has called on the Cebu Provincial Government to expedite its investigation into the deaths of a mother and her newborn, while provincial health consultant Dr. Nikki Catalan has defended hospital personnel and cited improvements in staffing and services under Gov. Pamela Baricuatro’s administration.

The case involves Angelle Fe Isugan, who died with her child after being brought to the provincial government-operated hospital on Nov. 4.

Her husband, Jhovell Patlingrao, said in a social media video that he rushed her to the facility after she suffered severe bleeding. He questioned the medical attention she received, including an alleged internal examination despite prenatal records indicating placenta previa, a condition in which the placenta covers or partially covers the cervix.

Patlingrao also raised concerns about the time it took hospital personnel to refer his wife to a higher-level facility as her condition worsened.

The Provincial Health Office (PHO), headed by Dr. Jenina Canoy, has started an investigation and required incident reports from the attending physician and other medical personnel involved in the case.

Garcia wants the review expedited, saying her office has received several complaints about the hospital’s personnel and services. These include another reported newborn death, an allegation that a patient was refused admission after the facility’s cut-off time, and complaints about the unavailability of a complete blood count test and other laboratory services.

“Among nadawat, nadungog, nabasa, ug nasabtan ang mga nagsunod-sunod nga reklamo ug kabalaka bahin sa personnel ug services sa Barili District Hospital,” Garcia said.

She urged the Integrated Provincial Health Office (IPHO), which supervises the hospital, to speed up its investigation into the deaths of Isugan and her child.

“We respectfully hope nga mapaspasan sa IPHO ang ilang gihimong investigation sa kaso sa inahan nga adunay placenta previa ug namatay uban sa iyang anak,” she said.

Garcia said the investigation must establish what happened without prematurely assigning blame to either the families or healthcare workers.

Catalan, meanwhile, said the hospital’s operating conditions and the efforts of its personnel should also be considered in evaluating the complaints.

She said the 25-bed infirmary had only 58 personnel when the current provincial administration assumed office, including two full-time doctors and 17 nurses.

By July 2026, the provincial government had hired another 57 workers, bringing the hospital’s workforce to 115. The current personnel complement includes nine doctors, 28 nurses, 14 midwives and nursing attendants, 18 allied health personnel, 21 administrative and clerical employees, and 25 support and utility workers.

Catalan also reported that the facility handled 25,212 admissions and consultations from January to September 2026. These included 1,532 inpatient admissions, 7,300 emergency room consultations and 16,380 outpatient consultations.

She said the hospital laboratory, which did not operate around the clock before July 2025, now provides 24-hour service. It had conducted 4,960 tests as of Nov. 15.

Despite the additional personnel and expanded services, Catalan acknowledged that the hospital continues to operate beyond its intended capacity. Inpatient bed utilization reached 124 percent, while emergency room occupancy stood at 230 percent.

“Our doctors, nurses, and medical and nonmedical staff are doing their best to serve your constituents under these conditions,” she said.

Catalan also said she would stand by hospital personnel against judgments made before the investigation establishes the facts.

“Every life is precious, and every complaint about patient care deserves to be taken seriously,” she added.

The exchange also covered the availability of government medical assistance through the Medical Assistance for Indigent and Financially Incapacitated Patients (MAIFIP) program.

Catalan claimed that the Barili District Hospital had not received an allocation under the program since September 2025, citing information from provincial social workers. She urged Garcia to help address the reported funding gap.

“Congresswoman, this is your district, too,” Catalan told the lawmaker.

Garcia countered that her office had already extended assistance to patients and families who sought help, including residents outside the Third District and in Cebu City.

She also cited assistance extended through the Barili Infirmary, which the municipal government operates, saying both facilities need resources to serve residents.

However, Garcia maintained that staffing levels, available services and financial assistance should not divert attention from the questions raised by the reported deaths.

“The issue we hope to address is not simply the number of personnel, consultations, or services available. It is the loss of lives, the questions of grieving families, and the need to understand whether there were gaps in care, and whether anything could have been done differently,” she said.

She clarified that the MAIFIP funding concern was separate from the complaints about patient care and the deaths.

Garcia said she was not seeking to condemn hospital employees but wanted the families’ concerns heard and addressed through a fair investigation.

“Listening to patients and respecting healthcare workers are not opposing goals. Both are important to improving healthcare for everyone,” she said.

She also expressed willingness to return to the hospital and meet with its personnel to discuss possible improvements.
