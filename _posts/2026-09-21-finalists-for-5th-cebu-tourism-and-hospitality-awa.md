More than 60 tourism and hospitality workers, educators and industry initiatives have made the cut for the 2026 Cebu Tourism and Hospitality Awards (CTHA), with this year’s finalists reflecting the people and programs shaping Cebu’s tourism industry.

  

The Hotel, Resort & Restaurant Association of Cebu Inc. (HRRACI) announced the finalists across eight award categories, recognizing frontline employees, behind-the-scenes workers, supervisors, managers, educators, sustainability programs and marketing campaigns.

  

HRRACI President Mia Singson-Leon said judges are looking beyond how well nominees perform their regular duties, with leadership, innovation and measurable impact among the factors considered.

  

For employees, supervisors and managers, the panel also considers how nominees improve systems, train colleagues and contribute to guest experiences and team performance.

Bejagan of Seda Central Bloc Cebu; Marjorie C. Booc of bai Hotel Cebu; Argen Marie C. Catubig of Tambuli Seaside Resort and Spa; Shaun Andrei P. Del Socorro of NUSTAR Resort Cebu; Marc Jay G. Gonzaga of Crimson Resort & Spa Mactan; and Cleford O. Maguate of Radisson Blu Cebu.

The awards also recognize sustainability efforts through the Sustainability Champion of the Year category. Finalists are “Dusit Tree of Life” by Dusit Thani Mactan Cebu, “Growing with Purpose” by La Joya Farm Resort and Spa, “Skills Behind Walls” by Radisson Blu Cebu, and “A Perfect Circle: Transforming Hospitality Waste Into Community Happiness” by Savoy Hotel Mactan Newtown.

  

For Best Marketing Campaign of the Year, the finalists are “Pink Power” by bai Hotel Cebu; “LIKHA: A Journey Beyond Wine, Culinary, & Arts” by Mercure Mactan Cebu; “NUSTAR Dragon Boat Regatta: Advancing Cebu’s Sports Tourism Story” and “WOVEN: 4 Years of Elevated Moments” by NUSTAR Resort Cebu; “The Bloc Collective: A Lokal Pop-up” by Seda Central Bloc Cebu; and “#BookDirect” by Tambuli Seaside Resort and Spa.

  

Singson-Leon said the awards also highlight how the industry has responded to challenges, with some finalists remaining in tourism through difficult years and helping retrain teams, while others have taken on mentorship roles for young workers from the Visayas and Mindanao.

  

She identified technical skills combined with genuine care, adaptability to changing markets and technology, and pride in Cebuano identity as qualities shared by many of this year’s finalists.

  

The hospitality industry is also adjusting to artificial intelligence, changing travel markets, the continuing fuel crisis and uncertainty arising from conflicts in the Middle East, making adaptability an increasingly important quality for tourism workers and businesses, Singson-Leon said.

  

At the same time, she said Cebuano identity remains central to the industry, reflected in local food, the way visitors are welcomed and the experiences created for tourists.

  

The 5th CTHA is co-presented by Ayala Center Cebu and Cignal TV and endorsed by the Department of Tourism Central Visayas.
