Filipino real estate entrepreneur Robbie Antonio is expanding his development strategy beyond branded residential projects as he looks to participate in the next phase of Philippine urban growth through a broader mix of property and infrastructure-related ventures.

  

Antonio said his focus now includes luxury residences, resorts, hotels, retail spaces, affordable housing, and potential investments in office, industrial, and data center assets.

  

The strategy builds on his experience in design-led and branded developments, which he said will remain a key part of creating projects that deliver long-term value.

  

> “There are easier ways to build. But I’ve seen what happens when you start with clarity, work with people at the top of their field, and design something that can stand the test of time," he said.

  

Antonio has developed projects through partnerships with international architects, designers, and luxury brands, including The Centurion in Manhattan, New York, developed with I.M. Pei of Pei Partnership Architects; Essensa East Forbes in Bonifacio Global City, developed with I.M. Pei through Pei Cobb Freed & Partners; and branded residential projects such as The Milano Residences with Versace Home, Livingston Tower at Acqua with Missoni Home, Iguazu Tower at Acqua with YOO by Starck, and Century Spire in Makati with Armani/Casa interiors and architecture by Daniel Libeskind.

  

For Antonio, these developments show how real estate can contribute to shaping cities rather than simply adding structures.

  

> “Buildings should do more than occupy space,” Antonio said. 

  

“They should create value, shape communities, and remain relevant even as markets change," he added.

  

Across the companies he has led and worked with, Antonio said he has completed 33 in-city vertical projects and 26 residential projects, delivered more than 17,700 vertical residential units, and managed over 71 million square feet of property across 93 developments.

  

He said the experience provides a foundation for exploring new segments of the property market as Philippine cities continue to expand.

  

> “Real estate is ultimately a long-term business. The challenge is to build assets that can hold value, serve real demand, and contribute to the way cities grow," he said.

  

Antonio said future projects will continue to focus on combining market discipline, design excellence, international partnerships, and responsiveness to evolving urban needs.

  

As demand grows across residential, commercial, and emerging infrastructure sectors, Antonio said he sees opportunities to help raise standards in the country’s built environment through projects designed for long-term relevance.
