Mandaue City is preparing to conduct mosquito-misting operations in all public schools as the Department of Education (DepEd) division steps up efforts to protect learners amid a rise in dengue infections.

  

City Legal Officer Atty. Marinel Oro said three schools have already undergone misting in coordination with the City Health Office: Opao Elementary School, Tabok II Elementary School and Canduman Elementary School.

  

The division is awaiting schedules for the remaining campuses, but Oro said the target is to cover all public schools, including those that have not reported dengue cases.

  

“Ang misting is mao na atong goal nga tanang schools, but we understand nga dili gyud na nato mahurot tanan immediately,” Oro said. (Misting all schools is our goal, but we understand that we cannot complete the operations immediately.)

  

The initiative comes after the division recorded 36 dengue cases among elementary pupils and one case at the secondary level in November.

  

Three learners have died from dengue-related complications, including two from Opao Elementary School and one from Tabok II Elementary School. Oro said 31 of the affected learners were already recovering.

  

Aside from misting, the division has installed Olyset insecticide-treated mosquito nets in selected ground-floor classrooms to keep mosquitoes from entering learning areas.

  

Schools have also been directed to strengthen their daily 4 p.m. cleanup routine to eliminate potential mosquito breeding sites.

  

Oro said the routine follows four measures: taob, taktak, tuyo and takip. These involve emptying containers that collect water, removing dirt and debris, drying wet surfaces and covering water containers.

  

Each school has also formed a dengue brigade composed of school personnel and learners to help ensure that cleanup activities are carried out regularly.

  

Oro said school heads have been reminded to share dengue prevention information with parents and encourage them to seek medical attention immediately if their children develop a fever.

  

Learners whose fever persists for two to three days are advised to undergo a complete blood count to help determine whether they have dengue.

  

She also assured parents that absences due to illness would be excused and that schools could arrange catch-up activities for affected learners.

  

“Priority gyud nato safety sa mga bata,” Oro said. (The safety of the children is really our priority.)

  

The division is also seeking the support of Parent-Teacher Associations to reinforce dengue prevention at home, recognizing that infections can occur both inside and outside school premises.

  

Oro said preventive measures would be implemented in all campuses regardless of whether they had recorded cases, stressing that schools would not wait for infections to emerge before taking action.
