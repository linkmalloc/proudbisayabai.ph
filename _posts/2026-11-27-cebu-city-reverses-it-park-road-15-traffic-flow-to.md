Motorists heading to Cebu IT Park may soon avoid one of the traffic bottlenecks along Gov. Cuenco Avenue as Cebu City changes the direction of Road 15 beginning November 27.

  

The city will conduct a dry run of the new traffic scheme starting at midnight Friday, turning Road 15 from an exit route into an entry point for vehicles entering the business district.

  

Under the revised arrangement, motorists leaving IT Park will use V. Padriga Street and make a U-turn beneath the Archbishop Reyes Avenue flyover instead of crossing traffic along Road 15.

  

The adjustment will give motorists three access points into IT Park: J.M. Del Mar Street, Road 15 and V. Padriga Street.

  

Cebu City Traffic Management Coordination Assistant Department Head Atty. Kent Jongoy said vehicles exiting IT Park have been disrupting traffic along Gov. Cuenco Avenue, forcing motorists traveling in both directions to stop.

  

“Duol na unta kaayo ko sa Ayala pero ma-traffic na hinuon ko,” Jongoy said. (I am already supposed to be very near Ayala, but I still end up stuck in traffic.)

  

He said the city reviewed traffic data from both the Cebu City Government and IT Park before deciding to reverse the direction of Road 15.

  

Mayor Nestor Archival said the main objective is to eliminate vehicle movements that cross the traffic flow at the busy junction.

  

“So mao nay main idea in order nga wala nay mu-cross sa 15,” Archival said. (So that is the main idea, to prevent vehicles from crossing at Road 15.)

  

Jongoy said the adjustment is expected to benefit motorists traveling northbound and southbound, particularly those using the Banilad-Talamban corridor.

  

The new scheme builds on traffic management measures implemented under the city's Bantay Discipline Zone.

  

Jongoy said travel time from Talamban Gym to the TESDA flyover has declined from an estimated one hour and 35 minutes to less than an hour.

  

He attributed the improvement to designated loading and unloading areas, fewer traffic conflict points, assistance from private establishments and better management of public utility vehicle stops.

  

Archival said vehicle volume along the corridor also increased from around 40,000 before the traffic program to more than 60,000 after its implementation, even as travel time declined.

  

The city will initially test the Road 15 arrangement through a dry run to give motorists time to adjust to the revised routes. 

  

Authorities have posted traffic advisories and are coordinating with nearby barangays and IT Park stakeholders.

  

Jongoy said the city hopes the new traffic arrangement will eventually become permanent if it delivers the projected improvements.

  

“We are looking at our projection nga hopefully this will be permanent and this can give our motorists ... a smoother and easier driving experience,” he said.

  

Archival said the city also hopes to use the Banilad-Talamban traffic program as a model for gradually addressing congestion in other areas if the interventions prove effective.
