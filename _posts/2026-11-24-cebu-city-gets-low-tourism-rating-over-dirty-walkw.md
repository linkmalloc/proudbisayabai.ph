Overflowing garbage bins, inadequate public toilets and poorly connected pedestrian routes are among the problems affecting tourists visiting Cebu City's heritage attractions, prompting the city government to order immediate improvements.

Tourism stakeholders gave the Cebu City Government a rating of only four out of 10 for its efforts to attract and accommodate visitors during a recent tourism summit.

Kenneth Siasar, chief of staff of Mayor Nestor Archival Sr., said the assessment prompted the mayor to direct concerned offices to address the problems, particularly around Magellan's Cross, Plaza Sugbo and nearby heritage sites.

“I do not know kung this is positive, but we are using this as a motivating factor for us. Ang ilang rating sa atong participation in terms of attracting tourists and in terms of accommodating tourists, four out of 10,” Siasar said during a press conference on Monday, Nov. 23.

Participants cited the absence of a clear pedestrian route connecting Fort San Pedro, Plaza Independencia, Magellan's Cross, the Basilica Minore del Santo Niño and the Parian heritage district.

They also raised concerns over limited parking, accumulated garbage, event tarpaulins left in public areas and insufficient public toilets.

Tourists visiting Magellan's Cross also reportedly encounter children and other individuals repeatedly asking for money, forcing visitors to watch their belongings instead of enjoying the attractions.

Siasar said the city immediately sent Department of Public Services personnel to clean the Magellan's Cross area after the summit.

Archival also ordered regular monitoring and the assignment of workers specifically tasked with maintaining cleanliness in the area.

The city is coordinating with its public safety office and anti-mendicancy committee to address solicitation around tourist destinations, particularly during weekends when visitor numbers increase.

To address the shortage of public toilets, the city temporarily opened the ground floor of the Legislative Building for tourists to use its restroom facilities. Archival is also considering a separate public toilet near Plaza Sugbo.

Siasar said he presented the stakeholders' concerns to the mayor during Monday's flag-raising ceremony.

He also shared feedback from the Balik Cebu Core Group, whose members observed city cleaners sweeping the walkways around Magellan's Cross following Archival's directive.

"Day one, 10 points. Mayor Nestor and his chief of staff kept their word, aksyon agad," a group member said in a message shared with Siasar.

However, the city still needs to address longer-term concerns, particularly the development of connected pedestrian routes that would allow visitors to move more conveniently between heritage attractions.

The tourism summit participants also gave private establishments a rating of six out of 10.

Siasar said Cebu City currently allocates P10 million annually for tourism. Archival may consider increasing the budget once the Cebu City Tourism Commission submits a clear tourism development roadmap for 2027.

He said the mayor wants the commission to present specific programs and figures to justify any additional funding.

Siasar said the low assessment should serve as motivation for the city to improve its facilities and services for visitors.
