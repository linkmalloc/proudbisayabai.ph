Portions of the Sangguniang Panlungsod (SP) office at Mandaue City Hall sustained cracks and ceiling damage following the magnitude 5.2 earthquake that struck on April 6.

The minor structural issues were discovered after the Mandaue City Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Office conducted inspections across city government offices to ensure the safety of employees and the public after the Monday afternoon tremor. 

The earthquake’s epicenter was traced to Bogo City.

Mandaue City Administrator Gonzalo “Sally” Malig-on Jr. said the city is still awaiting the final assessment from the Office of the Building Official but emphasized that no major damage has been recorded so far.

Initial findings from the CDRRMO showed that the SP office is currently unsafe for occupancy, particularly due to ceiling damage above work cubicles. 

As a precaution, personnel were temporarily evacuated and the affected area was cordoned off.

CDRRMO head Buddy Ybañez confirmed that employees are not allowed to enter or remain in the affected section. 

He added that their office and the building officials are coordinating to release a full report and determine necessary repairs.

“Recommend gyud namo nga i-repair nalang gyud ang kisame,” Ybañez said, stressing the need for immediate ceiling repairs.

He also noted that some cracks observed in other parts of city hall were pre-existing and superficial.

Meanwhile, Malig-on said Mayor Thadeo Jovito “Jonkie” Ouano is waiting for the final report before deciding on repair priorities and identifying funding sources.

SP office aide Roel Pundavela recalled the tense moments during the quake, saying the situation could have been more dangerous if the shaking had lasted longer.

“Kung na taud-tauran to, kuyaw gyud to,” he said, adding that they were fortunate to evacuate quickly.

Employees have been advised to remain alert for possible aftershocks as authorities continue to monitor the situation. (VCL)
