A proposed traffic rerouting scheme at Cebu IT Park is undergoing further review after its first dry run on Friday, Nov. 27, failed to ease congestion amid heavy vehicle volume and road constraints.

  

The Cebu City Transportation Office (CCTO) suspended the test late Friday as traffic worsened around the business district, prompting officials to reassess the arrangement rather than proceed with its implementation.

  

CCTO Assistant Department Head Kent Francisco Jongoy said the city would study the traffic data gathered during the trial and recommend adjustments or alternative measures.

  

“We're not saying that we're stopping. We're saying that we're going back to the drawing board and looking for adjustments,” Jongoy said in an interview on Saturday.

  

The proposed scheme would make Road 15 an entry point to IT Park, while motorists leaving the business district would exit through V. Padriga Street and make a U-turn beneath the Archbishop Reyes Avenue flyover.

  

The arrangement aimed to prevent vehicles exiting IT Park from crossing traffic along Road 15, which affects motorists traveling between Talamban and the city.

  

However, the dry run coincided with payday, an approaching long weekend and increased holiday activity, contributing to unusually heavy traffic.

  

Jongoy said the CCTO recorded nearly 16,000 vehicles during its afternoon monitoring period. The traffic survey team started counting at 3 p.m. and continued until 10 p.m., with the tally already reaching nearly 16,000 by around 9 p.m.

  

The count covered vehicles heading toward Ayala, passing over or beneath the flyover, and using the side road.

  

Traffic volume rose sharply in the afternoon, with the office recording an increase of around 3,000 to 4,000 vehicles within an hour at about 4 p.m. Congestion became heavier between 5 p.m. and 9 p.m.

  

The morning traffic data initially showed better results. Jongoy said travel time from the Country Mall area to the stretch beyond the flyover remained below 10 minutes between 7 a.m. and 9 a.m.

  

Conditions deteriorated as more vehicles entered the area later in the day.

  

Jongoy also cited poor lane discipline and the limited width of the side road as factors that affected the scheme. Some motorists failed to follow designated lanes, particularly those heading toward Mabolo and Banilad, disrupting vehicles that needed to use the U-turn slot.

  

He said the city must also consider existing road conditions before implementing any changes. The Department of Public Works and Highways had provided information on the road and offered assistance on possible side-road expansion.

  

The mayor has asked the traffic office to present its findings. Jongoy said the CCTO was preparing recommendations for the Traffic Management Council, which the city hopes to present and make public as early as next week, possibly by Friday.

  

Jongoy said the dry run was intended to determine whether the proposed arrangement could work under actual road conditions, rather than serve as a pass-or-fail test.

  

“It's not a pass or fail. It's always a... feasibility,” he said.
