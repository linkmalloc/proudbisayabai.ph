The Mandaue City Government is trying to secure permission to inspect a privately owned property beside Opao Elementary School after 15 dengue cases were reported among its students and two five-year-old children died from the disease.

  

Mayor Thadeo Jovito “Jonkie” Ouano said residents had raised concerns that the property could be a mosquito breeding site, prompting the city to locate its owner and seek access to the premises.

  

The city initially checked whether the lot belonged to Virginia Farms or Virginia Foods. However, with assistance from former Mandaue Chamber of Commerce and Industry president Mark Anthony Ynoc, officials learned that neither company owned the property.

  

Ouano said the city had yet to gain entry because a security personnel guards the premises. Officials are continuing efforts to identify and contact the property owner.

  

He clarified that the city was not accusing any company or property owner of causing the dengue cases, stressing that the suspected breeding site was identified through residents' concerns and still needed to be inspected.

  

The mayor visited the families of two five-year-old children who died from dengue in Barangays Umapad and Opao on Thursday, Nov. 19. He also confirmed that 15 dengue cases had been recorded among Opao Elementary School students.

  

Following the reports, Ouano met with City Health Officer Dr. Debra Catulong and other city personnel at the Opao Barangay Hall to discuss additional measures to prevent further infections.

  

The city will continue implementing the Department of Health's 4S strategy, which includes information campaigns and community clean-up activities to eliminate potential mosquito breeding sites.

  

It has also coordinated with the Department of Education Mandaue City Division Administrative Office, headed by Dr. Edgar Espina, after the division requested another round of misting operations in schools.

  

Ouano said the City Health Office was checking its available chemicals for the operations and would procure additional supplies if needed.

  

Although the city had conducted misting operations in previous months, another round will proceed following DepEd's request.

  

The mayor acknowledged parents' concerns over the reported infections, saying he understood their worries as a parent himself.

  

“One life is too many,” Ouano said, stressing that even a lower number of cases compared with other local government units should not be dismissed.

  

Aside from the financial assistance extended by the city government, Ouano said he also provided personal assistance to the families of the two children who died.
