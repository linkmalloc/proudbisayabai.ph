In a time when the cost of living continues to rise and every peso counts, a short film by Joseph Sabello striking a deeply emotional chord with viewers online.



“Gasolina” is more than just a story about fuel prices — it is a moving portrait of fathers who carry burdens in silence. It sheds light on the quiet sacrifices many often overlook: the sleepless nights, the constant worrying, and the unspoken pressure to provide, no matter the cost.



At the heart of the film is a father struggling to keep his family afloat as gasoline prices climb. Each day becomes a balancing act of stretching limited resources, meeting his family’s needs, and hiding his own fears behind the familiar phrase, “I’m okay.” For him, breaking down is not an option. Because in his mind, if he falls apart, everything else might too.



What makes “Gasolina” even more touching is the story behind its creator. Sabello, who grew up without a father, crafted a narrative that resonates with thousands — capturing a kind of love he experienced from a distance, yet understood so deeply. Through his perspective, the film becomes both a tribute and an exploration of fatherhood: not loud or showy, but enduring, and often unseen.



Set against the backdrop of rising fuel costs, the film mirrors a reality familiar to many Filipino families. It reminds viewers that behind every full tank is a sacrifice — often made quietly by a father doing everything he can to keep his family moving forward.



“Gasolina” is a story that invites reflection. It asks us to look closer, to appreciate more, and to recognize the silent battles fought every day by the people we rely on the most.



Because sometimes, the strongest love is the one that never asks to be noticed.
