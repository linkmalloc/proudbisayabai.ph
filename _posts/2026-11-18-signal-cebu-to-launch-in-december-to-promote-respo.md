A new technology communicators' group is set to launch in Cebu this December to help the public better understand emerging technologies, artificial intelligence (AI) and their effects on everyday life.

Vanj D. Padilla, founder and strategic adviser of Shared Knowledge Asia Pacific (SKAP), said Signal Cebu will bring together journalists, content creators, bloggers, influencers, storytellers and information officers to promote technology literacy and responsible digital participation.

The initiative will also provide training and capacity development for members, particularly in explaining technical concepts and translating technology-related developments into information that ordinary people can understand.

Padilla said the group aims to address the limited public communication surrounding technology, as more Filipinos adopt digital tools and AI without necessarily understanding their risks.

"Only a few content creators explain to the people about technology, about the effect of technology on people's lives," she said during a media press conference at Winzen Cafe on Monday, Nov. 16.

She said Signal Cebu would also serve businesses, technology startups, universities and local government units that need to communicate their products, services and digital systems to the public.

The group will welcome members with different skills and interests, including reporting, video production, livestreaming, photography and other forms of digital storytelling.

Padilla said the initiative was part of a broader push to promote an open and safe Internet, particularly as children and young people become increasingly exposed to digital platforms.

She said Cebu has a high rate of technology adoption, but public education on online safety has not kept pace with its use.

"The problem is that they already know how to use it, but no one is giving them precautions," she said, citing risks such as online scams, fraudulent transactions, data leaks and malware.

Padilla said the Internet Society was needed to help educate communities on how to use digital platforms while protecting themselves from harmful online activities.

She also hopes to expand the advocacy beyond Cebu, which she described as the first area in the Visayas to receive the initiative, with possible activities in Davao, Iligan, Zamboanga and Iloilo before expanding to Luzon.

Padilla's push for stronger digital education follows her recent participation in the Internet Society Foundation's Community Advocacy Fellowship, through which she attended training in Brussels, Belgium, in October.

As an advocate for an open and safe Internet, she also met with representatives of the European Parliament to learn how European institutions develop policies and laws protecting citizens in their use of the Internet.

She said Europe has taken a more aggressive approach to regulating AI and other disruptive technologies to protect citizens from potential risks.

One of the lessons she highlighted was data sovereignty, which concerns how data, technology infrastructure and AI systems are governed within a particular jurisdiction.

Padilla said the Philippines, including Cebu, has been adopting AI rapidly, but its regulatory framework has not kept pace.

"The regulation here is not yet at par with the usage," she said.

She cited the country's National AI Roadmap and the creation of a National AI Council, but said additional safeguards and enforceable measures were needed to address potential abuses.

She added that the country continues to rely on existing measures, including the Data Privacy Act and E-Commerce Act, in addressing digital-related concerns.

Padilla also pointed to the fragmented state of institutional information as another challenge in adopting AI.

She said valuable information held by companies, government offices and other institutions remains scattered in physical files, archives and separate storage systems, making it difficult to integrate into AI platforms.

While employees may already be using AI tools individually, she said institutions still lack organized knowledge systems that would allow the technology to support their operations effectively.

Padilla also encouraged journalists and media practitioners to establish Wikipedia accounts and participate in documenting knowledge through open platforms.

She said Wikipedia and its sister projects serve as repositories of information that can contribute to the data used by AI systems.

She argued that journalists, who regularly produce information and document public events, should also have accurate and neutral biographies that can help establish their professional records online.

She said contributions must follow Wikipedia's standards, including neutrality and the avoidance of personal opinions or biased commentary.

Padilla also highlighted the importance of revitalizing Cebuano Wikipedia through greater participation from human contributors.

She said Cebuano Wikipedia has approximately 6.4 million articles, many of which were generated through automated translations of English Wikipedia entries rather than original contributions from Cebuano writers.

While the extensive collection of Cebuano-language articles helps AI systems generate responses in the language, she said the volume does not necessarily reflect the depth of local cultural knowledge.

She cited traditional practices and community knowledge, including local ways of preparing food, that may remain undocumented online.

Padilla described the potential loss of such information as "cultural extinction by omission," warning that knowledge passed down through generations could become inaccessible if communities fail to document it.

She said the Wikimedia movement hopes to encourage more Cebuano contributors through community initiatives such as Wikiclub Cebu, allowing residents to document their language, heritage, arts and traditions.

Padilla stressed that human participation remains essential even as AI becomes more influential in accessing and processing information.

"We cannot stop AI, but instead, what we can do is adapt," she said, emphasizing the need to strengthen the role of humans in guiding how technology is used.

She added that the broader advocacy aims to protect Filipinos from misinformation, manipulation and algorithmic bias while ensuring that local knowledge remains accessible to future generations.
