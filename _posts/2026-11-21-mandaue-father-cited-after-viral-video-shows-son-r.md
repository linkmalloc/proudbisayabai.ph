An eight-year-old boy’s unsafe position on a moving motorcycle has prompted traffic authorities in Mandaue City to cite his father for reckless driving, despite the motorist’s explanation that his child had difficulty sitting because of an earlier injury.

Rey Cañete Rosaceña, 30, voluntarily appeared before the Traffic Enforcement Agency of Mandaue (TEAM) on Thursday, Nov. 19, after a video of the incident in Barangay Tawason circulated on social media.

The footage, recorded by a concerned citizen, showed the boy kneeling on the motorcycle’s rear portion without a helmet while his father drove. The child was not seated properly on the passenger seat.

TEAM head Hyll Retuya said the agency had given Rosaceña until Nov. 23 to appear and explain the incident. Otherwise, TEAM would have sought assistance from the Land Transportation Office (LTO), which had expressed interest in issuing a show-cause order against the motorist.

Retuya said TEAM coordinated with the LTO but initially held off on further action to allow Rosaceña to explain his side.

During his appearance, Rosaceña said he had just returned from the market when he decided to pick up his son from school because it was along his route and classes had already ended.

He said he did not intend to endanger his child and chose to appear before TEAM rather than evade responsibility.

“Sa tinuod lang, gihigugma man gud na nako akong anak intawn. Mas gihigugma pa na nako sa akong kaugalingon,” Rosaceña said.

He explained that their home was near the elementary school, which was why he did not bring a helmet for his son for what he considered a short trip.

Rosaceña also said he usually asks someone else to fetch the child when he is working. Their family relies on a motorcycle for daily transportation because pedicabs and tricycles are not readily available in their area.

He further disclosed that his son had slipped and landed on his buttocks, leaving the child with swelling and pain that made sitting properly difficult.

“Ang circumstances man gud ato kay na-yandak siya, ang iyang lubot ang nadakdak, maong naingon ato to siya,” he said.

The father apologized to Retuya and expressed remorse over the incident, acknowledging that he would have to face the consequences.

He also urged other motorcycle riders to follow traffic regulations, particularly the mandatory use of helmets, even when traffic enforcers are not around.

“Nakat-on gyud ko sa nahitabo karon, sa akoang nasinati,” he said.

Rosaceña added that he would personally explain his side to the LTO following Retuya’s advice.

TEAM nevertheless issued him a citation for reckless driving and allowing a back rider to travel without a helmet.

Retuya acknowledged the circumstances surrounding the child’s injury but said these did not exempt the father from complying with traffic regulations.

“Bisag unsay iyang rason, kabalo man ta naa man tay humanitarian reasons gyud unta. Pero to set an example sa general public, ato gyud gihapon siyang gi-isyuhan kay kalapasan gyud gihapon to sa traffic law,” Retuya said.

He said the agency wanted to enforce traffic rules consistently and prevent motorists from believing that personal circumstances could excuse violations.

“So kana, to set the tone straight, gi-isyuhan gihapon nato si sir. So mao na nga fair enough sa iyaha ug sa public nga wala tay gipalapas diri, wala tay favoritism,” he added.

Retuya also urged deputized barangay tanods to apprehend motorists who violate traffic rules, particularly those who fail to wear helmets.

He reminded Rosaceña to ensure that both he and his passenger wear helmets whenever they travel by motorcycle. Retuya said TEAM had also apprehended other motorists in the same area for similar violations.

The agency said the incident should remind motorists that traffic rules apply regardless of their circumstances, as consistent enforcement is necessary to prevent road accidents and protect passengers, particularly children.
