The Department of Health (DOH) is intensifying its online information campaigns on vaccination, HIV prevention and reproductive health while working with law enforcement agencies to remove advertisements promoting illegal abortion services.

  

During Friday’s Senate budget deliberations, Senator JV Ejercito said the DOH has partnered with Meta, the parent company of Facebook, and the Department of Information and Communications Technology (DICT) to address vaccine misinformation and rebuild public confidence in immunization.

  

“According to DOH, yes, they have a partnership at present with Meta and DICT. So, they are very much active now, ang DOH sa (the DOH is now very active on) social media,” Ejercito said in response to Senator Vicente Sotto III.

  

Sotto cited a Commission on Audit report showing that only 58.56 percent of children were fully immunized in 2025, falling short of the government’s 95-percent target.

  

Ejercito said the Senate restored funding for immunization and health promotion programs, including campaigns to counter misleading claims about vaccines circulating online.

  

The DOH is also coordinating with the National Bureau of Investigation, the DICT’s Cybercrime Investigation and Coordinating Center, and the Philippine National Police Anti-Cybercrime Group to investigate online advertisements offering abortion services.

  

The department’s Health Facilities and Services Regulatory Bureau checks whether suspected facilities have the necessary licenses and comply with regulations. It also refers possible violations to authorities for investigation.

  

Ejercito said about 60 percent of identified online content had been taken down, although he did not specify the total number of posts covered or the period of the takedowns.

  

Sotto and Senator Joel Villanueva urged stronger enforcement after presenting examples of abortion services advertised on Facebook and TikTok. Sotto said those involved in illegal operations should face criminal charges.

  

For HIV prevention, testing and treatment, the DOH has proposed approximately P2 billion under its 2027 budget.

  

Ejercito also cited a P75.5-million allocation for the Philippine National AIDS Council. Meanwhile, the Philippine Health Insurance Corp. has paid P3.4 billion in HIV-related benefit claims covering 102,586 beneficiaries.

  

On adolescent reproductive health, the DOH recently signed an agreement with the Department of Education and the Department of the Interior and Local Government to implement reproductive health education in schools for students in Grades 5 to 12.

  

The proposed 2027 budget also includes approximately P786 million for adolescent health programs.
