The Philippine National Police (PNP) is deploying more than 72,000 personnel nationwide for this year’s Undas observance, strengthening road safety operations and warning the public against individuals posing as priests to solicit money from cemetery visitors.

PNP chief Gen. Jose Melencio C. Nartatez Jr. on Tuesday directed police commanders to increase patrols along major highways, accident-prone roads and routes leading to cemeteries and transport terminals as millions of Filipinos prepare to travel for All Saints’ Day and All Souls’ Day.

In a statement, Nartatez said police units would also conduct information campaigns urging motorists to inspect their vehicles before traveling, particularly their brakes, tires, lights and other essential components.

  

The deployment will be supported by augmentation forces from the Armed Forces of the Philippines, Bureau of Fire Protection, Philippine Coast Guard, local government units and other force multipliers.

The PNP will be under full alert status from Oct. 30 to Nov. 2.

Nartatez reminded motorists that police visibility alone could not prevent all road accidents, stressing that road users must also exercise caution and discipline.

He urged drivers to avoid speeding, distracted driving and sluggish driving, and to take extra care on downhill roads, sharp curves and congested routes, particularly during peak travel periods.

Meanwhile, the National Capital Region Police Office (NCRPO) warned cemetery visitors in Metro Manila to watch out for individuals falsely claiming to be priests or authorized religious ministers.

The NCRPO said people may exploit the annual observance by offering prayers, blessings and other religious services for departed loved ones in exchange for money despite lacking the proper religious affiliation or authorization.

  

Abrahano advised the public to verify the identity of anyone claiming to be a priest by contacting cemetery administrators or the concerned parish or diocese.

  

The NCRPO issued the warning as thousands of families are expected to visit cemeteries, memorial parks and columbaria across Metro Manila to commemorate their departed relatives.
