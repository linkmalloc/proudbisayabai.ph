United Nations Secretary-General Antonio Guterres has called for a coordinated global effort to strengthen countries’ ability to manage artificial intelligence (AI), warning that the technology’s rapid development is exposing new cybersecurity threats and deepening inequalities.

  

Speaking at a news conference during the Bridgetown Special Summit on AI Safety in Barbados on Monday, Guterres said AI was advancing rapidly and transforming work, education and communication while creating opportunities in health, science and development.

  

But he warned that the growing capabilities of AI systems also bring risks that countries must be equipped to address.

  

“As artificial intelligence becomes more powerful and more widespread, the risks are also becoming clearer,” Guterres said.

  

He pointed to recent technological outages and cyberattacks as evidence of vulnerabilities in digital systems that societies increasingly rely on, saying AI was amplifying those threats.

  

“AI systems are becoming more capable and more autonomous,” he said. “We are seeing new cybersecurity risks. New possibilities for manipulation and misuse.”

  

Guterres said countries must have the tools, knowledge and capacity to evaluate AI systems entering their markets, manage potential dangers and protect their populations when problems arise.

  

Although AI is already affecting economies and societies worldwide, he said many countries still lack the resources to assess and regulate the technology effectively.

  

“That gap must be closed. Every country must have the capacity to manage AI safely,” he said, warning that existing protections remain fragmented and are often unable to keep pace with technological developments.

  

He called for a shared understanding of emerging AI risks and credible, independent methods of evaluating powerful AI systems.

  

Guterres also urged governments, private companies, financial institutions, foundations and other donors to increase investments in AI capacity-building, particularly in developing regions.

  

“The risks are global. Our response must be global. That is why we must act now,” he said.

  

Barbados Prime Minister Mia Amor Mottley also warned that concentrating AI development and operational capacity in only a few regions could widen existing inequalities.

  

“Urgent investments are therefore necessary to build AI capacity in all regions of the world,” Mottley said.

  

She identified infrastructure, datasets, skills and AI models that reflect local values and priorities as essential components of building that capacity, along with preparing workers for changes brought by the technology.

  

Mottley said AI development must uphold human rights, support sustainable development and allow people to have control over their future.

  

She also cautioned against allowing the technology to repeat past forms of exploitation by turning people’s personal data into commodities for the benefit of a few.

  

“We do not want to see the commoditization of human beings again as data packs just for the interests of a few who stand to make extraordinary profits,” she said.

  

Mottley called for countries to develop AI locally to strengthen sovereignty, support local learning and harness the technology's scientific potential.
