Mandaue City Councilor Jennifer del Mar is seeking penalties for people who damage, reposition or otherwise interfere with government-owned closed-circuit television (CCTV) cameras, citing their importance in public safety and police investigations.

Del Mar said reports of cameras being damaged or deliberately turned away from certain areas have raised concerns over the city's ability to monitor public spaces and preserve footage that could help authorities identify suspects.

She said some individuals, mostly minors, allegedly reposition the cameras to avoid being recorded, particularly when engaging in activities that violate the city's curfew regulations.

“We want to protect the assets of the LGU (local government unit) because all the CCTVs we installed were funded by the city, only for them to be damaged,” Del Mar said.

She stressed that CCTV footage has become an important tool in resolving cases, making it necessary to keep the equipment properly maintained and protected.

“Kasagaran nga nadakpan karon nakuha sa CCTV footage, so kana siya, if gub-on ang CCTV, somehow makaapekto pod siya sa mga investigations,” (Most of those apprehended nowadays are caught through CCTV footage, so damaging the cameras could somehow affect investigations) she said.

Del Mar also cited instances in which cameras were allegedly repositioned to prevent authorities from monitoring people drinking or loitering in public areas, including minors who may be violating curfew hours.

“We need to inform them that dili na siya pwede,” (We need to inform them that this is not allowed) she said.

To address the problem, Del Mar introduced an ordinance on first reading that seeks to prohibit the unauthorized removal, destruction, tampering with, obstruction of or interference with city-owned CCTV systems and their components.

The proposed measure also seeks to impose penalties on violators.

Its explanatory clauses recognize the role of CCTV cameras in maintaining peace and order, preventing crime, monitoring public spaces, and supporting law enforcement and disaster response operations.

The measure states that unauthorized interference with the systems could compromise public safety, obstruct criminal investigations and undermine the delivery of public security services.
