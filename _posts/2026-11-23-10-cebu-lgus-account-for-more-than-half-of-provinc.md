Ten local government units (LGUs) accounted for more than half of Cebu province's dengue infections, with Talisay City and Toledo City leading the list as the provincial case count reached 8,882 from Jan. 1 to Nov. 20, 2026.

  

Provincial Health Office (PHO) data showed that Talisay recorded 1,008 cases and six deaths, while Toledo had 893 infections and three fatalities.

  

Medellin ranked third with 563 cases and one death, followed by Minglanilla with 466 cases and one fatality.

  

Dalaguete reported 408 infections and three deaths, while Consolacion recorded 385 cases and four fatalities.

  

The other LGUs in the top 10 were Cordova with 346 cases and three deaths, Danao City with 332 cases and three deaths, Liloan with 311 cases and five deaths, and Argao with 299 cases and two deaths.

  

Combined, the 10 LGUs recorded 5,010 cases, or 56.4 percent of the province's total infections. They also accounted for 31 of the 59 dengue-related deaths reported across Cebu.

  

Although Talisay had the highest number of infections and fatalities, other towns recorded notable deaths relative to their case counts.

  

Madridejos, for example, reported four deaths among 174 cases, while Ronda recorded two fatalities from 35 infections. Tudela also reported one death despite having only four cases.

  

Meanwhile, 24 of the province's 50 reporting LGUs had no recorded dengue-related deaths during the period. These included Carcar City, which logged 206 cases, and Naga City, with 202.

  

Monthly figures also showed a sharp rise in infections toward the latter part of the year. November recorded 2,114 cases and 20 deaths as of Nov. 20, the highest monthly totals during the reporting period.

  

October had 1,877 cases and 12 deaths, while August and September recorded 1,667 and 1,493 infections, respectively.

  

PHO head Dr. Sheila Faciol acknowledged the continued increase, particularly in November, and said the office was coordinating with local governments and rural health units (RHUs) to strengthen community-level prevention efforts.

  

She recognized local health offices for carrying out preventive activities and reminding residents to keep their surroundings clean.

  

“Manglimpyo sa panimalay, manglimpyo sa tugkaran, manglimpyo sa community,” Faciol said.

  

The provincial government is also providing dengue kits and test kits and conducting misting activities to support local prevention efforts.

  

Faciol also confirmed that provincial hospitals continue to operate beyond their bed capacity as they accommodate patients with dengue and other illnesses, including influenza.
