The Cebu Provincial Government is preparing water trucks, treatment facilities and alternative water sources to ensure emergency supplies reach communities that may experience shortages during the expected prolonged dry spell.

The Capitol is coordinating with local government units (LGUs) to identify areas that may need assistance, particularly upland communities with limited access to alternative water supplies.

Engineer Aldrin Barade, head of the Cebu Province Inter-LGU Waterworks System Office (CPIWSO), said the province plans to prioritize mountain barangays when deploying its water trucks.

“We will deploy our trucks to critical areas, especially katong mountain barangays,” Barade said during a press conference.

Under the proposed distribution setup, designated water stations will serve as replenishment points for trucks operated by the provincial government and LGUs, allowing them to deliver supplies directly to communities.

Gov. Pamela Baricuatro said the province would supplement the resources of municipalities and cities, particularly those without enough equipment to distribute water during emergencies.

“You have to understand that we sa Province, we augment sa mga LGUs,” Baricuatro said.

To guide the deployment, the Provincial Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Office (PDRRMO) is inventorying water trucks and fire trucks available across Cebu's local governments.

Barade said the assessment would identify LGUs with sufficient equipment and those that need additional vehicles or logistical assistance.

The province is also preparing to use its six water treatment plants as potential sources of emergency water deliveries. Two facilities, located in Moalboal and Barili, have been completed and are already operational.

Nearby municipalities may coordinate with the Capitol to access water from these facilities. The remaining treatment plants are still under construction, with the Provincial Engineering Office helping speed up their completion.

The provincial government is also exploring the establishment of additional water stations in Tabogon, Tabuelan and Tuburan. Other areas being considered for water treatment facilities include Barili, Malabuyoc and Oslob.

Apart from preparing delivery equipment and treatment facilities, the Capitol is conducting a province-wide inventory of water sources, including rivers and springs, that could be developed for public consumption, irrigation or impounding.

Barade said Baricuatro directed various provincial offices to take part in the initiative, including validating potential water sources identified during the governor's visits to different LGUs.

The inventory will help determine which sources could provide alternative supplies when existing water systems experience reduced output during prolonged dry conditions.

Barade said the PDRRMO had already received reports of dwindling water supplies in some localities.

For longer-term preparedness, Baricuatro is also considering barangay-level water systems and a proposed inter-LGU water supply network to improve access and distribution across municipal boundaries.
