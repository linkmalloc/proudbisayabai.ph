Mandaue City is setting aside up to P12 million for this year's Christmas decorations, extending its holiday lighting display beyond City Hall to five major roads.

  

Mayor Thadeo Jovito “Jonkie” Ouano said the allocation is higher than the estimated P8 million to P9 million spent on last year's decorations.

  

“Mas taas lang siguro ni siya gamay. Siguro mga last year murag mga P8 to 9 million. Unya karon mga P10 to 12 siguro, slightly higher gamay,” Ouano said.

  

The city adopted a Fantasy Land theme for this year's display, which aims to give residents a different Christmas experience through new installations and wider coverage.

  

Ouano said the City Hall grounds were already 80 percent ready for the official lighting ceremony on Monday, Nov. 23. A test lighting was scheduled for Sunday evening, Nov. 22, to check the installations before the event.

  

The city prioritized the City Hall display to meet the opening schedule, although work on decorations along the major roads may continue beyond Monday.

  

The five roads included in the expanded lighting project are Ouano Avenue, United Nations Avenue, A.S. Fortuna Street, A.C. Cortes Avenue and M.C. Briones Street.

  

In previous years, the city's road decorations were concentrated mainly along A.C. Cortes Avenue and United Nations Avenue, with portions of Ouano Avenue also covered.

  

This year, the city plans to install Christmas lights on streetlights along all five roads, requiring additional time and work.

  

The Fantasy Land-themed display at the City Hall grounds will open to the public after Monday's Christmas tree lighting ceremony, allowing residents to visit and spend time with family and friends.

  

“Para malingaw sad ang mga Mandauehanon, makasuroy sila diri and spend time with their family and friends,” Ouano said.

  

The mayor said preparations began in August, with the city reusing some materials from last year's display while adding new installations.

  

Despite economic uncertainties, including developments in the Middle East and the effects of the extreme El Niño, Ouano said the city would continue its Christmas celebrations.

  

“Bisag unsa pay mahitabo, magpadayon man ang Pasko,” he said.
