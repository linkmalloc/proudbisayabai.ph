Cebu City Mayor Nestor Archival has 15 days to explain his actions concerning the Monterrazas de Cebu development after the Office of the Ombudsman directed him to submit a counter-affidavit and supporting evidence in response to criminal and administrative complaints.

  

Archival received the joint order late last week and immediately forwarded it to the Cebu City Legal Office (CLO), which is preparing his response, his chief of staff, Kenneth Siasar, said on Monday, November 23, 2026.

  

Siasar clarified that the directive covers complaints that had previously circulated on social media and does not involve a new case against the mayor.

  

“It’s not something new or a surprise,” Siasar said.

  

He said Archival had avoided commenting on the allegations until he received the official documents. The mayor has since referred the matter to the legal office, which is working to meet the Ombudsman's deadline.

  

“We will try, because the Ombudsman has set a deadline and we have to reply,” Siasar said.

  

In a joint order dated November 11, Deputy Ombudsman for the Visayas Dante Vargas directed Archival to submit his counter-affidavit and supporting evidence within 15 days of receiving the directive.

  

The complaints stem from a case former government official Ed Karlon Rama filed before the Ombudsman's Area Office for the Visayas on August 19.

  

Rama accused Archival of violating Sections 3(e) and 3(h) of Republic Act 3019, or the Anti-Graft and Corrupt Practices Act. He also charged the mayor with conduct prejudicial to the best interest of the service, grave abuse of authority and grave misconduct.

  

Rama questioned Archival's failure to act against the hillside development despite concerns that the project could contribute to flooding in communities below it in Barangay Guadalupe.

  

He also raised concerns about the mayor's alleged financial interest in the development, citing a 713-square-meter property identified as Lot 6, Block 5 of Monterrazas Subdivision, which Archival and his spouse reportedly own.

  

Rama argued that the property interest could create a conflict between Archival's private financial interests and his official duties as city mayor.

  

He also invoked Republic Act 6713, or the Code of Conduct and Ethical Standards for Public Officials and Employees, alleging that Archival failed to disclose his interest in the property and recuse himself from matters involving Monterrazas.

  

Rama asked the Ombudsman to pursue criminal and administrative proceedings against the mayor and consider placing him under preventive suspension while the investigation is ongoing.

  

Archival has acknowledged owning property within Monterrazas but denied concealing its acquisition or allowing his personal interests to influence his decisions concerning the development.

  

He said he began acquiring the property gradually in 2016 and completed the purchase around 2022. He maintained that he declared the property in his 2025 Statement of Assets, Liabilities and Net Worth.

  

The mayor also defended his decision not to issue a cease-and-desist order against the project, despite calls from Vice Mayor Tomas Osmeña and some city councilors to stop construction.

  

Archival cited the Department of Environment and Natural Resources' decision to lift its stoppage order against the development in April 2026, saying he saw no basis to halt the project again.

  

He also referred to reports from the University of the Philippines and the city's Technical Infrastructure Committee, which he said did not identify Monterrazas as the main cause of flooding in downstream communities.

  

Mont Property Group, the project's developer, has likewise denied allegations that its development caused widespread flooding and that it illegally cut more than 700 trees.

  

Under the Ombudsman's directive, Archival must furnish Rama with a copy of his counter-affidavit and supporting evidence and submit proof of service to the anti-graft office.

  

Rama will then have five days from receipt of the mayor's submission to file a reply-affidavit. The order states that the period will not be extended.

  

The directive also warns that Archival will waive his right to submit a counter-affidavit if he fails to meet the deadline, allowing the investigation to proceed under existing rules.

  

The order does not establish the mayor's guilt. The Ombudsman must still complete its criminal and administrative investigations.
