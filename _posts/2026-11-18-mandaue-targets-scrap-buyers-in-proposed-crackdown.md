Mandaue City is seeking to hold junkshops and scrap buyers accountable for purchasing stolen government properties, as local officials move to curb the theft and resale of public infrastructure materials.

The City Council approved on first reading Draft Ordinance No. 133-2026, which seeks to prohibit the destruction, vandalism, cutting and theft of government-owned properties, public signages and public utility wiring and cables.

The proposed measure also seeks to regulate transactions involving infrastructure materials at junkshops, scrap dealers, metal recyclers and similar establishments to prevent them from becoming outlets for stolen public property.

Councilor Jennifer del Mar, who authored the measure, said it was prompted by incidents involving the theft of city-owned and national government-owned properties, including public signages, road stud lights, utility wires and cables.

She cited a recent incident in which a stolen government-owned signage was allegedly sold to a scrap buyer who knew it belonged to the city.

“Kato bitaw last week nga gikawat ang government-owned signage unya gipatimbang unya ang scrap buyer iya sad gipalit, unya kita baya sila nga it was owned by the city government, so nganong mupalit man og kinawat,” Del Mar said.

She said buyers who knowingly accept stolen materials also contribute to the demand that sustains the theft of public properties.

“The scrap buyers are buying it as well knowing nga kinawat… if there’s no demand, there will be no supply as simple as that, kung walay mupalit, walay mamaligya,” she said.

“Now we are eyeing on the scrap buyers,” Del Mar added.

She said the proposed ordinance would provide another way to address theft, particularly incidents involving minors who cannot face the same penalties as adult offenders.

“Panuguon naman lang ang mga minor de edad kay di man ka file ang city ana, so now we are warning the scrap buyers as well,” she said.

Del Mar urged residents to report junkshops suspected of buying stolen government properties and utility materials to her office, the Business Permits and Licensing Office or the Mandaue City Police Office.

The draft ordinance covers public assets such as traffic and directional signages, streetlights, drainage covers, waiting sheds, public buildings and other government infrastructure.

It also covers electrical and telecommunications wiring and cables, including copper wires and fiber-optic cables.

The proposed measure states that removing these materials can create road safety hazards, interrupt essential services and cause financial losses to the government and the public. It also identifies establishments that knowingly buy or receive stolen infrastructure materials as part of the illegal trade.

The draft cites Sections 16 and 458 of Republic Act No. 7160, or the Local Government Code of 1991, which authorize local government units to promote public safety, maintain peace and order, regulate businesses and protect public properties.

Del Mar said violators could face a fine of up to P5,000 and revocation of their business permits.

The proposal follows a Nov. 6 incident in which the Mandaue Safety Maintenance Area Response Team apprehended a man allegedly stealing road stud lights along Ouano Avenue in Barangay Tipolo.

Around three road stud lights and their wires were reportedly taken. Authorities said the materials were allegedly intended for resale, with the proceeds reportedly meant to buy illegal drugs. The fixtures are made of aluminum.

Del Mar said numerous road stud lights across the city had already been removed and sold to scrap buyers, with the aluminum fixtures fetching money when sold by weight.

“Ibaligya nila sa mga mamalit… sayang ang resources sa government kung ato lang i destroy og ingon ana and its for our own benefit,” she said.

She lamented the theft of public facilities, saying these are intended to serve the community.

The city is also coordinating with the Children’s Desk of the Mandaue City Police Office on cases involving minors, Del Mar said.

“We need to refer the case, it’s going to be a tough conversation pa gyud,” she said.
