Cebu City will deploy more personnel at Colon Night Market to discourage people from soliciting money and visitors from giving alms, Mayor Nestor Archival Sr. said.

  

Archival said he had instructed the personnel overseeing the night market to increase their presence and remind the public that both asking for and giving money are prohibited in the area.

  

The city is also considering using microphones to broadcast reminders, particularly as the night market draws diners, families and tourists.

  

“Ang mangayo ug ang manghatag, dakpon,” Archival said. (Those who ask for and those who give alms will be arrested.)

  

He stressed that the restriction applies to both people soliciting money and those handing out donations.

  

“Sa night market, bawal ang pagpanghatag. Bawal sad ang pagpangayo,” he said. (Giving alms is prohibited at the night market. Asking for alms is also prohibited.)

  

The planned enforcement comes as the city seeks to address solicitation in the dining area, where visitors gather to eat and spend time with family and friends.

  

Archival's reminder also comes ahead of the Christmas season, when carolers traditionally go around neighborhoods and establishments asking for donations.

  

The mayor recently waived the P50 permit fee for Christmas carolers but retained the requirement for them to register with the city.

  

He said registration would help authorities identify those going from house to house, following complaints from residents and institutions about individuals allegedly using caroling as an opportunity to steal.

  

The waiver also does not authorize caroling or solicitation along public roads and other prohibited areas. Archival said caroling along roads or inside vehicles is prohibited because it can endanger participants and motorists.

  

Under Cebu City Ordinance No. 1931, carolers may go from house to house or from one establishment to another, subject to rules on schedules and locations. The ordinance prohibits caroling inside public utility vehicles and similar forms of public transportation.

  

Archival also encouraged residents who want to share food or provide other forms of assistance this Christmas to coordinate with the Department of Social Welfare and Services instead of distributing donations indiscriminately in public areas.
