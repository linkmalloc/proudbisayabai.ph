The Bureau of Internal Revenue (BIR) has cautioned taxpayers against a fraudulent notice demanding P68,309 to release more than P1.4 million in supposedly frozen funds.

The document claims its recipient has an outstanding tax liability that must be settled before the money can be released. However, the BIR's Revenue District Office No. 81 in Cebu City North denied issuing the notice and declared it fraudulent.

Dated Nov. 13, 2026, the document uses the BIR's name and official seal to appear legitimate. It also bears a signature that does not match the officer identified in the notice.

The fake notice cites provisions of the National Internal Revenue Code to make its payment demand appear legally supported. It directs the recipient to send the supposed tax payment to foreign bank accounts and promises the immediate release and transfer of the frozen funds once payment is made.

The bureau warned the public not to follow the document's instructions or send money to the accounts indicated.

The BIR reminded taxpayers to settle obligations only through authorized payment channels, including accredited banks, authorized Revenue Collection Officers and official online payment facilities.

It also advised the public against transferring tax payments to personal or unauthorized bank accounts, even when the individuals requesting payment claim to represent the government.

Those who receive similar notices should verify their authenticity with their respective Revenue District Office or through the BIR's official communication channels before taking any action.

Recipients of the fraudulent notice were also urged not to send money or disclose personal and banking information to the people behind the scheme.

Anyone who has already transferred money should immediately contact their bank and report the incident to the appropriate authorities.

The BIR said it would not tolerate the unauthorized use of its name, official seal or personnel's identities to deceive taxpayers, reaffirming its commitment to protecting the public and maintaining trust in government tax collection.
